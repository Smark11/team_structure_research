The base rates

India has 2,117 global capability centers across 3,728 units, employing about 2.36 million people on $98.4B of revenue in FY26,7 up from roughly 1,700 centers, 1.9 million people and $64.6B two years earlier.154 Something like 110–120 new centers open every year, and 35% of the population was set up in the last two years.38155 This is not a niche move. It is also not a move with a good track record, once you read the distribution rather than the totals.

Who is counting

Every population figure in this section comes from Zinnov, EY or ANSR — firms that sell GCC set-ups, surveys to GCCs, or both. Treat their maturity and attrition numbers as a ceiling on how good things are, not a floor. Where two of them disagree, we say so.

Zinnov's maturity ladder runs Outpost → Satellite → Portfolio Hub → Transformation Hub, and puts today's population at 13% / 43% / 39% / 5%.7 Read plainly: 56% of India GCCs are still execution shops, and only about 27% reach Portfolio Hub — real end-to-end ownership of a portfolio — within five years of opening.7 That 27% is the single best base rate available for "an extension that graduates," and it comes from the firm that sells GCC set-ups, so it is an upper bound. inferred

The older data is worse. Forrester found in 2007 that "more than 60% of the captive centers in India alone are struggling," citing lack of management support, spiraling costs, skyrocketing attrition and lack of integration — and noted that many captives existed because an expatriate executive wanted to move home.30 The 2008–09 cohort was then sold off: Citi sold Citigroup Global Services to TCS for ~$505M in October 2008 and its Indian IT unit to Wipro in 2009, each tied to a multi-year service contract; AXA's 600-person captive went to Capita; AOL, Aviva, Prudential UK and Philips put theirs on the block.27156 The analyst reasons at the time were fixed cost, management distraction, inflexible capacity in downturns, and a talent market that preferred vendor employment for career growth.27 That last reason has inverted — GCCs now pay 15–40% above IT services at mid-senior levels and post the lowest attrition of any segment15740 — but the other three have not. inferred

The current population is healthier: Everest counted 452 new set-ups in 2023, and about 50 captives sold in 2020–23 out of 1,450 created.5 A ~3% divestiture rate is a different world from 2009. What has not changed is the shape of the ladder: most sites are chartered as execution and stay there.7

Reference sites

Eight sites that the evidence actually documents. Each card says what charter the site took, how it moved, and the one lesson that transfers to a ~140-person data org. Headcounts are the parent's own numbers unless tagged otherwise.15158

Target · Bengaluru, 2005→

IT support → "integrated global headquarters"

Opened via ANSR "with a small team focused on building IT capabilities to support the US headquarters." documented159 Now 5,700+, ~40% technology, with Business, Tech, Product, Data and UX co-located; the India store-design team does about half of new store designs and 80% of remodels. documented15 The operating rule its president states: "ownership of outcomes rests with where the center of gravity for the capability sits."15 ANSR's "25% of Target's global workforce" can only mean corporate workforce. inferred159

Lesson. A hybrid by design, reached after ~15 years, by hiring product, data and UX alongside engineering — not by handing over a customer domain on day one. inferred judgment

Walmart Global Tech · Bengaluru, Chennai, Gurugram

Global platform work beside a separately owned local product

Walmart says its India teams "build technology solutions to help millions of Walmart customers around the world" across e-commerce, supply chain and payments; Chennai was added in 2021 and global WGT headcount passed 20,000 in 2022. documented49160 Flipkart and PhonePe are separate operating companies, not GCC charters. documented161 Aggregator claims that Bengaluru "owns" global search, pricing and payments have no Walmart statement behind them. folklore

Lesson. The documented shape is a structural hybrid: global-platform work plus a locally owned product company next door. inferred judgment

Lowe's India · Bengaluru, 2015→

"One team, multiple locations" → platform VPs in India

Opened February 2015 as a ~500-person Global Innovation Center on customer experience, technology and analytics; the MD's stated model was "one team, multiple locations" — an explicit extension charter. documented31 Ten years on: 5,000+ associates, with India-based VPs for Applied AI/Data & Analytics, Omnichannel Platforms and Engineering Platforms, product management as a listed function, under an SVP who is both CTO and MD. documented26

Lesson. The cleanest graduating-extension case on record — and it graduated into platform-and-capability ownership with resident PM, not customer-domain P&L. inferred judgment

JPMorgan · Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad

The largest support charter in the country

50,000+ in August 2023, ~55,000 in 2026, adding ~1,000 technologists in cloud, cybersecurity and "AI data pipelines," with a 2M sq ft Mumbai campus for ~30,000 by 2029. documented1584 The firm's own language is consistently "technology solutions, business and functional support to serve all of our clients globally."158

Lesson. Scale does not produce a domain charter. At 55k the parent still calls it support; product and risk ownership stay near the regulator and the P&L. inferred judgment

Hotstar · Star India / Novi Digital, 2015→

Full domain ownership — and full separability

Built in India from 2015, scaled to record concurrency (25.3M in 2019, ~59M in 2023), its platform reused for Disney+ in Southeast Asia, Israel, South Africa and MENA. documented1 It was a home-market product with local P&L, not a GCC charter. After losing IPL digital rights in 2022, "Hotstar lost a considerable portion of its technology team" to the Jio side; the business is now a Reliance-majority JV. documented31 Reported 2023 tech layoffs after Disney pulled tech into Burbank could not be verified. folklore

Lesson. The strongest proof an India org can own a streaming product end-to-end — and the clearest warning that a self-contained India org is easy to lose whole. inferred judgment

Warner Bros. Discovery · Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune

Platform charter, stated as such

The "India Innovation Hub" lists charters for streaming technology and content platforms, data engineering and ML, applied data science, enterprise data services, ad platforms and product management. documented10 Hyderabad opened September 2023 with ~1,200 planned in year one, described as "fundamental to building our global technology platforms, which power our suite of products worldwide." documented1086 Recruiting copy, so the charter is aspirational as well as descriptive. inferred

Lesson. The closest peer precedent: a media company standing up a data/ML platform hub in India, explicitly as platform, during a cost-cutting cycle. inferred judgment

Netflix · Mumbai

A documented absence

Netflix has no engineering site in India; the Mumbai location page lists Content, Marketing, PR, Product and Operations only. documented162 The Blind explanation — a senior-heavy culture incompatible with India's junior-heavy pyramid — is repeated everywhere and sourced nowhere. folklore

Lesson. The one streaming company that most tightly couples ownership to seniority has not built an India engineering site at all. Suggestive, not evidence. inferred judgment

Google & Amazon · Bengaluru, Hyderabad and others

"Built for India, scaled globally"

Google's Bengaluru campus post says "we have been building from India, for the world," citing Google Pay and flood forecasting. documented2 Amazon's India office pages headline "Built for India, scaled globally." documented163

Lesson. The products India owned first were India-first products that later globalized, not global products handed over. The largest India engineering orgs in the world got to ownership through a local customer. inferred judgment

What distinguished owners from back offices, read across the cases: (1) India teams first owned something whose customers or center of gravity were local — Hotstar, Google Pay — or, in Target's store-design case, a self-contained capability with clear outputs; (2) product management and data science were hired in India alongside engineering (Target, Lowe's) rather than left in the US; (3) the site head carried a global functional title, not a country title — two-thirds of "Mega GCC" heads have technical backgrounds and many run a global portfolio in addition to India;38 (4) time — the successes are 10–20-year stories. The stagnation cases are almost all support charters that never got a product or platform to own. inferred

Contractor → FTE: documented failure modes

The historical record is about captives generally, not contractor conversion specifically; the mechanisms transfer because the transaction is the same — a vendor's people become yours, and the vendor gets a say in which ones.5 inferred

  • The vendor controls who you get. Forrester's Martorelli on build-operate-transfer: "Don't expect the service provider to be anxious to simply transfer all of their best people to you." documented5 Converting your current contractors is the same transaction: the vendor decides who is offered and who is counter-offered. inferred
  • Knowledge loss. Companies that offshored to vendors "did not retain valuable skills and knowledge" (Forrester's Aase). documented164 Whatever the contractors know that is not written down leaves with the people who do not convert. inferred
  • Non-solicit and non-compete terms. No verified public source documents specific vendor non-solicitation terms or a litigated case; this is standard MSA practice and nothing more can be said about it here. folklore Do not assume you can hire your own contractors without a negotiated release fee. judgment
  • Salary-band shock. GCC hikes average 9.8%, AI/ML specialists 21.1%, with niche skills commanding ~1.7x the standard hike. documented45165 Vendor bill rates hide individual pay; converting at market GCC bands means paying materially more per head than the vendor pays the same person — or accepting only the people the market does not want. inferred
  • Leadership vacuum. Zinnov: "the first three hiring decisions shape the center more than the next ninety-seven combined"; some centers run past 100 employees with no permanent head, and hiring managers after ICs produces a site with no bridge to the global org. documented33 Momentum "can quickly sag as a result of executive turnover or loss of interest." documented5
  • Same people, different badge. No single documented case, but the mechanism is documented in pieces: converting an execution team without adding PM, data science and second-line managers leaves a Satellite-stage site, which is 43% of the population;7 and captives "become stagnant with marginal improvements over time" without external pressure (West Monroe's Borowski). documented5

Conditions under which India ownership works

Leadership seeding

The evidence favors a site head with a global functional mandate, hired first, over either a country manager or a lift-and-shift of vendor delivery leads.3833 Forrester's 2007 data warns specifically against the returning-expat-for-personal-reasons pattern.30 The norm is still thin: EY's 2025 survey found about 80% of centers have under 10% of leadership roles based in India,29 while 52% report "shared accountability for global decisions" and 26% are merely "formally consulted."157 Global roles led from India numbered 6,500+ in 2024, projected to 30,000 by 2030.15438 Zinnov's sharpest scarcity call is second-line managers "who can own outcomes… without constant escalation to the country head."29 Nothing rigorous exists on "send a US leader versus hire local"; the boomerang pattern is folklore.

Product and data science on site

The documented owners all have product management in India: Target co-locates Business, Tech, Product, Data and UX; Lowe's lists product management as a function; Hotstar was a whole company. documented15261 Zinnov's line that "maturity that took a decade is now a design choice made on day one" is marketing,7 but it reflects a real shift: new mid-market GCCs increasingly open with product and data owners instead of graduating to them. inferred

The distributed-work research

Herbsleb and Mockus (2003) found that distributed work items "take about two and one-half times as long to complete as similar items where all the work is colocated," and that the mechanism is that "distributed work items involve more people" — headcount per item strongly predicts calendar time. documented14 The delay is not in the wire; it is in needing more people to resolve each change when expertise is split.

Three further results sharpen this. Cummings, Espinosa and Pickering (108 teams, 22 countries) found that time-zone separation hurts performance more than spatial separation, because temporal boundaries are harder to cross with tooling. documented25 Carmel and Agarwal's tactics are to reduce intensive collaboration through modular, well-defined work division, and to reduce cultural and temporal distance. documented16 Ramasubbu and Balan found dispersion "significantly reduces development productivity" and hurts conformance quality, with structured process mitigating a large part of it. documented166

The counterweight is Bird et al. (2009) on Windows Vista: components built by geographically distributed teams showed a negligible difference in post-release failures versus co-located ones, smaller still after controlling for developer count — under consistent tooling, shared process, long-tenured distributed leads and site-level ownership. documented13 The companion result from the same data is that diffuse ownership — many engineers and organizations touching a binary — predicts failure-proneness better than churn, complexity or coverage. documented23 Distance did not hurt quality; diffuse ownership did.

Judgment

For a US-Pacific/IST split the working-hours overlap is 0–2.5 hours, which is the worst case in the Cummings data. Any charter that requires shared ownership of the same work items across sites — India engineers picking tickets off a US backlog, US and India engineers co-owning a pipeline — will run at roughly 2–2.5x calendar time, and the overlap window is too small to buy that back with stand-ups. Shared ownership of the same work is ruled out. What the research supports is whole-module ownership: India owns entire components or a whole domain with its own decision rights, and the cross-site interface is an API or a contract, not a meeting. Bird's result says that shape does not cost quality; Nagappan's says the alternative does.

One gap to name. Everything about decision-rights frameworks, budget ownership and on-call arrangements at India GCCs is vendor and consultant material; no primary source documents RAPID/DACI adoption, on-call ownership or budget authority at a named site. folklore The claims elsewhere on this site about decision rights are therefore argued from the distributed-work research and the case pattern, not from GCC-specific data. judgment

Talent constraints, 2024–2026

The two big surveys disagree on attrition by six to seven points. EY reports GCC attrition falling 13% → 11% → 9% from 2023 to 2025, the lowest of any India tech segment, on a sample averaging ~800 heads per center.157 Zinnov reports 15.4% → 13.3% → 15.1% → 16% into 2026, with high-performer attrition at 16.5% and AI/cloud roles at 18–25% on 18–24-month tenures.45 Different samples, different definitions. Use 9–16% for the site overall and 18–25% for the data and ML roles the org actually needs. judgment IT services attrition is now in the same band — Infosys 12.9%, TCS 12.3%, Wipro 14.5% — so the GCC advantage is at the senior and niche end, not the mass.167

Judgment

The evidence discriminates on three things and is silent on the rest. It rules out shared ownership of the same work across a 12.5-hour gap; it shows that the graduated sites hired product, data science and managers before ownership moved, on a 10–15-year clock against a 27%-in-five-years base rate; and it shows platform charters scale and drift in equal measure unless a product outcome depends on them. It does not tell you which domain, which components, or how to write a graduation gate — those are choices. The Recommendation page makes them; this page is what it stands on.
