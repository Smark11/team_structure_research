5.1The base rates

India has 2,117 global capability centers across 3,728 units, employing about 2.36 million people on $98.4B of revenue in FY26,5 up from roughly 1,700 centers, 1.9 million people and $64.6B two years earlier.142 Zinnov counts about 110 new centres opened between early 2024 and late 2025, EY more than 120 since January 2023 — roughly 50 a year — and 35% of mid-market centres were set up in the last two years.141143 Common, and mostly stuck: read the distribution, not the totals.

Who is counting

Every population figure in this section comes from Zinnov, EY or ANSR — firms that sell GCC set-ups, surveys to GCCs, or both. Treat their maturity and attrition numbers as a ceiling on how good things are, not a floor. Where two of them disagree, both figures are shown.

Zinnov’s maturity ladder runs Outpost → Satellite → Portfolio Hub → Transformation Hub, and puts today’s population at 13% / 43% / 39% / 5%.5 Read plainly: 56% of India GCCs sit in the two execution tiers and 44% in the two ownership tiers, where Portfolio Hub means real end-to-end ownership of a portfolio.5 That is a population snapshot, not a graduation rate. Zinnov separately states that 27% of centres reach its portfolio-hub tier within five years of set-up; the method behind that figure is not published, so it is reported here as Zinnov’s claim. It says how many centers are execution shops today; it says nothing about how many centers chartered as extensions ever leave that tier, and no cohort graduation rate is published — Zinnov does not follow centers over time, and nobody else does either. The snapshot comes from the firm that sells center set-ups, so read the 44% as a ceiling. inferred

The older data is worse. Forrester found in 2007 that “more than 60% of the captive centers in India alone are struggling,” citing lack of management support, spiraling costs, skyrocketing attrition and lack of integration — and noted that many captives existed because an expatriate executive wanted to move home.144 The 2008–09 cohort was then sold off: Citi sold Citigroup Global Services to TCS for ~$505M in October 2008 and its Indian IT unit to Wipro in 2009, each tied to a multi-year service contract; AXA’s 600-person captive went to Capita; AOL, Aviva, Prudential UK and Philips put theirs on the block.28145 The analyst reasons at the time were fixed cost, management distraction, inflexible capacity in downturns, and a talent market that preferred vendor employment for career growth.28 That last reason has inverted — GCCs now pay 25–40% above IT services at mid-senior levels and post the lowest attrition of any segment47146 — but the other three have not. inferred

The current population is healthier: Everest counted 452 new set-ups in 2023, and about 50 captives sold in 2020–23 out of 1,450 created.29 A ~3% divestiture rate is a different world from 2009. What has not changed is the shape of the ladder: most sites are execution shops today, and nothing published says how many were chartered that way and left.5

5.2Reference sites

Eight cards, nine companies; one of them is an absence. Each card says what charter the site took, how it moved, and the one lesson that transfers to a ~140-person data org. Headcounts are the parent’s own numbers unless tagged otherwise.1627

Target · Bengaluru, 2005→

IT support → “integrated global headquarters”

Opened via ANSR “with a small team focused on building IT capabilities to support the US headquarters.” documented33 Now 5,700+, ~40% technology, with Business, Tech, Product, Data and UX co-located; the India store-design team does about half of new store designs and 80% of remodels. documented16 The operating rule its president states: “…ownership of outcomes resting with where the center of gravity for the capability sits.”16 ANSR’s “25% of Target’s global workforce” can only mean corporate workforce. inferred33

Lesson. A hybrid by design, reached over roughly two decades, by hiring product, data and UX alongside engineering — not by handing over a customer domain on day one. inferred judgment

Walmart Global Tech · Bengaluru, Chennai, Gurugram

Global platform work beside a separately owned local product

Walmart says its India teams “build technology solutions to help millions of Walmart customers around the world” across e-commerce, supply chain and payments; Chennai was added in 2021 and global WGT headcount passed 20,000 in 2022. documented39147 Flipkart and PhonePe are separate operating companies, not GCC charters. documented148 Aggregator claims that Bengaluru “owns” global search, pricing and payments have no Walmart statement behind them. folklore

Lesson. The documented shape is a structural hybrid: global-platform work plus a locally owned product company next door. inferred judgment

Lowe’s India · Bengaluru, 2015→

“One team, multiple locations” → platform VPs in India

Opened February 2015 as a ~500-person Global Innovation Center on customer experience, technology and analytics; the MD’s stated model was “one team, multiple locations” — an explicit extension charter. documented34 Ten years on: 5,000+ associates, with India-based VPs for Applied AI/Data & Analytics, Omnichannel Platforms and Engineering Platforms, product management as a listed function, under an SVP who is both CTO and MD. documented25

Lesson. The cleanest graduating-extension case on record — and it graduated into platform-and-capability ownership with resident PM, not customer-domain P&L. inferred judgment

JPMorgan · Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad

The largest support charter in the country

50,000+ in August 2023, ~55,000 in 2026, adding ~1,000 technologists in cloud, cybersecurity and “AI data pipelines,” with a 2M sq ft Mumbai campus for ~30,000 by 2029. documented327 The firm’s own language is consistently “technology solutions, business and functional support to serve all of our clients globally.”27

Lesson. Scale does not produce a domain charter. At 55k the parent still calls it support; product and risk ownership stay near the regulator and the P&L. inferred judgment

Hotstar · Star India / Novi Digital, 2015→

Full domain ownership — and full separability

Built in India from 2015, scaled to record concurrency, platform reused across Disney+ markets; a home-market product with local P&L, not a GCC charter. documented1 After the 2022 IPL rights loss its technology team drained toward Jio — per one trade newsletter quoting an unnamed executive inferred26 — and the business is now a Reliance-majority JV.1 Full account: Case Studies, Disney.

Lesson. The strongest proof an India org can own a streaming product end-to-end. Its dispersal was an economics story — the team followed the cricket rights — and shows that a self-contained site follows its market’s P&L, which is true of any charter; it does not show that domain charters fail. inferred judgment

Warner Bros. Discovery · Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune

Platform charter, stated as such

The “India Innovation Hub” lists charters for streaming technology and content platforms, data engineering and ML, applied data science, enterprise data services, ad platforms and product management. documented12 Hyderabad opened September 2023 with ~1,200 planned in year one, described as “fundamental to building our global technology platforms, which power our suite of products worldwide.” documented1255 Recruiting copy, so the charter is aspirational as well as descriptive. inferred

Lesson. The closest peer precedent: a media company standing up a data/ML platform hub in India, explicitly as platform, during a cost-cutting cycle. inferred judgment

Netflix · Mumbai

A documented absence

Netflix has no engineering site in India; the Mumbai location page lists Content, Marketing, PR, Product and Operations only. documented149 The Blind explanation — a senior-heavy culture incompatible with India’s junior-heavy pyramid — is repeated everywhere and sourced nowhere. folklore

Lesson. The one streaming company that most tightly couples ownership to seniority has not built an India engineering site at all. Suggestive, not evidence. inferred judgment

Google & Amazon · Bengaluru, Hyderabad and others

“Built for India, scaled globally”

Google’s Bengaluru campus post says “we have been building from India, for the world,” citing Google Pay and flood forecasting. documented2 Amazon’s India office pages headline “Built for India, scaled globally.” documented150

Lesson. The products India owned first were India-first products that later globalized, not global products handed over. The largest India engineering orgs in the world got to ownership through a local customer. inferred judgment

What distinguished owners from back offices, read across the cases: (1) India teams first owned something whose customers or center of gravity were local — Hotstar, Google Pay — or, in Target’s store-design case, a self-contained capability with clear outputs; (2) product management and data science were hired in India alongside engineering (Target, Lowe’s) rather than left in the US; (3) the site head carried a global functional title, not a country title — two-thirds of “Mega GCC” heads have technical backgrounds and many run a global portfolio in addition to India;141 (4) time — the successes are 10–20-year stories. The stagnation cases are almost all support charters that never got a product or platform to own. inferred

5.3Contractor → FTE: documented failure modes

The historical record is about captives generally, not contractor conversion specifically; the mechanisms transfer because the transaction is the same — a vendor’s people become yours, and the vendor gets a say in which ones.29 inferred

  • The vendor controls who you get. Forrester’s Martorelli on build-operate-transfer: “Don’t expect the service provider to be anxious to simply transfer all of their best people to you.” documented29 Converting your current contractors is the same transaction: the vendor decides who is offered and who is counter-offered. inferred
  • Knowledge loss. Companies that offshored to vendors “did not retain valuable skills and knowledge” (Forrester’s Aase). documented151 Whatever the contractors know that is not written down leaves with the people who do not convert. inferred
  • Non-solicit and non-compete terms. No verified public source documents specific vendor non-solicitation terms or a litigated case; this is standard MSA practice and nothing more can be said about it here. folklore Do not assume you can hire your own contractors without a negotiated release fee. judgment
  • Salary-band shock. GCC hikes average 9.8%, AI/ML specialists 21.1%, with niche skills commanding ~1.7x the standard hike. documented51152 Vendor bill rates hide individual pay; converting at market GCC bands means paying materially more per head than the vendor pays the same person — or accepting only the people the market does not want. inferred
  • Leadership vacuum. Zinnov: “the first three hiring decisions shape the center more than the next ninety-seven combined”; some centers run past 100 employees with no permanent head, and hiring managers after ICs produces a site with no bridge to the global org. documented40 Momentum “can quickly sag as a result of executive turnover or loss of interest.” documented29
  • Same people, different badge. No single documented case, but the mechanism is documented in pieces: converting an execution team without adding PM, data science and second-line managers (the India-centre literature’s term for managers who own outcomes without escalating to the country head; the memo calls them engineering managers) leaves a Satellite-stage site, which is 43% of the population;5 and captives “become stagnant with marginal improvements over time” without external pressure (West Monroe’s Borowski). documented29

5.4Conditions under which India ownership works

Leadership seeding

The evidence favors a site head with a global functional mandate, hired first, over either a country manager or a lift-and-shift of vendor delivery leads.40141 Forrester’s 2007 data warns specifically against the returning-expat-for-personal-reasons pattern.144 The norm is still thin: EY’s 2025 survey found about 80% of centers have under 10% of leadership roles based in India,32 while 52% report “shared accountability for global decisions” and 26% are merely “formally consulted.”146 Global roles led from India numbered 6,500+ in 2024, projected to 30,000 by 2030.141142 EY’s 2025 survey, as reported by VARINDIA, names the missing layer as second-line managers who can run complex teams without escalating to the country head; Zinnov’s version is the 7:1–8:1 candidate ratio and hiring across the pyramid.32141 Nothing rigorous exists on “send a US leader versus hire local”; the boomerang pattern is. folklore

Product and data science on site

The documented owners all have product management in India: Target co-locates Business, Tech, Product, Data and UX; Lowe’s lists product management as a function; Hotstar was a whole company. documented11625 Zinnov’s line that “maturity that took a decade is now a design choice made on day one” is marketing,5 but it reflects a real shift: new mid-market GCCs increasingly open with product and data owners instead of graduating to them. inferred

Cross-border personal data

judgment None of the reports behind this site covers data-transfer law, so this is a condition stated without a source, not a finding. QoE telemetry carries device, IP and account identifiers; fraud and paid-sharing data carries payment tokens, household graphs and, for European subscribers, EU-scoped personal data. Processing either in India runs through India’s DPDP Act 2023 and whatever transfer mechanisms the company already operates. For QoE that is a Phase 0 question with a named owner — privacy counsel, answered by month 3, before the first transfer. For fraud it is possibly disqualifying: if the answer is “pseudonymized only” or “no EU data,” the domain is not transferable as scoped, and the month-18 decision is between ads measurement (if the org has an ads tier) and the ML-platform expansion; neither is a fallback. Get it from counsel in Phase 0, not at the phase gate.

The distributed-work research

The distance research (Foundations §3.10) gives the base rate for the alternative: cross-site work items took about 2.5× as long at Lucent because more people were needed per item, not because anyone was slower;6 temporal separation costs more than distance;24 and Microsoft found no quality penalty for distributed work once ownership was site-level and tooling identical, while diffuse ownership predicted defects at 86% precision.415

Calendar time per work item2.5×

Co-located vs. cross-site items at Lucent; the mechanism is more people per item, not slower people.6

Judgment

For a Pacific/Eastern-to-IST split the overlap is zero at nominal hours and at most 2.5 hours if Eastern and India both stretch; Pacific never reaches it (Applying It, Fig 4.3). Cummings found temporal separation costs more than distance, and this is about as separated as two sites get. Any charter that requires shared ownership of the same work items across sites — India engineers picking tickets off a US backlog, US and India engineers co-owning a pipeline — pays the Herbsleb penalty on every item, and the overlap window is too small to buy that back with stand-ups. Shared ownership of the same work is ruled out. What the research supports is whole-module ownership: India owns entire components or a whole domain with its own decision rights, and the cross-site interface is an API or a contract, not a meeting. Bird’s result says that shape does not cost quality; Nagappan’s says the alternative does. None of it is GCC-specific data — no primary source documents decision rights, budget authority or on-call at a named India site — so it is argued from the distributed-work research and the case pattern.

5.5Talent constraints, 2024–2026

The two big surveys disagree on attrition by six to seven points. EY reports GCC attrition falling 13% → 11% → 9% from 2023 to 2025, the lowest of any India tech segment, on a sample averaging ~800 heads per center.146 Zinnov reports 15.4% → 13.3% → 15.1% → 16% into 2026, with high-performer attrition at 16.5% and AI/cloud roles at 18–25% on 18–24-month tenures.51 Different samples, different definitions. Use 9–16% for the site overall and 18–25% for the data and ML roles the org needs. judgment IT services attrition is now in the same band — Infosys 12.9%, TCS 12.3%, Wipro 14.5% — so the GCC advantage is at the senior and niche end, not the mass.153

Two things in that table bind the charter. First, senior scarcity: 77% of 2025 GCC hiring was expected to be mid-to-senior, up from 63%, and the scarce thing is second-line managers, not engineers, at candidate-to-hire ratios of 7:1 to 8:1.3240 Second, the supply shock cuts both ways: ~80,000 roles disappeared at the top five IT services firms over 18 months to mid-2025 while GCCs are on track to hire more than 510,000 people in 2026, and 63% of GCCs are hiring niche AI/ML and 54% data engineering — every center is bidding for the same people this org needs.47146 Claims that Hyderabad or Pune run 10–25% cheaper than Bengaluru for data roles appear only in staffing-firm blogs. folklore

judgment The 18–25% row is the one a gate has to be set against. An attrition gate below the base rate for the roles being converted is decorative: it fails in a normal year, gets waived, and teaches everyone that the gates are optional. The Recommendation’s gate is therefore at the market and directional — regretted attrition of the converted cohort at or below 25% annualized and not rising quarter over quarter — with retention grants vesting at months 18 and 30 as the instrument behind it. At 18–25% on a converted cohort of 30–35, expect 12–18 departures by month 30 regardless; the gate detects a vendor-controlled or under-banded transfer, it does not prevent normal churn, which is why the plan carries a backfill line for it (Applying It §4.5).

Judgment

The evidence discriminates on three things and is silent on the rest. It rules out shared ownership of the same work across the twelve-hour gap; it shows that the graduated sites hired product, data science and managers before ownership was described as theirs, on a 10–20-year clock, in a population where 56% of centers are still execution shops and no graduation rate exists; and it shows platform charters scale, with thinner evidence than the folklore that they drift unless a product outcome depends on them. It does not tell you which domain, which components, or how to write a graduation gate — those are choices.
