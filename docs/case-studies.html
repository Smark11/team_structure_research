How eight streaming and media companies organize data

Eight companies, 2012–2026. Every one keeps the platform central; every new hub got a capability, not a domain.

2.1What all eight have in common

Eight companies, one shape. Every company in this set that runs data at scale keeps the platform central and puts the people near the domain: Netflix, Spotify, Google and Disney each run one experimentation platform, and NBCU centralized data under a CDO while keeping teams embedded with the businesses.2023505657 documented The counter-examples are retrofits after failure: Spotify’s squads negotiating experiment buckets by hand until 2018, Amazon’s 25 finance databases until 2019, Paramount’s two clouds until 2026.445658 documented “Embedded” means embedded people, never embedded platforms; nobody publicly runs per-domain metric definitions or per-domain A/B stacks.56 inferred

The five remote sites chartered as new hubs by a parent — Netflix Warsaw, Spotify London, Amazon Hyderabad, Prime Video Bengaluru and WBD India — hold capability charters.812 documented The four other documented sites are different cases: Google Zurich grew into product ownership over roughly eighteen yearsdocumented13, Sky’s Osterley is the site the Peacock platform came frominferred59, Spotify Boston kept the charter it was acquired with, and Hotstar was a home-market product.114 documented None of them is a data organization.12 inferred The table carries the evidence; the timeline shows when each company moved.

2.2Netflix

Why it matters here

judgment Netflix is the closest documented analogue to a mid-sized data org with one central leader: one reporting line and hiring bar, people aligned to verticals, the org rather than the partner prioritizing scarce talent. Its only non-US engineering hub got infrastructure and production tooling, not a data domain. That is the pattern a ~140-person org should expect to copy, not the exception.

Structure

Since at least 2020 Netflix has run a single Data Science and Engineering group spanning data engineering, analytics engineering, visualization engineering, experimentation and causal-inference science, and algorithms research.2250 documented The 2020 analytics post states the design principle: “we align our analytics professionals with the listed business area verticals rather than organizing them within a single functional horizontal,” so that they “possess deep business context”; the verticals are content, studio, membership and growth, marketing, product, streaming and platform, and finance.50 documented That is a hub-and-spoke: one reporting line, one craft standard, people deployed into verticals. inferred Analytics engineers are “a specialized resource and a rare commodity” who “are given freedom to choose their projects” by business impact, so the central org, not the business partner, allocates scarce talent.50 documented

How it changed and why

Elizabeth Stone joined in 2020 to run product data science and engineering, became Netflix’s first CTO in October 2023, and in February 2026 became Chief Product and Technology Officer over “product, engineering, and data teams”.7981 documented The data leader absorbed product and engineering, not the reverse; that is the strongest public signal of a centralized data org that reports high.81 inferred No public statement ties a data-org reorg to the 2022 ads-tier and paid-sharing upheaval; the shape looks stable across the 2020–2024 posts.5060 inferred

Where experimentation sits

The January 2022 post by Martin Tingley and colleagues lays it out: several data science teams “partner directly with Product Managers, engineering teams, and other business units,” a central experimentation platform (XP) provides allocation, measurement and tooling, and Netflix “intentionally encourage[s] collaboration between the centralized experimentation platform and the data science teams that partner directly with Netflix business units”.22 documented Embedded scientists “own all aspects of the analysis of a study” and bring “our analytics point of view to lively cross-functional debates on roll-out decisions”; launch decisions belong to neither the scientist nor the platform.61 documented The ML platform follows the same split: one team provides “an entire ecosystem of tools around Metaflow” on a single company-wide S3/Iceberg lake, and “all the use cases were engineered by practitioners themselves”.60 documented

Global sites

Engineering is overwhelmingly in Los Gatos and Los Angeles. In January 2023 Netflix announced its first engineering hub outside the US, in Warsaw, to “help build the products that our internal and external creative partners use to deliver Netflix shows and films”.880 documented By March 2026 Warsaw held ~300 staff, was described as “Netflix’s only technology hub outside of the United States,” and its engineering was focused on infrastructure, gaming and production technology.8 documented India offices (Mumbai; Hyderabad opened March 2026) are content, production and AVGC, not engineering.99 documented No data-science or analytics charter for Warsaw appears in any public statement.8 inferred

What is attributable

Nothing public separates the structure’s contribution from the “context not control” culture the same posts rely on.2250 documented What can be defended: central platform plus embedded scientists scaled to hundreds of ML projects and company-wide experimentation without fragmenting into per-domain platforms.2260 inferred

2.3Spotify

Why it matters here

judgment Spotify is the cautionary tale for “autonomous teams, no central platform”: squads negotiating experiment buckets by hand in 2017, and a CEO in 2023 calling out “work around the work.” Its Boston hub is also the best precedent for graduation, because it owned a whole capability from day one. Do not import the 2012 whitepaper; import the 2018 platform and the 2023 verdict.

Structure

The October 2012 Kniberg and Ivarsson whitepaper described ~30 squads across three cities, squads as “mini-startups” with long-term missions, tribes capped “smaller than 100 people or so,” and chapters and guilds for craft alignment; it opened with the disclaimer everyone skips: “This article is only a snapshot of our current way of working — a journey in progress, not a journey completed”.82 documented Squads were told to use “metrics and A/B testing to find out what really works”.82 documented An undated (c. 2018) Spotify Insights post puts data scientists and user researchers together in Product Insights teams embedded with product areas, “not a centralized function”.62 inferred

How it changed and why

Spotify’s own people disowned the “model” almost immediately. Kniberg wrote in June 2015 that the material “wasn’t actually intended to be a generic framework or ‘model’ at all”.100 documented Ivarsson wrote in July 2016 that “Spotify is not a model” and named cross-team alignment as a growing problem.101 documented Jeremiah Lee, a former Spotify PM, wrote in April 2020 that the model “was only ever aspirational” and that leadership “incrementally transitioned to more traditional management structures”.37 documented

Then the 2023 re-centralization. On 23 January 2023 Spotify cut ~6% and named Gustav Söderström Chief Product Officer and co-president overseeing “the majority of our engineering and product work,” to “drive more efficiency, control costs, and speed up decision-making” after 2022 opex growth “outpaced our revenue growth by 2X”.83 documented On 4 December 2023 it cut ~17%; Daniel Ek wrote that “By most metrics, we were more productive but less efficient. We need to be both,” and that there were “too many people dedicated to supporting work and even doing work around the work”.84 documented Ek never names the squad model, but that is a management verdict on an autonomy-heavy structure: the cost was coordination, not output.84 inferred

Where experimentation sits

Central, and increasingly so. A team was spun up in 2013 to build the ABBA tool; a 2017 hack-week diagnosis found experiments coupled one-to-one to feature flags, A/B events at ~25% of total event volume, data scientists running inconsistent notebook analyses, and teams “manually coordinating bucket ranges between experiments,” which was “error prone”; the 2018 Experimentation Platform with a central Metrics Catalog followed.56 documented By 2023 it served “hundreds of squads and thousands of developers,” had scaled from “fewer than 20 priority experiments per year” to thousands, and was externalized as Confidence.64 documented In 2025 the platform team reported a 64% learning rate against a 12% win rate, automated fail-rate checks (sample-ratio mismatch, pre-exposure imbalance, misconfigured serving), and an “engagement team” acting as a “center of excellence for internal customer success,” noting that “adding experiment reviewers” can “drastically impact learning rates”.21 documented The data platform itself is a dedicated organization with Data Collection, Data Processing and Data Management areas, serving “payments to experimentation”.63 documented

Global sites

R&D hubs as of 2019 were Stockholm, Gothenburg, New York, San Francisco, Boston, and London, opened April 2019 as “Spotify’s first major technology center outside the US and Sweden”.9 documented Boston exists because of the March 2014 acquisition of The Echo Nest, a music-intelligence company whose Somerville office Spotify committed to keep running.14 documented Nothing public assigns India an R&D charter; non-Stockholm hubs grew from an acquired capability or talent-market access and hold product-area and capability charters, not data-domain charters.914 inferred

What is attributable

The one clean structure-to-outcome link is negative and self-reported: squad autonomy produced coordination waste in experimentation in 2017 and “work around the work” company-wide in 2023.5684 documented The growth to thousands of experiments a year belongs to the platform and its center-of-excellence practices, not to the squad model.2164 inferred

2.4YouTube / Google

Why it matters here

judgment Google supplies two things: the oldest documented experiment review gate (a council of engineers with a checklist) and the only remote site that ever grew into product ownership. The second took eighteen years and 5,000 people. A 140-person org should plan on the first and not on the second.

Structure

Google publishes almost nothing about YouTube’s data-science org; job-title conventions suggest an embedded-by-product model with a shared job ladder rather than one central analytics org. inferred The one primary artifact is the 2010 KDD paper on Google’s overlapping experiment infrastructure, which is company infrastructure, not a product-team tool.20 documented

How it changed and why

No public YouTube data-org reorg exists; the only Google structure event on record, the April 2023 merger of Brain and DeepMind into Google DeepMind, is frontier-ML centralization and not relevant here.2086 documented

Where experimentation sits

Tang, Agarwal, O’Brien and Meyer describe a domains/layers/experiments infrastructure deployed in March 2007 and are explicit that “an experiment infrastructure alone is insufficient”: they added automated data-file checks, real-time monitoring, standardized metrics so that “two experimenters should use the same filters,” an experiment council, “a group of engineers who review a light-weight checklist that experimenters fill out prior to running their experiment,” and a forum where results are interpreted and archived.20 documented Governance is a central review gate staffed by engineers, not a data-science sign-off; product teams design and run their own experiments.20 documented Whether YouTube runs on this exact stack today is not public.20 inferred

Global sites

Zurich is Google’s largest development center outside the US: ~2,000 people in 2017 growing toward 5,000, hosting Search, Maps, Calendar, YouTube, Gmail, Assistant, Photos and Translate engineering and described as the European home base for ML.85 documented By 2022 it held ~5,000 staff and “Zurich is currently the largest headquarters of the YouTube development team alongside the headquarters in San Bruno”.13 documented That is a product-engineering charter at full-peer scale, the best example in this set of a non-HQ site owning product surface area; whether Zurich owns YouTube’s analytics function is not documented.13 inferred

What is attributable

Only the 2010 paper’s own result: experiment count, launches and distinct experimenters all rose after the 2007 infrastructure and the council and forum processes.20 documented

2.5Amazon & Prime Video

Why it matters here

judgment Amazon is the fully decentralized end of the spectrum, and it paid for it in 25 finance databases. Its India sites hold service charters with single-threaded leaders on site. If the ~140-person org ever moves a domain to India, the Amazon lesson is to move the decision-maker and the whole separable team, not just the analysts.

Structure

AWS’s own explainer: two-pizza teams under ten people with “single-threaded ownership” of one product or service and its full lifecycle, adopted to “minimize lines of communication and decrease overhead of bureaucracy”.66 documented Bryar and Carr’s Working Backwards, as summarized by Schuler, records the correction: “the biggest predictor of a team’s success was not whether it was small” but its dependencies, so Amazon shifted emphasis from team size to a single-threaded leader with a separable team.7 inferred

…the best way to fail at inventing something is by making it somebody’s part-time job.Bryar & Carr, as summarized by Schuler7

Amazon has no company-wide data organization; data engineers, BIEs, scientists and economists report into business orgs. inferred Werner Vogels documents the consequence: before the Galaxy data lake, operations-finance data lived in “more than 25 databases with regional teams creating their own local version of datasets”; a finance-operations team, not a central data org, built Galaxy in 2019.58 documented

How it changed and why

The structural change on record is the single-threaded-leader correction and the 2019 Galaxy retrofit; both were responses to coordination cost, and both moved toward a shared layer without creating a central data org.758 documented

Where experimentation sits

Weblab is Amazon’s internal A/B platform; Ron Kohavi “was the director of data mining and personalization at Amazon, where he was responsible for Weblab,” which places it in the early 2000s.67 documented The circulated numbers (“created 2011, 546 experiments year one, 12,000+ per year now”) conflict with Kohavi’s tenure.102 folklore What is safely inferable: a central platform used by business teams under their own single-threaded leaders, with launch decisions inside the owning team and no public central council.67 inferred

Global sites

Prime Video engineering is organized as Prime Video Experience and Technology, “the technical backbone of Amazon Prime Video’s global streaming service,” covering discovery and search, playback, download, live, encoding, delivery, client experiences, content protection and anti-fraud.11 documented Amazon’s Hyderabad campus, opened August 2019, is its largest building worldwide at 15,000 seats, hosting AWS, Kindle, Alexa and Amazon.in teams; India country manager Amit Agarwal said “This facility will build services globally”.10 documented Prime Video’s Bengaluru site is expanding “spanning across X-Ray, playback services, live technologies, player and client testing and other foundational technologies,” with a Principal Engineer role to “provide the needed engineering and architecture guidance” and “work with other global Prime Video teams”.11 documented India sites hold capability and service charters inside global orgs with single-threaded leaders on site; none is carved out as “India owns the commerce data domain”.11 inferred The oft-repeated “Amazon IDC began in Hyderabad in 2004” has no primary source.10 folklore

What is attributable

Two things: the single-threaded-leader model’s self-diagnosed failure mode (dependencies, not size) and the silo cost that forced Galaxy.758 documented Prime Video’s product outcomes cannot be tied to its org structure from public material.11 inferred

2.6Disney

Why it matters here

judgment Disney is the legacy-media control case on both axes. Its one documented experimentation org is central platform plus federated domains, and the domains it lists are this reader’s domains. Its one India engineering org of scale owned a whole market end to end, which is what made it possible to lose whole: the team followed the rights, and the business followed the JV. Own the platform layer; do not build an island.

Structure

Disney’s streaming tech org has been reorganized four times in eight years, each time for a CEO-level strategy reason. March 2018: the Direct-to-Consumer and International segment under Kevin Mayer bundled “BAMTECH (technology and digital products center)” with Hulu, ESPN+ and international channels.8789 documented Since February 2023 technology has been one shared org, Disney Entertainment and ESPN Technology, reporting jointly to the Disney Entertainment co-chairs and the ESPN chairman: one tech org, three P&Ls.103 documented Since August 2024 Adam Smith, from YouTube, has run it as Chief Product and Technology Officer with “data scientists” explicitly in his org; product-side data science sits inside P&T, not a corporate data function.68 documented

How it changed and why

On 12 October 2020 Chapek collapsed DTCI and Media Networks into Disney Media and Entertainment Distribution under Kareem Daniel, responsible for “P&L management and all distribution, operations, sales, advertising, data and technology functions worldwide”; the most centralized Disney’s data and tech ever got.88 documented Iger returned, Daniel exited, and on 8 February 2023 DMED was unwound into Disney Entertainment, ESPN and Experiences to put “decision-making back in the hands of our creative teams”; DMED lasted 28 months.89 documented In April 2026, after SVP Ajay Arora left, the Data Product and Engineering group was moved under Tony Donohoe, EVP of the ad platform; the “Commerce, Data and Identity” alliance that had overseen commerce, growth and account management was dissolved, and the internal “Atlas” data initiative was cited as needing “tighter integration with ad technology systems,” all during a ~1,000-person cut.43 documented Data went from a monetization chief (2020) to the content businesses (2023) and back to ad-tech (2026); a media data org’s reporting line follows whichever P&L is being asked to grow this year.43 inferred In parallel, Hulu was absorbed onto the BAMTech-lineage stack over 2023–2026 without a headcount event: Hulu on Disney+ shipped 27 March 2024, and the 2025 P&T cuts were under 2%.52104106 inferred

Where experimentation sits

Disney Streaming’s blog is the only first-party account of an experimentation org in the legacy-media set. In 2019 it formed a central Experiment-X team because “different teams were using different tools,” organized the team “around domains like product, backend engineering, data science and audience intelligence,” and gave a Core Experimentation sub-team ownership of automated, standardized analysis (the ExpAn library) so stakeholders did not depend on analysts; by January 2022 it had onboarded 19 stakeholder teams running experiments across “Landing Page, Sign Up Funnel, Commerce, and Quality of Service (QoS),” with experiment velocity in the OKRs.23 documented That is central platform plus federated execution, scoped across Disney+, Star+, Hulu and ESPN+ before the apps themselves were merged.23 documented The data platform: a Kinesis/Flink real-time pipeline feeding recommendations and operational analytics (2020), and data governance as a dedicated function under a VP on Snowflake with “a single copy of data” (2021).6970 documented

Global sites

India is the important case. Disney+ Hotstar (April 2020) was built on Hotstar’s own platform, not BAMTech’s, to “take advantage of Hotstar’s existing infrastructure and customer base,” with a UX distinct from global Disney+.1 documented Hotstar was a full-stack, full-domain engineering org from 2015 with its own infrastructure and data platform.107 inferred After the IPL digital rights went to Viacom18/Jio in 2022, “Hotstar lost a considerable portion of its technology team” to the Jio side along with sports and sales leadership; the team followed the rights economics.26 inferred In November 2024 Disney folded its whole Indian TV and streaming business into the JioStar JV (Reliance 63.16%, Disney 36.84%, Reliance operating), with the apps merged into JioHotstar on 14 February 2025.1 documented Disney’s only India engineering center of scale was a home-market product with full ownership of a market, and it is no longer Disney’s; there is no Disney streaming engineering site in India on the public record after the JV.1 inferred

What is attributable

Disney streaming’s swing from ~$4B annual losses to profit across 2023–2025 is driven by pricing, password-sharing enforcement, content cuts and the bundle, not org design.105 folklore What is defensible: one shared tech org across three P&Ls made the Hulu and Star+ consolidations reusable, and a centralization that ignored where P&L accountability sat (DMED) did not survive a CEO change.8990 documented

2.7Warner Bros. Discovery

Why it matters here

judgment WBD has the most explicit India charter in the set, and it is capability-shaped: data engineering, ML, applied data science, ad platforms, enterprise data. It was stood up in four months at peak cost-cutting and grew while US headcount fell. That is what a platform charter looks like when a CFO, not a CTO, sponsors it.

Structure

Two stacks before the merger: HBO Max launched May 2020 on WarnerMedia’s platform; folklore Discovery+ launched January 2021 on Discovery’s global DTC platform.91 documented After the April 2022 merger, CTO Avi Saxena and CPO Tyler Whitworth both came from Discovery; the acquirer’s smaller tech leadership took over the combined org.93 documented WBD’s public data footprint is almost entirely ad sales: the “Olli” first-party data platform (April 2024) runs on Snowflake clean roomsdocumented and appears to sit in ad sales.71 inferred Nothing public says where streaming product analytics, experimentation or data science report; data appears to run as embedded functions with no single owner.71 inferred

How it changed and why

In his one substantive interview, Saxena described twelve months of planning “to build a cohesive platform” with “engineer teams from both HBO Max and Discovery+ sides” contributing, a “20% faster” bar and four CDNs; he did not say “Discovery+ stack”.93 documented The August 2022 HBO Max cuts (70 people, 14% of the content org) were content, casting and international, not tech; the July 2024 ~1,000 cuts were finance and business affairs with “cuts to Max staffers in the single digits”.53108 documented “HBO Max engineers left en masse” has no source that quantifies it.53 folklore In June 2025 WBD announced a split into Streaming and Studios (HBO, HBO Max) and Global Networks (CNN, Discovery, Discovery+; later renamed Discovery Global), re-splitting the portfolio by brand while still sharing a platform.94 documented Paramount Skydance’s agreement to buy Streaming and Studios is expected to close in Q3 2026 by one report, with a 1 June 2027 outside date.4495 documented

Where experimentation sits

Unknown. No public source places WBD’s streaming experimentation function; the only documented data org is the ad-sales platform.71 inferred

Global sites

The India hub was announced 17 May 2023 as an “International Development Centre” with 1,200 professionals in year one, and inaugurated 20 September 2023 with CFO Gunnar Wiedenfels present.55109 documented That the CFO, not the CTO, fronted it signals the mandate.55 inferred WBD’s careers site describes an “India Innovation Hub” across Bangalore, Hyderabad and Pune with charters in streaming technology and content platforms; data engineering and machine learning; applied data science; identity, partnerships, trust and safety; enterprise data services and automation; product management; and advertising platforms.12 documented The charter is capability and platform ownership across streaming, data/ML, ad-tech and enterprise data, not a market or a product domain; it was stood up at peak cost-cutting and grew while US headcount shrank.12 inferred

What is attributable

Max was more stable than HBO Max at launch, which was the explicit goal.93 documented A single-stack, single-leadership decision made within ~4 months of close produced a shippable combined product in 13 months, versus Disney’s 2019–2026 Hulu absorption, at the cost of the acquired side’s engineering culture; the 2025 split shows the consolidation was product-level, not durable.9394 inferred

2.8NBCUniversal / Peacock

Why it matters here

judgment NBCU wrote down the two things nobody else did: a dual-accountability sentence (“embedded with and equally accountable to”) and a non-HQ site that owns the entire streaming platform and got it reused three times. Both are directly reusable phrasing for an India charter.

Structure

Peacock was built in ~12 months as a cloud-native AWS system by joint NBCU and Sky teams; the AWS case study quotes Sky’s platform and reliability leads alongside Peacock CTO Patrick Miceli.59 documented The UK team was the NOW TV team.59 inferred By 2024 Miceli was CTO, Direct-to-Consumer and Global Streaming, at NBCUniversal and Sky, running a platform across 70+ countries powering Peacock, SkyShowtime and Showmax.72 documented Ad-tech data is separate: “One Platform” and the NBCUnified identity graph (200M person IDs) sit in Global Advertising and Partnerships; NBCU has two data orgs.73 documented

How it changed and why

In January 2023 NBCU merged “research and decision sciences teams including applied analytics, data engineering, and data science into one portfolio-wide Television & Streaming organization” under Will Gonzalez, promoted from Peacock CDO to EVP and Chief Data Officer reporting to chairman Mark Lazarus. Lazarus’s memo says the research teams from Entertainment Networks, NBC Sports and Telemundo join the central org but “will remain embedded with and equally accountable to those businesses”; the stated trigger was content windowing across networks and Peacock.57 documented This is the only explicit public articulation of a dual-accountability data model in the set.57 documented In January 2026 USA, CNBC, MSNBC, E!, Golf, Fandango and Rotten Tomatoes spun off as Versant while Peacock, NBC, Bravo, studios and parks stayed; the 2023 cross-portfolio data org lost much of its network half three years later, with nothing public on how it was divided.97 documented Sky has cut about 3,000 positions since 2023, including 2,000 call-centre roles earlier in 2025, and in September 2025 announced 600 technology roles (London, Livingston, Leeds) while “consolidating some technology operations across its international footprint”; the platform is being rationalized as one org, not Sky-plus-Peacock parallel teams.30 documented

Where experimentation sits

A “DTC Decision Sciences” team in New York owns ML for recommendations, content predictive modeling and MarTech.110 documented A product analytics and experimentation team, also New York, delivers “high quality analysis and well-designed experiments” for “product, design, engineering and editorial” across hypothesis, sample size, design and analysis.74 documented Peacock experimentation is an analytics-side service function, not a platform-engineering function as at Disney; the tooling presumably lives in the Sky/NBCU platform org.74 inferred

Global sites

The UK (Osterley, Leeds) is the platform’s engineering core and its origin: the platform was built there first, so this is not a remote site that was handed a charter but the site the charter came from.5975 inferred Sky’s careers pages say Tech, Product and Data teams there “collaborate with Comcast and NBCUniversal” on “Peacock to NOW,” and sit “Across the EU. On both sides of the USA. And in India”.75 documented Peacock rolled into six European markets on Sky’s platforms in 2021.96 documented In India, the Comcast India Engineering Center in Chennai “support[s] Comcast, NBCUniversal and Sky businesses,” with recent work on frontline AI, syndication, the Xfinity app and cybersecurity; documented its founding year and headcount are.76 folklore There is no evidence of a Peacock- or NBCU-specific data or engineering site in Bangalore or Hyderabad; CIEC is Comcast-wide and cable-centric.76 inferred

What is attributable

Peacock reached scale (the largest US live-streamed event, the 2024 NFL wildcard game; 300 live events a day at the 2024 Olympics) on a platform built in twelve months by a team that had already built NOW TV, and that platform was reused for SkyShowtime and Showmax: the strongest “platform ownership at a non-HQ site works” datapoint in legacy media.5972 documented Peacock’s peak losses (~$2.8B in 2023) are a rights story, not an org story.72 folklore

2.9Paramount

Why it matters here

judgment Paramount is the negative control: leadership merged in 2023, pipelines stayed separate until a new owner forced it in 2026. Org consolidation without platform consolidation bought nothing measurable. If the India site owns shared pipelines the US org depends on daily, that dependency is what makes the site sticky when the next reorg comes.

Structure

Paramount+ launched on 4 March 2021 out of CBS All Access, after the December 2019 CBS–Viacom merger; Paramount Streaming is run by Tom Ryan, co-founder of Pluto TV, which Viacom bought in 2019 and whose engineering is in West Hollywood, Toronto and Berlin.7798111 documented

How it changed and why

The defining fact is a non-decision. Rather than build on Pluto TV, Paramount “opted instead to simply relaunch CBS All Access under the Paramount Plus name,” and the two stacks stayed separate.112 documented By 2026 the three services “run on two different clouds with no connectivity between them”.44 documented In October 2023 Pluto and Paramount+ product and tech leadership combined under Ryan, an org merger without a platform merger.78 inferred After Skydance closed in August 2025, Pluto TV and BET+ began migrating onto the Paramount+ stack, targeted for summer 2026 and the explicit blueprint for absorbing HBO Max once the WBD deal closes.4454112 documented

Where experimentation sits

No public record for streaming product data or experimentation. That data pipelines and recommenders were still separate per service in 2026 implies data teams were embedded per service through 2025 and are only now being centralized under the content and monetization groupings.44 inferred

Global sites

Essentially empty. Paramount’s India presence was Viacom18, a JV now Reliance’s, plus content and sales offices; there is no Paramount streaming or data engineering centre in India on the public record.77 inferred Pluto’s Berlin and Toronto offices are the only documented non-US streaming engineering sites, and SkyShowtime runs on the Sky/NBCU platform, not Paramount’s.7277 documented

What is attributable

The attributable outcome is negative: seven years of parallel stacks meant no cross-service personalization, unified ad targeting or shared data pipelines until 2026, and the 2023 leadership merger produced no data or tech integration until a new owner forced it.4478 documented

2.10Remote-site charter map

Every documented remote site in the eight cases, sorted by how it came to hold its charter. The left column is the five sites a parent chartered as new hubs (Netflix Warsaw, Spotify London, Amazon Hyderabad, Prime Video Bengaluru, WBD India); each got a capability. The right column is the four sites usually cited as counter-examples, and they are different cases: Google Zurich grew into product ownership over eighteen yearsdocumented13, Sky Osterley is the origin site of the platform it ownsinferred59, Spotify Boston kept the charter it was acquired with, and Hotstar was a home-market product that is no longer the parent’s.114 documented No case in this set chartered a remote site on a data domain as its founding mandate.12 inferred

2.11What nobody publishes

No company in either set has published outcome data tying structure to results. Every streaming P&L turn in 2023–2025 is better explained by pricing, password-sharing enforcement and content cuts than by any org chart, and the streaming-native companies’ claims that testing drives “every corner of the business” do not separate structure from culture.50105 inferred YouTube’s data org is undocumented; no legacy-media company has published how its experimentation function reports; and India-site charters exist only as careers-page copy.1220 inferred What is attributable is narrower: the coordination failures structure created and companies later wrote about fixing.75658 documented
