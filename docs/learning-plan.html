judgment This ranking is for this decision: a ~140-person data org at a large streaming/media company that has chartered its India site on whole platform components (telemetry ingestion and the experimentation engine, then data-quality tooling) plus one domain (quality of experience), with a gated month-18 decision between fraud, ads measurement or ML-platform expansion to follow (Recommendation §1.1–1.3), and now has to execute the phases. It is not a general management syllabus. Books that are excellent at the wrong altitude (first-line management, IC career) rank low or are dropped. Every book, blog, podcast, and episode below was checked on 2026-08-29 against a publisher page, author site, podcast feed, or episode page; anything that could not be confirmed is in the final section, not silently included.

The months below are the Recommendation’s happy path: Gate 0 at month 3 (no later than 6), Gate 1 at 9, Gate 2 at 18, steady state at 30. Every later date is an offset from Gate 0, not a calendar date; if Gate 0 slips to month 6, everything after it slips three months. judgment

6.1Ten books, ranked

Order matters

Team Topologies before An Elegant Puzzle, so the sizing heuristics land inside a structural frame; Galbraith before the charter; The Culture Map before the first India interview loop.41154

  1. Team Topologies, 2nd edition

    Matthew Skelton & Manuel Pais · IT Revolution, 2025

    The only book on the list with a vocabulary precise enough to settle the India question. Whole components, a whole domain, or contribution to US-owned work translate directly into platform, stream-aligned, or a collaboration mode that never ends, and the interaction modes (collaboration, X-as-a-service, facilitating) are the levers of each phase. Collaboration mode across a twelve-hour gap is time-boxed by definition; the Phase 1 gate is the point at which a team’s interface becomes X-as-a-service. Buy the September 2025 second edition, which adds cross-industry cases, not the 2019 first. Read the Conway’s Law chapter alongside Fowler’s bliki entry.41120

    Read: month 1 — before any org chart is drawn

  2. An Elegant Puzzle: Systems of Engineering Management

    Will Larson · Stripe Press, 2019

    The sizing-teams and path-to-high-performing-teams chapters are the most practical text available on how many people a team and a manager should carry, when to split, and why shuffling people between teams during a reorg destroys more than it creates. Larson’s rule that you fix one team at a time rather than spreading thin is the single most useful check against a big-bang India transition. The short, blunt organizational-design section on running a reorg should be reread in the month before Gate 1→2. Skip the career-ladder appendices for this purpose.128

    Read: month 1 — alongside Team Topologies

  3. The Culture Map

    Erin Meyer · PublicAffairs, 2014

    Most US–India engineering failures are scaling failures on Meyer’s eight dimensions, not skill failures: communicating (low- vs high-context), evaluating (direct vs indirect negative feedback), leading (egalitarian vs hierarchical), deciding (consensual vs top-down). This bears on structure: an extension model where India teams take tickets from US leads bakes in the hierarchical, indirect pattern, which is one more reason the site never starts as one. Use it to write interview questions for a site lead who can bridge both maps, and to set explicit feedback and decision norms in the charter. Overrated part: the French-versus-Dutch meeting anecdotes; skim those.154

    Read: month 2 — before the site-head interview loop and the conversion offers

  4. The Engineering Executive’s Primer

    Will Larson · O’Reilly, 2024

    Where An Elegant Puzzle is about teams, the Primer is about the executive’s own operating system: writing an engineering strategy, running planning, measuring an engineering org, and managing the CEO and CFO who will ask why a site that costs more than the vendor did is not yet faster than the vendor was. “Writing an engineering strategy” gives you the structure for the charter document; “Measuring engineering organizations” gives you the structure for the gate dashboard. Drafts of most chapters remain on lethain.com, so preview before buying.155156

    Read: months 1–2 — before the charter is announced

  5. Designing Organizations, 3rd edition

    Jay R. Galbraith · Jossey-Bass/Wiley, 2014

    The one book your business peers will recognize. The Star Model (strategy, structure, processes, rewards, people) is the frame a CFO, CHRO, or COO expects, and its most useful lesson for a data org is that structure is the least powerful lever: lateral processes (how domains and platforms coordinate) and reward systems (are India teams measured on tickets closed or outcomes owned?) decide whether a structure works. Use it to write the two or three charter pages the non-technical executives will read. It is dry and dated in its examples; read the Star Model, lateral-process, and matrix chapters and skip the rest.157

    Read: month 2 — before the charter is announced and the rewards terms are set

  6. Scaling People: Tactics for Management and Company Building

    Claire Hughes Johnson · Stripe Press, 2023

    The operational manual for the people side of the transition: hiring and onboarding at scale, writing operating principles, running performance and compensation cycles, and building the operating cadence a new site needs so it is not dependent on a US manager’s calendar. Her material on foundational documents (mission, principles, cadence) is what a site charter needs so that autonomy is designed, not hoped for. Hughes Johnson ran Stripe’s international expansion, so the advice translates. It is long; the hiring, onboarding, and operating-cadence sections are the parts that matter here. Skip the rest on a first pass.158

    Read: months 3–4 — as the site head starts and the converted cohort lands

  7. Data Mesh: Delivering Data-Driven Value at Scale

    Zhamak Dehghani · O’Reilly, 2022

    Read with skepticism. You do not need to adopt data mesh to use its vocabulary: domain ownership, data as a product, self-serve platform, and federated computational governance map cleanly onto stream-aligned, platform, and enabling teams. Its real contribution to the India decision is the argument that domain ownership only works when the platform makes ownership cheap, which is the case for the components-first, quality-of-experience-second order the Recommendation uses. Overrated part: the governance chapters are aspirational and the implementation guidance is thin. Essential: Part II (principles) and the chapter on organizing teams. Do not let anyone use this book to justify full decentralization of a 140-person org in year one.136

    Read: month 3 — before the Phase 1 transfer

  8. The Art of Action

    Stephen Bungay · Nicholas Brealey, 2011; 10th-anniversary edition 2022

    Mission command (Auftragstaktik) is the best available model for what an owning India team should look like: leaders state intent and constraints, teams decide how. The three gaps (knowledge, alignment, effects) explain most remote-site dysfunction better than time-zone arguments do. Use it to write the briefing for the Phase 2 handover, the first whole domain: what outcome, what boundaries, what you will and will not ask them to escalate. (The Phase 1 briefing at month 3 is simpler: a schema, an SLA, a pager.) The military history is the reason people skip it; do not, it is the argument. This book is why each phase gate should be framed as widening the scope of intent given to the site, not as a change in reporting lines.159

    Read: months 5–6 — before the Phase 2 briefing is written and the dissolution is announced at month 6

  9. Accelerate: The Science of Lean Software and DevOps

    Nicole Forsgren, Jez Humble & Gene Kim · IT Revolution, 2018

    Overrated as a general management book (it is a research summary), and its four metrics (lead time, deployment frequency, change failure rate, time to restore) do not transfer to data work; the gates use contract breaches, P1 incidents and backfill hours instead (Recommendation §2). Essential for one purpose: the structural finding that loosely coupled teams outperform wherever they sit, which is the part of Accelerate that does transfer. A phase gate built on site-independent measures plus a data-quality or SLA measure is harder to game and harder for a US team to block with “they’re not ready yet.” Also read the organizational-culture chapter; the Westrum generative-versus-bureaucratic distinction is a fair description of the difference between an owning site and a ticket-taking one. Skip the statistical-methodology appendix.131

    Read: months 6–7 — before the first Phase 1 gate quarter is reviewed

  10. Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments

    Ron Kohavi, Diane Tang & Ya Xu · Cambridge University Press, 2020

    Read before the experimentation engine transfers at month 3. Kohavi’s case is that trustworthy experimentation is a platform discipline (metrics governance, guardrails, institutional memory), not a set of analysts, which is why the engine (assignment, exposure logging, metrics computation) moves and the analysis, the catalog and the review gate stay central. Part I is written for executives and takes an evening; the rest is for whoever runs the platform.160

    Read: month 2 — before the experimentation engine transfers at month 3

Verified but not in the ten

  • High Output Management, Andrew S. Grove (Vintage, 1995): still the best primer on managerial leverage and task-relevant maturity, a useful lens on phase gates, but at first- and second-line altitude for this decision; optional, month 10.161
  • Team of Teams, Stanley McChrystal et al. (Portfolio, 2015): good on shared consciousness across a distributed organization, weak on how to structure it; Bungay covers the same ground with more rigor.162
  • Structure in Fives, Henry Mintzberg (Prentice Hall, 1983): verified and foundational, but Galbraith gives the same executive vocabulary with less 1980s baggage; overrated for practitioners.163
  • Fundamentals of Data Engineering, Joe Reis & Matt Housley (O’Reilly, 2022): verified and good, but not for you; hand it to the site head and every converted FTE at month 3, at the Phase 1 transfer, as a shared lifecycle vocabulary.164

6.2Blogs and newsletters

All twelve checked live on 2026-08-29. “Read first” is the specific entry point, not the home page. judgment

  1. Irrational Exuberance

    Will Larson · lethain.com · active August 2026

    Posts dated August 2026 (“Roadmap decisions rather than dates,” “Middle management roles are also a trap”). The most consistently useful writing on engineering strategy, org sizing, and executive operation. Read first: the “executive” tag, which holds draft chapters of the Primer, then the posts on sizing teams and running reorgs.156

  2. Team Topologies news

    Skelton & Pais · teamtopologies.com · active August 2026

    A vendor blog, so filter the consulting pitches. The value is the case studies and the ongoing refinement of team types and interaction modes. The 2026 agentic-AI-readiness posts are less relevant to this decision than the earlier material on platform teams and cognitive load. Read first: the case-study posts.165

  3. The Pragmatic Engineer

    Gergely Orosz · newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com · active, weekly

    1.1M+ subscribers; July 2026 AMA on engineering organizations. Best for how real companies structure this rather than theory; the deep dives on how specific companies run engineering justify the paid tier during a redesign year. Read first: search the archive for the platform-teams and reorg pieces.166

  4. Elided Branches

    Camille Fournier · elidedbranches.com · active May 2026

    May 2026 “Guidelines for Respectful Use of AI”; November 2025 “Revisiting Manager READMEs”; June 2025 “Dude, Where’s My Strategy?” The sharpest available writer on platform engineering as an organizational, not technical, problem. Read first: “Dude, Where’s My Strategy?”, then the platform-engineering posts.167

  5. Conway’s Law (bliki)

    Martin Fowler · martinfowler.com · updated October 2022

    A reference page, not a newsletter, and still the canonical short statement of the Inverse Conway Maneuver. It is the intellectual basis for the claim that extension teams produce extension-shaped architecture. Read first: the page itself, ten minutes, in month 1.120

  6. Data Products

    Chad Sanderson · dataproducts.substack.com · active July 2026

    July 2026 “The Shift Left Manifesto v2.” The most practical writing on data contracts and producer-side ownership, which is the mechanism by which a domain-owning team in India can be trusted by a consuming team in the US. Read first: “The Rise of Data Contracts,” then “The Shift Left Manifesto v2.”133

  7. benn.substack

    Benn Stancil · benn.substack.com · active, weekly (July 2026)

    Increasingly about AI and the industry rather than data-team design, which is why it ranks here and not higher. Still the best skeptic on data-org orthodoxy. Read first: “Disband the analytics team,” the best argument against the central analysis function this memo keeps; read it to know what the central function must earn.168

  8. Netflix Technology Blog

    Netflix · netflixtechblog.com · active August 2026

    August 2026 “A Tale of Two Flink Autoscalers”; May 2026 “Data Projects.” Peer relevance: how a streaming company draws the platform/domain line for data. Read first: “Data Projects: Managing Data Assets at Netflix Scale” for how a platform team defines ownership units.169

  9. The Analytics Engineering Roundup

    Tristan Handy, dbt Labs · roundup.getdbt.com · active August 2026

    Weekly curated links plus editorial; also hosts the podcast. The roundups on “data teams in the AI era” are the relevant thread. Read first: the podcast feed (below) rather than the roundups.170

  10. charity.wtf

    Charity Majors · charity.wtf (Substack) · active 2026

    Most relevant for her writing on the engineer/manager pendulum and on why management is not a promotion, which matters when you convert senior contractors and must decide who becomes a lead. Read first: “The Engineer/Manager Pendulum” (2017, still cited weekly).171

  11. Spotify Engineering

    Spotify · engineering.atspotify.com · active August 2026

    August 2026 “When Can LLMs Replace Humans in A/B Tests?”; July 2026 data-lake indexing post. Relevant for experimentation-platform and data-lake ownership patterns in a media company. The “Spotify model” org posts are old and overrated; ignore them. Read first: the 2026 experimentation and data-lake posts.172

  12. LeadDev

    LeadDev · leaddev.com · active 2025–2026

    Broad, sometimes shallow, but reliable for practitioner accounts of reorgs and managing managers (“How to redesign a broken delivery flow,” “Engineering leadership in 2025”). Read first: the reporting section’s 2025 engineering-leadership pieces.173

Flagged, not ranked

Locally Optimistic is still up, but the most recent post is August 2025. Its archive on data-team structure is good; treat it as a reference, not a subscription.174 Kohavi now writes mainly on LinkedIn, which is not verifiable as a stable URL; the book’s companion site, experimentguide.com, is live and lists his quarterly classes, and is the only Kohavi web source cited here.175

If time is short

The non-negotiables are the months 1–2 books (Team Topologies, An Elegant Puzzle, the Primer, Galbraith) plus The Culture Map in month 2 and Kohavi’s Part I before the experimentation engine moves. Everything else on this page is supporting material. If you read only one thing before the charter is announced, read Team Topologies; if you read only one thing before the site-head interview loop, read The Culture Map.
