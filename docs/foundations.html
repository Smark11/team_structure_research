3.1What the canon can and cannot do

Nine bodies of work, 1933 to 2026, reduce to four variables. Task interdependence decides the coordinating mechanism; the mechanism decides what distance can tolerate; ownership clarity decides quality; and structure is the weakest of the levers you hold.4373946

It contains almost no controlled comparisons. The strongest test of Conway’s Law is a natural experiment on software products, never replicated on data pipelines.113 The data-org literature is practitioner writing, unreplicated and silent on geography; the best of it admits its model “works for 10 people…could probably work for 20. Beyond that, we don’t know.”114115 Nothing in it addresses domain-versus-capability ownership for a data team at a remote site; that answer is assembled from adjacent findings, and the disagreements at the end of this page are where it comes from.616

3.2Galbraith: the Star Model and the information-processing view

So what for the India site

judgment The reporting line of the India site matters less than whether the planning cycle, the promotion criteria and the on-call rewards treat India as a peer. If those three still assume a US centre, the box on the chart is decoration. Galbraith’s reconfigurable organization is a stable structure plus deliberately designed lateral processes;116 most second-site failures are lateral-process failures (planning, code review, escalation), not box failures.

The Star Model (strategy, structure, processes, rewards, people) is the most-copied org-design framework in existence; McKinsey 7-S and Nadler-Tushman are its descendants.39 Its real content is two claims underneath the five points.

First, organization design is an information-processing problem. Task uncertainty sets how much information must be processed during execution, and when the hierarchy saturates there are four moves: create slack resources, create self-contained tasks (which reduce the need to process information), or invest in vertical information systems or lateral relations (which increase capacity to process it).37 documented Every later vocabulary maps onto those four moves. A domain-owned India site is a self-contained-task strategy. A platform or capability site is a vertical-information-system strategy. A graduating extension is a lateral-relations strategy that hopes to become self-contained tasks over time.37

Second, the alignment claim: the five points must be mutually consistent, and structure is the least important of them to get right first, because processes, rewards and people can compensate for a mediocre structure but not the reverse. Galbraith is explicit that leaders over-focus on the org chart.39 documented

3.3Mintzberg: which configuration a data org is

So what for the India site

judgment Pick the coordinating mechanism before you pick the box. If the India site’s work will be coordinated by mutual adjustment, distance is fatal. If it can be coordinated by standardized outputs or standardized skills, distance is survivable. Then hold HQ to output control, because process intervention is the documented way the divisionalized form dies.

Mintzberg’s 1980 synthesis proposes five configurations (simple structure, machine bureaucracy, professional bureaucracy, divisionalized form, adhocracy), each defined by a dominant coordinating mechanism: direct supervision, standardization of work processes, standardization of skills, standardization of outputs, or mutual adjustment.19 documented The 1981 HBR version’s thesis is that effectiveness comes from internal consistency of the configuration, not from adopting whatever is fashionable.117

judgment A 140-person data org is two configurations glued together, and that is most of its structural pain. The core platform (ingest, warehouse, orchestration, lineage, quality tooling) is a machine bureaucracy trending professional: standardized work and outputs, coordinated by platform architects and governance. The domain teams (commerce, experimentation, search, QoE, funnels) are a professional bureaucracy on good days, coordinated by the skills you hire for, and an adhocracy on bad days, coordinated by mutual adjustment because the question changes every quarter. This is inferred from Mintzberg’s definitions, not a claim he makes.19

His warnings apply directly. Professional bureaucracies resist central control; adhocracies are the most expensive to coordinate and drift toward bureaucracy under pressure. The divisionalized form, the only configuration coordinated by standardized outputs, is the closest analogue to “give India full domain ownership and measure it on outputs”, and he is blunt that it works only when divisions are genuinely separable and headquarters restrains itself to output control; when HQ intervenes in process, it collapses into a machine bureaucracy with extra layers.19 documented

3.4Conway’s Law, the Inverse Conway Maneuver, and the evidence

So what for the India site

judgment Read 2008 and 2009 together: distance did not hurt Vista; diffuse ownership did. An extension phase in which the US owns and India contributes is diffuse ownership by design, and the Vista data says to expect defects from it. Microsoft’s mitigations are the checklist: one toolchain, one process, long-tenured site leads, site-level ownership.

…organizations which design systems (in the broad sense used here) are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations.Melvin Conway, 1968118

Two things get forgotten about the 1968 paper. The mechanism is communication paths, not reporting lines: a system boundary appears wherever two groups cannot talk cheaply. And because the first design is never the best, “flexibility of organization is important to effective design.” Conway describes a constraint; he does not prescribe a strategy.118 documented The prescriptive flip, the Inverse Conway Maneuver, is Thoughtworks’ (Leroy and Simons, 2010; Tech Radar “Trial” in July 2014): evolve the team structure to promote the architecture you want.119120

The evidence, in descending order of strength, is the best empirical work on this page.113

  • MacCormack, Baldwin and Rusnak (2012) test the “mirroring hypothesis” on matched open-source and commercial products: loosely coupled organizations produce significantly more modular products. The strongest test of Conway in the literature; it shows organization → architecture, in software, and nobody has replicated it on data pipelines.113 documented
  • Nagappan, Murphy and Basili (2008), Windows Vista: eight organizational metrics (engineers and ex-engineers touching a binary, depth of master ownership, organizational intersection) predict failure-proneness at roughly 86% precision and 84% recall, beating churn, complexity, coverage and dependencies. Diffuse ownership predicts defects.4 documented
  • Bird, Nagappan, Devanbu, Gall and Murphy (2009), same data: components built by distributed teams showed a negligible difference in post-release failures versus collocated ones, smaller still after controlling for developer count. The mitigations were consistent tooling, shared process, long-tenured distributed leads and site-level ownership.16 documented

Durable reading: Conway is well supported as “ownership boundaries show up in the artifact” and “diffuse ownership costs quality”, not as “draw the org chart and the architecture follows”. Herbsleb and Grinter (1999) and Cataldo, Herbsleb and Carley (2008) set the limit: modular design is “necessary but not sufficient” for distributed work, because unanticipated events need communication the architecture cannot replace, and resolution time falls significantly when actual coordination matches the dependencies. Architecture reduces coordination need but never to zero.121122 documented

3.5Team Topologies

So what for the India site

judgment A written Team API per India team (what it owns, hours, how to engage, what it depends on) is the cheapest artifact on this page and the one that turns a transfer from a sentiment into a date. Collaboration mode across a twelve-hour gap should be time-boxed by default, never the steady state.

Skelton and Pais define four team types (stream-aligned, enabling, complicated-subsystem, platform) and three interaction modes (collaboration, X-as-a-Service, facilitation).4243 documented The principles: the team is the unit of delivery; team cognitive load is the thing to manage; Conway’s Law is used deliberately; platforms exist to reduce stream-aligned teams’ extraneous cognitive load, starting from the thinnest viable platform, “just enough capability to accelerate delivery”, possibly a wiki page.32

What they say about geography is thin and mostly in the 2022 Remote Team Interactions Workbook. Remote work “highlighted existing poor team interactions”; the fix is explicit Team APIs (what a team owns, how to engage it, working hours, channels), deliberate design of inter-team communication, and tracking and removal of team-level dependencies.47 documented

“The physical office was covering up poorly defined teams and poorly defined areas of focus.”47

The book favors co-located or same-timezone teams and treats the collaboration mode as expensive and therefore time-boxed. 42 inferred Its weakness for a data org is that it assumes stream-aligned teams can own an end-to-end value stream (a domain’s models, pipelines, metrics and decisions, which most data orgs cannot staff per domain) and says nothing about analytics work whose “flow” is a question, not a feature.114123 Treat it as a vocabulary and a set of constraints, not a blueprint.

3.6Span of control: “7” is a 1933 arithmetic exercise

The number “7” is treated as science and is not. Graicunas (1933) counted the relationships a manager must attend to, a number that explodes combinatorially (5 subordinates → 100, 6 → 222), and recommended four to five at senior levels: arithmetic, not evidence.124 documented Urwick, who had helped write that paper, popularized it in HBR in 1956 with a qualifier everyone drops.124125

“No supervisor can supervise directly the work of more than five or, at the most, six subordinates whose work interlocks.” Urwick, 1956, quoted in secondary sources.125

Miller’s “magical number seven” is about short-term memory, not management, so in span advice it is;126 folklore Google’s span norms are folklore too, since Project Oxygen studied manager behaviors, not span.127

What replaced the single number is a table. McKinsey’s five archetypes run player/coach 3–5, coach 6–7, supervisor 8–10, facilitator 11–15, coordinator 15+, set by how standardized the work is and how much the manager contributes individually: Mintzberg’s mechanisms as headcount.50 Larson’s engineering norm: people managers six to eight, “never more than eight”;49 hands-on technical managers three to five.128

judgment Span is a function of task interlock, standardization and manager type, not a constant: platform teams doing standardized work tolerate seven to eight; domain teams doing interlocked, uncertain work want five to seven; nothing on either site should run at ten. And “whose work interlocks” is the remote test: six India directs whose work interlocks with US teams put a US manager at the ceiling already; hence a site-local manager.

3.7Larson: sizing, four states, reorgs

So what for the India site

judgment Do not transfer anything smaller than six people. Concentrate the first India headcount on whole teams rather than partial ones, and apply the reorg tests to every proposed re-cut of the US org: if the answer to “are you routing around a person” is yes, stop.

Larson’s rules, from the 2018 sizing post and An Elegant Puzzle: teams are six to eight at steady state, and to create a team you grow one to eight or ten and bud it into two of four or five (the post);49 teams smaller than four are “transitory” or “exploratory”, and advocates of smaller teams are “cavalier in ignoring the downsides” (the book).128 documented Practitioner norms, not measurements, and the most widely adopted ones in the industry.

Every team is in one of four states (falling behind, treading water, repaying debt, innovating) and the fix is state-specific: add people, reduce WIP, add time, or keep the slack rather than reassigning it. Shift resources to the most-behind team; do not spread thin.129 documented

His reorg tests: is the problem structural (communication, decision friction, attention)? Are you reorganizing to work around a broken relationship (the worst reason; “karma always comes due”)? Does the problem already exist, or are you pre-solving? Are the conditions temporary?130 documented

The tension with the previous section is arithmetic. Larson’s team-size floor of six and his span ceiling of eight are the same number seen from two sides. A site with twelve people cannot honor both and also have two domains; if you cannot put six to eight people on something, Larson says you do not have a team, you have a project.49

3.8Accelerate and DORA, and the limits for data

So what for the India site

judgment The question is not “can India deploy” but “how many US teams must India talk to per change”. Count that number for each candidate charter before comparing anything else.

Accelerate reports survey research from the State of DevOps program: four delivery metrics (lead time, deployment frequency, MTTR, change-fail rate) predict organizational performance, and among the capabilities that predict the metrics is loosely coupled architecture: teams can make large design changes without permission, complete work without fine-grained coordination, deploy independently, and test without an integrated environment.131132 documented The memorable claim is that architecture mattered more than technology: high performance was possible on mainframes if teams and systems were loosely coupled.

judgment The limits for data are real. The four metrics assume the unit of change is a service deploy; a model change, a metric redefinition or a backfill has a different blast radius and reversibility. “Deploy independently” is aspirational when every domain reads every other domain’s tables: the coupling is in the data, not the code. Nobody has validated the metrics on work whose output is a decision. What transfers is the structural finding, that teams needing little cross-team coordination outperform, which is Galbraith 1974 and Thompson 1967 with a survey behind it.

3.9The data-org literature

So what for the India site

judgment Offense/defense is the one theory here. Defense work (fraud, commerce data quality, subscriber-state modeling) is coordinated by standardized outputs and is the plausible full-ownership candidate; offense work (experimentation analysis, browse, funnels) is adhocratic and belongs where the stakeholders sit. That is the canon’s vote. It loses on legal exposure and decision latency, not on standardization: fraud’s decisions are denominated in dollars and signed in the US, and its data may not be transferable as scoped. Applying It scores it: quality of experience goes first on the assumed baseline, with a month-3 re-decision — if counsel clears fraud and the contractors sit in commerce, fraud goes first instead. Otherwise fraud, ads measurement or ML-platform expansion is the month-18 decision, and the legal review decides which is even eligible. And enforce contracts in CI from day one, or the producer-ownership principle is a slide.

Practitioner writing, unreplicated, recycling one three-way choice: centralized, embedded, hybrid. The closest thing to a theory is DalleMule and Davenport’s offense–defense spectrum: defense (quality, security, compliance, a single source of truth) wants central control, offense (revenue, analytics, speed) wants flexibility, and the balance dictates structure.20 documented In a media company, commerce and fraud data are defense-heavy and experimentation and browse offense-heavy, so they should not be organized identically.20

The one mechanism is Sanderson’s data contracts: ownership attaches to the producer once an asset is shown to have value, and contracts define the failure state and must be enforced in CI/CD or they are decorative:133 Nagappan 2008 turned into a practice.4 The rest of the canon converges on one shape: a strong central team owning models and metrics, with embedded people who still report to the data org.114115134

Data mesh is a Team Topologies diagram applied to analytical data: domain ownership, data as a product, self-serve platform, federated computational governance.135136 documented The one field report is the 2026 retrospective from Thoughtworks, which coined the term: the scarce thing is people, not technology; domains lack data-competent staff and will not fund them; “data domains” get created by rebranding IT teams without decision rights; platforms get over-built before value is shown; governance stalls on stakeholder alignment.123 Netflix, the canonical strong central platform with distributed analytics engineering, is hub-and-spoke at an order of magnitude larger than this org. 137 inferred

3.10Distance: the empirical record on second sites

So what for the India site

judgment Follow-the-sun for data engineering is a trap unless the work is operational: on-call, backfills, incidents. Build common ground before separating work (shared toolchain, shared process, long-tenured leads on both sides), and budget the 2.5x for every ticket that crosses the ocean while a component is in transfer.

Where the canon is strongest and most ignored. Herbsleb and Mockus, on Lucent change data plus surveys, found cross-site work items took about 2.5x as long as comparable single-site items, not because individuals were slower but because more people were involved, informal communication was scarcer and finding the right person took longer; a 2025 retrospective by the same authors in the same journal reflects on the paper’s influence and notes that changes in business and technical environments have altered how the challenges of distributed work manifest — the delay mechanism is the durable part, not the multiplier.6 documented The name “Herbsleb’s law” for this result is; folklore the finding is not.6

Olson and Olson’s “Distance Matters” says remote collaboration works only with high common ground, loosely coupled work, collaboration readiness and technology readiness, and that technology does not substitute for the first two.36 documented Carmel and Agarwal decompose distance into geographic, temporal and cultural, and the tactics that work are to reduce intensive collaboration across sites (each site owns separable work), reduce cultural distance (liaisons, bridgeheads) and reduce temporal distance (overlap windows, asynchronous methods).18 inferred That is the direct case for site-level ownership of separable work.

Carmel, Espinosa and Dubinsky on follow-the-sun: duration falls only when handoff efficiency is high and the reduced within-site coordination outweighs the added cross-site coordination, which is rare for design-heavy work.138 documented Hinds and Bailey find distributed teams have more task, affective and process conflict, driven by reduced shared context, unequal information and technology mediation; the countermeasures are shared identity and shared context, not tools.139 documented

Bird et al. 2009 is the counterweight, and the reconciliation is Olson and Olson’s own: Microsoft had high common ground (one process, one toolchain, long-tenured leads, site-level ownership) and reduced coupling. Distance hurts coupled, low-common-ground work and is survivable for decoupled, high-common-ground work.1636

3.11Where the frameworks disagree

Team Topologies’ stream-aligned bias vs. staffing reality

Skelton and Pais say most teams should be stream-aligned and the platform should be thinnest viable; Dehghani says the same for data.32135 The Thoughtworks retrospective says domains lack data-competent staff and will not fund them, and that “domains” get created by re-badging IT teams; the practitioner literature says most orgs cannot staff data-competent domain teams114123 — which is how a thick central platform ends up doing the domain work. judgment The disagreement is not in principle but in arithmetic: Larson’s six-person floor times eight domains is 48 people of domain data engineering before a platform exists.49

Judgment

At 140 total that is arithmetically possible and financially unlikely. The resolution used on this site is hub-and-spoke: a central reporting line and hiring bar, people aligned to verticals, and no pretense that eight domain teams exist. Mesh vocabulary is used for contracts and producer ownership, not for the org chart.

Conway’s Law vs. deliberately mirrored design

Conway says the org constrains the architecture; the Inverse Conway Maneuver says design the org to get the architecture.118119 Herbsleb, Grinter and Cataldo show the mirror is necessary but never sufficient: congruence significantly shortens resolution time; it does not remove the residual coordination.121122 The second half of the disagreement is specific to data: mesh mirrors the org onto the data, but warehouse data is reciprocally interdependent in a way service code is not, because every domain reads every other domain’s facts.46

Judgment

“Give India a clean module and the coordination problem goes away” is partly true. Conway on data produces silos faster than it produces modularity. This site uses Inverse Conway for ownership boundaries only, budgets the residual coordination explicitly, and does not partition the warehouse by site.

Mintzberg’s adhocracy vs. Galbraith’s formal lateral process

Mintzberg says innovative work is coordinated by mutual adjustment and resists formalization; Galbraith says design the lateral processes explicitly.19116 For a remote site these are opposed: mutual adjustment is what distance destroys, so a site doing adhocratic work needs Galbraith’s formalized lateral processes, which Mintzberg predicts will slow the work.36

Judgment

The practical answer on this site: route adhocratic work (experimentation analysis, browse, funnel questions) to whichever site has the stakeholders, and formalize only the interfaces between sites, never the work inside a team.

Span-of-control ceilings vs. Larson’s team-size floor

Spans of five to eight and teams of six to eight are consistent only if every manager has exactly one team. A site with twelve people and two domains either honors the floor (two teams of six, fine), breaks the ceiling (one manager with twelve directs, not fine), or adds a layer (a site lead over two managers, a 1:4 manager ratio at twelve people).49130 The frameworks are silent; budget decides.

Judgment

This is the arithmetic that says the India site should start with fewer, whole teams rather than many partial ones, under a site-local manager whose span stays at the low end because the work interlocks with the US.

Judgment

Across the eight companies (Case Studies §2.1): what survived had a single accountable owner per separable unit of work; what failed had autonomy without ownership — Spotify’s “responsibility without accountability”, Amazon’s “dependencies, not size”.38140 Whatever India owns, the US org must be re-cut to not own it.

3.12Fifteen durable principles

judgment What survives the disagreements. Applying It cites these by number.

  1. Task interdependence dictates the coordination mechanism, and the mechanism dictates what distance can tolerate. Pooled → standardize; sequential → plan; reciprocal → mutual adjustment, which requires co-location or heroic effort.193646
  2. Self-contained tasks are the cheapest way to reduce information-processing load; lateral processes are the most expensive way to increase capacity. Prefer the former across sites.1837
  3. Ownership clarity predicts quality; diffuse ownership predicts defects. The best-replicated Conway-adjacent result, and it holds regardless of geography.416
  4. Distance per se does not degrade quality; coupling and low common ground do. Cross-site coupled work is about 2.5x slower, but decoupled work with shared process and tooling is not measurably worse.61636
  5. Architecture reduces coordination need but never eliminates it; budget for the residual. Congruence between required and actual coordination significantly shortens resolution time.121122
  6. Structure is the least important Star point to get right first; processes, rewards and people compensate for structure but not the reverse.39
  7. Internal consistency of the configuration beats fashion. Mixing divisional output-control with machine-bureaucracy process-control is the most common failure.19117
  8. Span of control is a function of task interlock and standardization, not a constant; “7” is folklore descended from a 1933 arithmetic exercise and a 1956 memory experiment. The literature’s ranges run from 3–5 for player-coaches to 15+ for fully standardized work; nothing on either site here should run above eight.4950124126
  9. A team is six to eight people; below four it is a project. Concentrate headcount on the most-behind team rather than spreading thin.49129
  10. Reorg only for structural problems that already exist and are not temporary; never to route around a person.130
  11. Platforms should start thin and be treated as products with internal customers; they exist to reduce the cognitive load of the teams they serve, not to accumulate the work domains will not fund.3242123
  12. Loosely coupled teams (able to change, test and deploy without cross-team permission) outperform, independent of technology stack. Transfer the structural finding, not the four metrics, to data work.131132
  13. Producers own data quality; contracts define the failure state and must be enforced mechanically.133135
  14. A single accountable owner per separable unit of work is what survived in every case study; autonomy without accountable ownership is what failed.3882140
  15. Remote work exposes poorly defined teams; the fix is explicit Team APIs, tracked dependencies and deliberately designed inter-team communication, not tooling.1847139
