What the canon can and cannot do

The case studies in section 2 show what companies did. This page is the mechanism underneath: why some of it worked. Nine bodies of work, 1933 to 2026, reduce to four variables that every credible source keeps returning to. Task interdependence decides the coordinating mechanism; the mechanism decides what distance can tolerate; ownership clarity decides quality; and structure is the weakest of the levers you actually hold.36472332

Be clear about what it is not. There are almost no controlled comparisons anywhere in it. The strongest test of Conway's Law is a natural experiment on software products, never replicated on data pipelines.121 The data-org literature is practitioner writing, unreplicated, and silent on geography; the best of it admits its own model "works for 10 people…could probably work for 20. Beyond that, we don't know."122123 Nothing in the canon addresses domain-versus-capability ownership for a data team at a remote site; that question has to be assembled from adjacent findings.1413

judgment Read each section as two to four claims that survive scrutiny, one marginal note on what it means for the India site, and nothing else. The disagreements at the end are the part that matters most; a framework that agrees with every other framework is not telling you anything.

Galbraith: the Star Model and the information-processing view

Galbraith's Star Model (strategy, structure, processes, rewards, people) is the most-copied org-design framework in existence; McKinsey 7-S and the Nadler-Tushman congruence model are its descendants.32 Its real content is not the five points. It is two claims underneath them.

First, organization design is an information-processing problem. Task uncertainty determines how much information must be processed during execution, and when the hierarchy saturates there are exactly four moves: create slack resources, create self-contained tasks (which reduce the need to process information), or invest in vertical information systems or lateral relations (which increase capacity to process it).47 documented Every later vocabulary maps onto those four moves. A domain-owned India site is a self-contained-task strategy. A platform or capability site is a vertical-information-system strategy. A graduating extension is a lateral-relations strategy that hopes to become self-contained tasks over time.47

Second, the alignment claim: the five points must be mutually consistent, and structure is the least important of them to get right first, because processes, rewards and people can compensate for a mediocre structure but not the reverse. Galbraith is explicit that leaders over-focus on the org chart.32 documented

The reconfigurable-organization work adds that a stable structure plus deliberately designed lateral processes (cross-unit teams, integrator roles, matrix) yields reconfigurability without perpetual reorgs.124 judgment Design the lateral links as carefully as the boxes. Most second-site failures are lateral-process failures (planning, code review, escalation), not box failures.

So what for the India site

judgment The reporting line of the India site matters less than whether the planning cycle, the promotion criteria and the on-call rewards treat India as a peer. If those three still assume a US centre, the box on the chart is decoration.

Mintzberg: which configuration a data org actually is

Mintzberg's 1980 synthesis proposes five configurations (simple structure, machine bureaucracy, professional bureaucracy, divisionalized form, adhocracy), each defined by a dominant coordinating mechanism: direct supervision, standardization of work processes, standardization of skills, standardization of outputs, or mutual adjustment.17 documented The 1981 HBR version's thesis is that effectiveness comes from internal consistency of the configuration, not from adopting whatever is fashionable.125

judgment A 140-person data org is not one configuration; it is two glued together, and that is the source of most of its structural pain. The core platform (ingest, warehouse, orchestration, lineage, quality tooling) behaves like a machine bureaucracy trending toward a professional one: standardized work, standardized outputs, coordinated by a technostructure of platform architects and governance. The domain teams (commerce, experimentation, search, QoE, funnels) are a professional bureaucracy on good days, coordinated by the standardized skills you hire for, and an adhocracy on bad days, coordinated by mutual adjustment because the question changes every quarter and nobody's playbook covers it. This is inferred from Mintzberg's own definitions, not a claim he makes.17

Mintzberg's warnings apply directly. Professional bureaucracies resist central control; adhocracies are the most expensive configuration to coordinate and drift toward bureaucracy under pressure. His divisionalized form, the only configuration coordinated by standardized outputs, is the closest analogue to "give India full domain ownership and measure it on outputs", and he is blunt that it works only when divisions are genuinely separable and headquarters restrains itself to output control; when HQ intervenes in process, it collapses into a machine bureaucracy with extra layers.17 documented

So what for the India site

judgment Pick the coordinating mechanism before you pick the box. If the India site's work will be coordinated by mutual adjustment, distance is fatal. If it can be coordinated by standardized outputs or standardized skills, distance is survivable. Then hold HQ to output control, because process intervention is the documented way the divisionalized form dies.

Conway's Law, the Inverse Conway Maneuver, and the evidence

Organizations "are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations."126

Two things get forgotten about the 1968 paper. Conway's mechanism is communication paths, not reporting lines: a system boundary appears wherever two groups cannot talk cheaply. And his corollary is that because the first design is never the best, "flexibility of organization is important to effective design." He describes a constraint; he does not prescribe a strategy.126 documented The prescriptive flip, the Inverse Conway Maneuver, is Thoughtworks' (Leroy and Simons, 2010; Tech Radar "Trial" in July 2014): evolve the team structure to promote the architecture you want.127

The evidence base, in descending order of strength, is worth knowing in detail because it is the best empirical work on this page.121

  • MacCormack, Baldwin and Rusnak (2012) test the "mirroring hypothesis" on matched open-source and commercial products with the same function: loosely coupled organizations produce significantly more modular products. The strongest test of Conway in the literature, and it shows organization → architecture, in software; nobody has replicated it on data pipelines.121 documented
  • Nagappan, Murphy and Basili (2008), Windows Vista: eight organizational-structure metrics (engineers and ex-engineers touching a binary, depth of master ownership, organizational intersection) predict failure-proneness at roughly 86% precision and 84% recall, beating churn, complexity, coverage and dependencies. The finding that matters here: diffuse ownership predicts defects.23 documented
  • Bird, Nagappan, Devanbu, Gall and Murphy (2009), same data: components built by geographically distributed teams showed a negligible difference in post-release failures versus collocated ones, smaller still after controlling for developer count. Microsoft's mitigations were consistent tooling, shared process, long-tenured distributed leads and site-level ownership.13 documented
  • Herbsleb and Grinter (1999), a Lucent case: modular design is "necessary but not sufficient" for distributed development, because unanticipated events require communication the architecture cannot substitute for.128 documented
  • Cataldo, Herbsleb and Carley (2008) formalize socio-technical congruence: when actual coordination matches the coordination required by technical dependencies, modification-request resolution time falls by roughly a third.129 documented

Durable reading: Conway is well supported as "ownership boundaries show up in the artifact" and "diffuse ownership costs quality". It is not supported as "draw the org chart and the architecture follows"; the Herbsleb work is explicit that architecture reduces coordination need but never to zero.128129

So what for the India site

judgment Read 2008 and 2009 together: distance did not hurt Vista; diffuse ownership did. An "extension" phase in which the US owns and India contributes is diffuse ownership by design, and it is the configuration the Vista data says to expect defects from. The mitigations Microsoft used are the checklist: one toolchain, one process, long-tenured site leads, site-level ownership.

Team Topologies

Skelton and Pais define four team types (stream-aligned, enabling, complicated-subsystem, platform) and three interaction modes (collaboration, X-as-a-Service, facilitation).34130 documented The organizing principles are that the team is the unit of delivery; that team cognitive load is the binding constraint and should be explicitly managed; that Conway's Law should be used deliberately; and that platforms exist to reduce stream-aligned teams' extraneous cognitive load, starting from the thinnest viable platform, "just enough capability to accelerate delivery", possibly a wiki page curating vendor services.28

What they say about geography is thin and mostly in the 2022 Remote Team Interactions Workbook. Remote work "highlighted existing poor team interactions"; the fix is explicit Team APIs (what a team owns, how to engage it, working hours, channels), deliberate design of inter-team communication, and tracking and removal of team-level dependencies.39 documented

"The physical office was covering up poorly defined teams and poorly defined areas of focus."39

The book favors co-located or same-timezone teams and treats the collaboration mode as expensive and therefore time-boxed; the exact pages were not fetched, so treat the timezone preference as inferred.34 Where the framework is weakest for a data org: it assumes stream-aligned teams can own an end-to-end value stream, which in data means a domain's models, pipelines, metrics and the product decisions they serve, a stack most data orgs cannot staff per domain; and it has nothing to say about analytics or data-science work whose "flow" is a question, not a feature.122131 Treat it as a vocabulary and a set of constraints, not a blueprint.

So what for the India site

judgment A written Team API per India team (what it owns, hours, how to engage, what it depends on) is the cheapest artifact on this page and the one that turns "graduation" from a sentiment into a date. Collaboration mode across 10.5 hours should be time-boxed by default, never the steady state.

Span of control: what the evidence actually is

The lineage matters because the number "7" is treated as science and is not. Graicunas (1933) proposed a formula for the relationships a manager must attend to (direct, cross, group), which explodes combinatorially (5 subordinates → 100 relationships, 6 → 222) and recommended four to five at senior levels. It is arithmetic, not evidence.132 documented Urwick popularized it in HBR in 1956 and disclosed he had helped Graicunas write the 1933 paper.133

"No supervisor can supervise directly the work of more than five or, at the most, six subordinates whose work interlocks."133

The qualifier "whose work interlocks" is the piece everyone drops. Miller's "magical number seven" (1956) is about short-term memory chunks and says nothing about management; its co-option into span advice is folklore.134 Google's Project Oxygen studies manager behaviors, not span, and is routinely cited for a number it never measured; Google's span norms are folklore in the public record.135

What replaced the single number is a table. McKinsey's five archetypes run player/coach 3–5, coach 6–7, supervisor 8–10, facilitator 11–15, coordinator 15+, driven by how standardized the work is and how much of the manager's own load is individual contribution; this is Mintzberg's coordinating mechanisms as a headcount table.43 The engineering norm is Larson's: hands-on technical managers three to five, people-focused managers five to eight depending on experience, never more than eight.41136

judgment Span is a function of task interlock (Urwick), standardization (McKinsey, Mintzberg) and manager type (Larson), not a constant. Platform teams doing standardized work tolerate 8–10; domain analytics-engineering teams doing interlocked, uncertain work want 5–7.

So what for the India site

judgment A remote site argues for the low end of every range, or for a site-local manager rather than a US manager with remote directs. "Whose work interlocks" is the test: a US manager with six India directs whose work interlocks with US teams is at the ceiling already.

Larson: sizing, four states, reorgs

Larson's rules, from the 2018 sizing post and An Elegant Puzzle: teams should be six to eight at steady state; to create a team, grow an existing one to eight or ten and bud it into two of four or five; teams smaller than four are "transitory" or "exploratory"; and people who advocate smaller teams are "cavalier in ignoring the downsides".41137 documented These are practitioner norms, not measurements, and they are the most widely adopted ones in the industry.

Every team is in one of four states (falling behind, treading water, repaying debt, innovating) and the fix is state-specific: add people when falling behind, reduce WIP when treading water, add time when repaying debt, and keep the slack when innovating rather than reassigning it. Crucially, shift resources to the most-behind team; do not spread thin.138 documented

His reorg tests: is the problem structural (communication, decision friction, attention)? Are you reorganizing to work around a broken relationship (the worst reason; "karma always comes due")? Does the problem already exist, or are you pre-solving? Are the conditions temporary? If a reorg passes, the sequence is validate necessity, project one-year headcount, set the manager-to-engineer ratio, identify logical teams and groups, staff, secure leadership commitment, then roll out with the reasoning, per-person impact documentation, and availability for the fallout.136 documented His "organizational debt" is narrower than Steve Blank's original (all the people and culture compromises made to ship); Larson means structural problems that never become the most pressing thing, with "organizational risk" as the volatile subset.139140

The tension with the previous section is arithmetic. Larson's team-size floor of six and his span ceiling of eight are the same number seen from two sides. A site with twelve people cannot honor both and also have two domains; if you cannot put six to eight people on something, Larson says you do not have a team, you have a project.41

So what for the India site

judgment Do not "graduate" anything smaller than six people. Concentrate the first India headcount on one whole team rather than two partial ones, and apply the reorg tests to every proposed re-cut of the US org: if the answer to "are you routing around a person" is yes, stop.

Accelerate and DORA, and the limits for data

Accelerate reports survey research from the State of DevOps program: four delivery metrics (lead time, deployment frequency, MTTR, change-fail rate) predict organizational performance, and among the capabilities that predict the metrics is loosely coupled architecture, meaning teams can make large-scale design changes without permission, complete work without fine-grained coordination with other teams, deploy and release independently, and test without an integrated environment.37141 documented The memorable claim is that architecture mattered more than technology: high performance was possible on mainframes if teams and systems were loosely coupled. The 2023 report adds that user-centric teams, faster code review and generative culture correlate with performance, and treats platform engineering as a first-class topic.142

judgment The limits for data are real. The four metrics assume the unit of change is a service deploy; a model change, a metric redefinition or a backfill has a radically different blast radius and reversibility. "Deploy independently" is aspirational when every domain reads every other domain's tables, because the coupling is in the data, not the code. And nobody has validated the metrics on work whose output is a decision. What transfers is the structural finding, that teams needing little cross-team coordination outperform, which is Galbraith 1974 and Thompson 1967 with a survey behind it.

So what for the India site

judgment The question is not "can India deploy" but "how many US teams must India talk to per change". Count that number for each candidate charter before comparing anything else.

The data-org literature

This literature is almost entirely practitioner, almost entirely unreplicated, and recycles the same three-way choice: centralized, embedded, hybrid (hub-and-spoke). The closest thing to a theory is DalleMule and Davenport: data strategy runs on an offense–defense spectrum (defense is quality, security, compliance, single source of truth; offense is revenue, analytics, speed), defense wants centralized control and offense wants flexibility, and the balance dictates structure.18 documented In a media company, commerce and fraud data are defense-heavy; experimentation and browse are offense-heavy, which predicts they should not be organized identically.18

The practitioner canon converges on one shape. Locally Optimistic's "One Size Fits None" says structure depends on ambient technical ability, distribution of analytical skill and engineering support, not a template; its "Data Business Partnership" says embedded practitioners should still report to the data org, HR-business-partner style, with a strong central team owning models and metrics.123143 dbt Labs reframes the question as "what, exactly, should be centralized, and what decentralized?" and reports a domain-based hybrid it can vouch for at 10, maybe 20 people; Handy's version is central platform, "data expertise everywhere".122144 Mode's "pulmonary model" rotates analysts between central and embedded seats to build domain context without losing methodological consistency.145 Stancil's "Disband the analytics team" argues analytics has over-promised: accept it as operational support or embed the people and stop maintaining a department; he has separately argued that data mesh is "delegated", not decentralized, because a central authority still sets standards.146 Sanderson on data contracts: ownership attaches to the producer once an asset is shown to have value, and contracts define the failure state and must be enforced in CI/CD or they are decorative.147 That last one is Nagappan 2008 turned into a mechanism.23

Data mesh is a Team Topologies diagram applied to analytical data: domain ownership, data as a product, self-serve platform, federated computational governance.148149 documented The 2026 retrospective from Thoughtworks, the firm that coined it, is consistent with every other critique: the binding constraint is people, not technology; domains lack data-competent staff and will not fund them; "data domains" get created by rebranding IT teams without decision rights; upskilling is under-budgeted; platforms get over-built before value is shown; governance stalls on stakeholder alignment.131 Kwan argues the reverse, that mesh fails because the infrastructure (versioning, contracts, consistency) was never built for decentralization, so reorgs relocate bottlenecks.150 Both are probably true. Netflix, the canonical strong-central-platform with distributed analytics engineering, is hub-and-spoke at roughly thirty times this org's scale; the post body could not be fetched, so this is inferred from the title.151

What the whole literature lacks: any controlled comparison, any replication, any treatment of geography. Domain-versus-capability at a remote site is unaddressed.123122

So what for the India site

judgment Offense/defense is the one theory here. Defense work (fraud, commerce data quality, subscriber-state modeling) is coordinated by standardized outputs and is the plausible full-ownership candidate; offense work (experimentation analysis, browse, funnels) is adhocratic and belongs where the stakeholders sit. And enforce contracts in CI from day one, or the producer-ownership principle is a slide.

Distance: the empirical record on second sites

This is where the canon is strongest and most ignored. Herbsleb and Mockus, on Lucent change data plus surveys, found cross-site work items took about 2.5x as long as comparable single-site items, not because individuals were slower but because more people were involved, informal communication was scarcer and finding the right person took longer; a 2025 retrospective in the same journal confirms the finding has held.14 documented The name "Herbsleb's law" for this result is folklore; the finding is not.14

Olson and Olson's "Distance Matters" says remote collaboration works only with high common ground, loosely coupled work, collaboration readiness and technology readiness, and that technology does not substitute for the first two.46 documented Carmel and Agarwal decompose distance into geographic, temporal and cultural, and the tactics that work are to reduce intensive collaboration across sites (each site owns separable work), reduce cultural distance (liaisons, bridgeheads) and reduce temporal distance (overlap windows, asynchronous methods).16 documented That is the direct case for site-level ownership of separable work.

Carmel, Espinosa and Dubinsky on follow-the-sun: duration falls only when handoff efficiency is high and the reduced within-site coordination outweighs the added cross-site coordination, which is rare for design-heavy work.152 documented Hinds and Bailey find distributed teams have more task, affective and process conflict, driven by reduced shared context, unequal information and technology mediation; the countermeasures are shared identity and shared context, not tools.153 documented

Bird et al. 2009 is the counterweight, and the reconciliation is Olson and Olson's own: Microsoft had high common ground (one process, one toolchain, long-tenured leads, site-level ownership) and reduced coupling. Distance hurts coupled, low-common-ground work and is survivable for decoupled, high-common-ground work.1346

So what for the India site

judgment Follow-the-sun for data engineering is a trap unless the work is operational: on-call, backfills, incidents. Build common ground before separating work (shared toolchain, shared process, long-tenured leads on both sides), and budget the 2.5x for every ticket that crosses the ocean during the extension phase.

Where the frameworks disagree

Team Topologies' stream-aligned bias vs. staffing reality

Skelton and Pais say most teams should be stream-aligned and the platform should be thinnest viable; Dehghani says the same for data.28148 The 2026 Thoughtworks retrospective and the practitioner literature say most orgs cannot staff data-competent domain teams and end up with a thick central platform doing the domain work anyway.131122 The disagreement is not in principle but in arithmetic: Larson's six-person floor times eight domains is 48 people of domain data engineering before a platform exists.41

Judgment

At 140 total that is arithmetically possible and financially unlikely. The resolution used on this site is hub-and-spoke: a central reporting line and hiring bar, people aligned to verticals, and no pretense that eight domain teams exist. Mesh vocabulary is used for contracts and producer ownership, not for the org chart.

Conway's Law vs. deliberately mirrored design

Conway says the org constrains the architecture; the Inverse Conway Maneuver says design the org to get the architecture.126127 Herbsleb and Grinter and Cataldo show the mirror is necessary but never sufficient: coordination need persists beyond any architecture, and congruence is worth roughly a third on resolution time, not everything.128129 The second half of the disagreement is specific to data: mesh mirrors the org onto the data, but warehouse data is reciprocally interdependent in a way service code is not, because every domain reads every other domain's facts.36

Judgment

"Give India a clean module and the coordination problem goes away" is one-third true. Conway on data produces silos faster than it produces modularity. This site uses Inverse Conway for ownership boundaries only, budgets the residual coordination explicitly, and does not partition the warehouse by site.

Mintzberg's adhocracy vs. Galbraith's formal lateral process

Mintzberg says innovative work is coordinated by mutual adjustment and resists formalization; Galbraith says design the lateral processes explicitly.17124 For a remote site these are opposed: mutual adjustment is what distance destroys, so a site doing adhocratic work needs Galbraith's formalized lateral processes, which Mintzberg predicts will slow the work.46

Judgment

There is no clean resolution. The practical answer on this site: route adhocratic work (experimentation analysis, browse, funnel questions) to whichever site has the stakeholders, and formalize only the interfaces between sites, never the work inside a team.

Span-of-control ceilings vs. Larson's team-size floor

Spans of five to eight and teams of six to eight are consistent only if every manager has exactly one team. The moment a site has twelve people and two domains you either violate the floor (two teams of six, fine), the ceiling (one manager with twelve directs, not fine), or add a layer (a site lead over two managers, a 1:4 manager ratio at twelve people).41136 The frameworks are silent; budget decides.

Judgment

This is the arithmetic that says the India site should start with fewer, whole teams rather than many partial ones, under a site-local manager whose span stays at the low end because the work interlocks with the US.

Case studies vs. frameworks

Spotify's 2012 whitepaper was a snapshot of about thirty squads that Kniberg said was not a framework; Lee reports it was aspirational, never fully implemented, and dismantled in favor of conventional management as the company tripled, with autonomy but no alignment and matrix reporting with no accountable owner.7648 documented Amazon's single-threaded leader is the opposite: one accountable person, a separable team, and the explicit lesson that separability of the work is the precondition.102 documented Netflix runs a strong central platform under distributed analytics engineering, the hub-and-spoke the mesh literature both recommends and complains is not decentralized enough.151 inferred

Judgment

The through-line: what survived had a single accountable owner per separable unit of work; what failed had autonomy without ownership. Whatever India owns, the US org must be re-cut to not own it. Half-ownership is the one configuration every source on this page says fails.

Fifteen durable principles

judgment What survives the disagreements. The Applying It page scores each candidate charter against this list; the numbers are stable references.

  1. Task interdependence dictates the coordination mechanism, and the mechanism dictates what distance can tolerate. Pooled → standardize; sequential → plan; reciprocal → mutual adjustment, which requires co-location or heroic effort.361746
  2. Self-contained tasks are the cheapest way to reduce information-processing load; lateral processes are the most expensive way to increase capacity. Prefer the former across sites.4716
  3. Ownership clarity predicts quality; diffuse ownership predicts defects. The best-replicated Conway-adjacent result, and it holds regardless of geography.2313
  4. Distance per se does not degrade quality; coupling and low common ground do. Cross-site coupled work is about 2.5x slower, but decoupled work with shared process and tooling is not measurably worse.141346
  5. Architecture reduces coordination need but never eliminates it; budget for the residual. Congruence between required and actual coordination is worth roughly a third on resolution time.128129
  6. Structure is the least important Star point to get right first; processes, rewards and people compensate for structure but not the reverse.32
  7. Internal consistency of the configuration beats fashion. Mixing divisional output-control with machine-bureaucracy process-control is the most common failure.12517
  8. Span of control is a function of task interlock and standardization, not a constant; "7" is folklore descended from a 1933 arithmetic exercise and a 1956 memory experiment. Ranges: 3–5 for player-coaches, 6–8 for coaching managers, 8–15 for standardized work.1321331344341
  9. A team is six to eight people; below four it is a project. Concentrate headcount on the most-behind team rather than spreading thin.41138
  10. Reorg only for structural problems that already exist and are not temporary; never to route around a person.136
  11. Platforms should start thin and be treated as products with internal customers; they exist to reduce the cognitive load of the teams they serve, not to accumulate the work domains will not fund.2834131
  12. Loosely coupled teams (able to change, test and deploy without cross-team permission) outperform, independent of technology stack. Transfer the structural finding, not the four metrics, to data work.37141
  13. Producers own data quality; contracts define the failure state and must be enforced mechanically.147148
  14. A single accountable owner per separable unit of work is what survived in every case study; autonomy without accountable ownership is what failed.1024876
  15. Remote work exposes poorly defined teams; the fix is explicit Team APIs, tracked dependencies and deliberately designed inter-team communication, not tooling.3915316
