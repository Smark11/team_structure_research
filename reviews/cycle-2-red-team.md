## Verdict

1. I would not sign v2, but it is a different memo from v1: the evidence problems are fixed, the scoring is on the page, and the cost and legal questions now exist. What is wrong now is the *plan*, not the *research*.
2. The weakest link is the calendar. Every gate, the QoE dissolution announcement and the Phase 2 transfer are absolute months (6, 9, 18), while Phase 1's start floats to month 6 and Phase 0 cannot close on time by the memo's own sequence (site head hires the managers; Gate 0 needs the managers started). The v1 fix for "announce nine months before transfer" is re-created by construction.
3. The second weakest link is that the two things that make this a "domain site" rather than an execution shop — the QoE domain and the experimentation contract — are each the half-ownership the memo bans, renamed. QoE scores "partial" on Test 1 only because its definition changes are rare, and then needs a staff-level lead with a roadmap the annual plan accepts; both cannot be true. The experimentation engine's only customer is on the other site, which is the memo's own drift condition.
4. Four cycle-1 items were applied cosmetically: A24 (the Phase 1 management arrangement contradicts Gate 0), A16 (T3/T5 fixed; T4/T6 still return work to a US team that stopped owning it), A23 (one date, but the downstream dates did not move), A13 (Charter Evidence says the plan "needs a backfill line"; the plan has none). Those are blocking by the rules of this cycle.
5. The cost table shows converted engineers cheaper per seat than the vendor while the prose says conversion is a cost step-up; the US offset depends on domains not backfilling seats the memo says they are short of. "Roughly neutral by year three" is not a finding, it is the offset line.

**Blocking items:** A2-1, A2-2, A2-3, A2-4, A2-6. Verification failures (Part 1) are folded into those.

## Part 2 — Critiques of v2

### A2-1 — Gate 0 requires two managers started; Phase 1 is designed for their absence

**Severity:** blocking
**Claim under attack:** memo §2 Gate 0: "Site head started · ≥ 1 engineering manager *started* per Phase 1 component (2)"; memo §2 Phase 1: "until the second-line managers are in seat, the site head runs the Phase 1 teams directly with two tech leads and the ≤ 8-direct rule is suspended"; same pair on index 1.3 and applying-it 4.4, 4.5 ("hired during Phase 1 while the site head still has no second-line managers under her"), Fig 4.2 note.
**The attack:** Two cycle-1 fixes were applied independently and they contradict each other. A10 made "one EM started per component" a hard Gate 0 criterion. A24 wrote the Phase 1 arrangement for a site with no EMs. Both cannot be in the same plan: either the two EMs have started before Phase 1 begins (and the suspension of the ≤ 8 rule is unnecessary and the "stated, not hidden" paragraph describes a configuration the plan never enters), or they have not (and Gate 0 has not passed, so Phase 1 has not started). The site's staffing paragraph makes it worse: Phase 2 hires are made "while the site head still has no second-line managers under her" — during Phase 1, i.e., *after* Gate 0 certified that two had started. If "second-line manager" is meant to be a different tier from "engineering manager" (a manager of managers), the memo never says so, the Charter Evidence page uses the terms as one tier ("second-line managers who can run complex teams without escalating"), and a 12–16-person site does not have a manager-of-managers tier. Underneath the contradiction is the real problem: applying-it 4.6 says the site head's "first job is hiring them". The site head starts at month 3–6; a manager search at 7:1 ratios is 3–5 months; the EMs start at month 7–10. Gate 0 as written cannot pass at month 3 and is unlikely at month 6.
**What would satisfy me:** Pick one. Either (a) keep the hard EM gate and delete the "site head runs the teams directly" arrangement, and move Phase 1's start to "when two EMs have started" — realistically month 7–9, with everything downstream re-dated (see A2-2); or (b) keep the arrangement and change Gate 0 to "two EM searches open with a shortlist; EMs started by the Phase 1 gate", which is honest about the six-month player-coach period and its span. Say which tier "second-line" means.

### A2-2 — The dates are absolute; the plan is not

**Severity:** blocking
**Claim under attack:** memo §2 "Phase 1 begins when Gate 0 passes: target month 3, no later than month 6 (T1)"; Phase 1 "Months 3–9"; Phase 1 gate "Two consecutive quarters …"; Phase 2 "Months 9–18"; §3 "dissolution is announced at month 6 … retention terms through month 12"; constants "Gates: months 3 (≤ 6), 9, 18, 30"; Fig 1.2; index 1.3; applying-it ownership table "P1 (month 3)", "P2 (month 9)".
**The attack:** Walk it with Phase 1 starting at month 6, which the memo permits and A2-1 makes likely. The Phase 1 gate needs two consecutive quarters of incident data on the transferred components: the earliest it can be judged is month 12. Phase 2 is dated month 9. So either QoE transfers before Phase 1's gate is judged — which the memo forbids ("Phase 1 gate still holding" is a Phase 2 criterion) — or Phase 2 slips to month 12–15 while its own dated criteria (QoE lead hired "before Phase 2", DQ team at ≥ 6, T5 at month 18) stay put. Now the retention fix: the US QoE team's dissolution is "announced at month 6" — a calendar month — which on this path is the *same day* Phase 1 starts and 6–9 months before the transfer actually happens. That is exactly the "announced nine months early, knowledge walks out" failure A14 was accepted to fix, re-created by writing the fix as a date instead of as "three months before the Phase 2 transfer". Retention "through month 12" then expires before the transfer. T11 is the only tripwire that says "the phase clock pauses and the gate dates move with it"; nothing else in the plan is clock-relative. Even on the happy path, Phase 1 at months 3–9 is *exactly* two quarters — zero slack for quarter alignment, for the baseline's variance to be estimated, or for a transfer that lands on the 15th of a month.
**What would satisfy me:** Re-express every gate, announcement and transfer as an offset from the previous gate ("Phase 2 transfer = Phase 1 gate + 0; dissolution announced = Phase 2 transfer − 3 months; retention = transfer + 3"), state the calendar only for the happy path and the T1 path side by side, and add the two-quarter minimum explicitly. Fix Fig 1.2, index 1.3, the ownership table and the constants together.

### A2-3 — T4 and T6 still return work to a US team that stopped owning it

**Severity:** blocking
**Claim under attack:** T4 fallback: "move metrics computation back to the US, keep assignment and exposure logging in India" (memo §1, §4; index 1.2d, 1.5; applying-it ownership table). T6 fallback: "dissolve the duplicate or formally re-transfer the component" (memo §4; index 1.5). Memo §4 preamble: "Fallbacks are written to be executable by the org as it exists at that date — v1's 'return it to the US team' fallbacks fired after that team had been dissolved."
**The attack:** The A16 fix was applied to T3 and T5 and not to the two tripwires most likely to fire. T4 fires "after month 9". The US experimentation platform engineers "stop owning the engine and its on-call" at month 3 (ownership table). At month 9–12 the memo moves metrics computation — the hardest third of the engine — back to a US team that has not touched it for six to nine months and whose engineers were redeployed or not backfilled (the cost table's offset *depends* on those seats being gone). This is the v1 defect verbatim, and the memo's own preamble says it was fixed. T6's "formally re-transfer the component" is worse: the only US capacity that exists for the component at that point *is the shadow team*, so the fallback rewards the behaviour the tripwire detects. And T4's residual — a team of six or seven in India owning assignment and exposure logging — is a feature-flag SDK and an event topic. That is two to three people of work under the memo's own six-person floor and its own warning about a "junior-heavy pyramid with no path to the second-line managers". The memo bans half-teams and then designs one as a fallback.
**What would satisfy me:** Apply the T3/T5 rule to T4 and T6: narrow, re-pair, or time-boxed shared state with a named end; transfer-back only after a US team is re-formed on a stated hiring clock. For T4 specifically, say what the six remaining people do, or fold assignment and exposure logging into the telemetry ingestion team and admit the experimentation engine was not a whole component.

### A2-4 — Quality of experience, as scoped, is a platform component wearing a domain label

**Severity:** blocking
**Claim under attack:** memo §1 scoring row: Test 1 "Partial — engineering consumers … sign off metric-definition changes, which are rare and industry-standardized; pipeline and dimension changes need nobody"; outcome "Modest … Playback engineering *could* compute its own — the Phase 2 gate tests whether it stops"; §1 "A platform-only site has no customer on its own site and nothing the US cannot ship without"; Phase 2 preconditions "a staff-level QoE analytics lead in India who owns the QoE roadmap"; Phase 2 gate "India-authored QoE roadmap accepted in the annual plan"; applying-it 4.2 Test 1 ("the score only holds because definition changes are rare"); 4.6 "equally accountable to the US playback stakeholders"; T5 fallback "an acceptable landing, not a failure".
**The attack:** Four things, each fatal to the domain label on its own.
(1) *The squeeze.* QoE passes Test 1 only because metric-definition changes are "rare and industry-standardized" (rebuffer ratio, startup time, EBVS, bitrate are Conviva/Mux-grade standards). A domain whose definitions rarely change has a roadmap of pipelines, dimensions and dashboards — platform work. If the roadmap is substantial enough for "a staff-level analytics lead" and for "the annual plan" to accept it — new metrics, new dimensions (device, CDN, ISP, live-event), root-cause and anomaly tooling — then definition changes are not rare, the engineering consumers are in the loop on every one, and Test 1 drops from partial to fail. The memo needs both halves and cannot have them.
(2) *The memo's own anti-drift criterion.* The reason for adding a domain is that a platform-only site has "nothing the US cannot ship without". The QoE row then concedes playback engineering "could compute its own". By the memo's own definition, QoE is not the thing the site needed. The hedge against drift is a domain that can drift.
(3) *The test is not a test.* The month-18 gate is an attestation by the playback engineering director that India's tables are "referenced by playback SLO dashboards and release gates". Pointing a dashboard at a table is an afternoon's work and costs the attester nothing; the client-side QoE computation that playback engineering actually trusts can keep running. And the memo pre-declares failure "an acceptable landing, not a failure" (T5, §5B). A hedge whose failure has no cost and whose pass condition is a signature is not a test of whether QoE is a domain; it is a way to keep the word "domain" in the memo for eighteen months. It is also a career trap: the staff-level lead hired at month 9 has a 50% chance, on the memo's own framing, of becoming a pipeline lead at month 18 when "the US liaison seat becomes the QoE metrics owner" — a one-person US seat outranking the India domain owner is the demotion the whole memo exists to prevent.
(4) *Dual accountability is the banned configuration.* The QoE team "reports to the site head and is equally accountable to the US playback stakeholders" (applying-it 4.6), with US engineering signing a decision-rights table and an "analytics partner seat" serving them. The memo classifies experimentation *analysis* as adhocratic, stakeholder-adjacent work that stays where the stakeholders sit. A QoE analytics partner to playback engineering is the same job with a different customer, across the same 0–2.5 h overlap — and NBCU's "embedded with and equally accountable to" is the memo's own definition of diffuse ownership ("Half-ownership is diffuse ownership by design. [judgment]", index 1.1).
**What would satisfy me:** Either (a) call QoE what the scoring supports — a capability (telemetry + QoE pipelines + metric implementation) with the definitions in the catalog from day one, drop the "one domain" framing, and rewrite §5B as "we do not have a domain that passes; here is what we will test for one at month 18"; or (b) keep the domain claim and make the test bite: a gate that requires playback engineering to *decommission* its own QoE computation (not attest to a reference), a roadmap that names three India-authored metric or dimension changes the US adopted, and a stated consequence for failure that is not "acceptable". Score Test 1 against the roadmap the lead is expected to have, not against a steady state with no changes.

### A2-5 — Playback telemetry pipelines belong to two India teams

**Severity:** major
**Claim under attack:** Phase 1 (a) "Telemetry and event ingestion with its streaming pipelines"; Phase 2 "Quality of experience end to end: playback telemetry pipelines, QoE metric definitions and computation …"; ownership table rows for telemetry and QoE; applying-it steady-state table (telemetry team 6–8, QoE team 8–10).
**The attack:** Playback events are the highest-volume telemetry source in a streaming company; they *are* most of "telemetry ingestion". At month 9 two India teams own "playback telemetry pipelines" — the ingestion team from Phase 1 and the QoE team from Phase 2 — and the memo never draws the line between them (raw events vs. sessionised playback? ingestion vs. transformation? who owns the schema of a new client heartbeat field?). The memo's whole method is "one owner per separable unit"; its first domain overlaps its first component. This is not the cross-site failure mode, but it is the failure mode Team Topologies and the mesh retrospective both describe: two teams, one pipeline, a dependency neither planned.
**What would satisfy me:** One sentence per team: ingestion owns raw playback events to a contracted landing schema; QoE owns everything downstream of it. Put it in the ownership table interface column and the Phase 2 row.

### A2-6 — The experimentation contract is half-ownership with a versioning scheme

**Severity:** blocking
**Claim under attack:** memo §1 "India owns assignment, exposure logging and the metrics-computation engine, including the implementation of metric definitions as versioned configuration; the US owns the semantic definitions, the catalog and the review gate. A definition change is a US-authored config change against a schema India maintains; a computation change is India's. That is X-as-a-service"; §3 "The one deliberate exception … an X-as-a-service interface, not shared ownership"; ownership table row; index 1.2d.
**The attack:** Walk three changes that happen every quarter in a real experimentation platform.
*A new guardrail metric* (say, rebuffer rate for treatment vs control). Analysis writes the semantic definition (US, day 1). It needs a new source join — QoE tables (India, Phase 2) or session facts (US warehouse) — which the current schema does not express. That is a schema change (India), then a config PR (US), then a computation change to support the join (India), then review (US). Four hand-offs, two sites, a 12-hour gap on each: one work item, two owners, Herbsleb's 2.5×. The contract only holds for metrics expressible in the schema as it stands; anything new — ratio metrics, CUPED covariates, sequential tests, a new unit of randomisation — is a schema change first.
*A redefinition of "active user."* Semantic change in the catalog (US). It is the denominator of most metrics, so every running experiment needs recomputation, and someone must decide whether to recompute in flight or from the next start. That decision is the review gate's (US); the backfill is India's on-call; and "backfill hours" is one of the three Phase 1 gate measures — so India's gate is charged for a change India did not make and could not refuse. The memo's anti-gaming logic runs in the other direction: the US can fail India's gate by redefining a metric.
*An SRM check flags an exposure-logging bug.* Sample-ratio mismatch is detected by analysis (US), in the US morning, when India is asleep. It is a P1 for every experiment on the platform. Either India's on-call is paged at 02:00 IST or the org waits a business day; the fix is India's; validation is the US's; the decision on whether the affected experiments are salvageable is the review gate's. Three round-trips minimum: two to three business days on an incident that a single-site team closes in hours. The Phase 1 gate counts P1 incidents, not P1 duration times experiments blocked, so this cost is invisible to the plan.
None of this is X-as-a-service. In Team Topologies the provider evolves the service on its own roadmap and the consumer uses it with minimal collaboration. Here the service evolves on the consumer's roadmap (every catalog change), the provider cannot say no (the catalog is authoritative), and the provider's only customer is on the other site — which is, word for word, the memo's own drift condition for a platform ("no customer on its own site"). The memo says that condition justifies adding a domain; it does not say why it is acceptable for the one component it calls the hard case. Then T4's fallback leaves six people on assignment and exposure logging (A2-3).
**What would satisfy me:** Either move the line to somewhere one owner can hold — India owns the *runtime* (assignment, exposure, computation) *and* the schema, with a published change process and SLA and the US as a customer who files requests, not PRs; or leave computation in the US and move only assignment and exposure logging into the telemetry team and stop calling the engine a component. Whichever, add the three walk-throughs above to applying-it so the reader can see the hand-off count, and change the gate from "P1 count" to "P1 count and hours-to-resolve across sites".

### A2-7 — The cost table contradicts the prose on the direction of the conversion cost

**Severity:** major
**Claim under attack:** memo §3 table: "40 contractor seats at a blended vendor rate — $2.4M ($60k/seat)"; "30–35 converted engineers, fully loaded — $1.7–2.0M ($55k)"; applying-it 4.5 "The conversion has a cost step-up that the baseline hides … converting at market GCC bands means paying materially more per head than the vendor pays the same person"; synthesis finding 7 "conversion at market bands is a cost step-up, not a saving".
**The attack:** The table prices a converted engineer at $55k loaded and a vendor seat at $60k — the site is *cheaper* per head than the vendor, which is the opposite of what the prose, the synthesis and C5 say. Do the arithmetic the memo's own inputs imply: a $60k bill rate at a 30–40% vendor margin means the vendor pays ~$36–42k; add the 25–40% centre premium the memo cites → $45–59k base; load it (benefits, facilities, equipment, ~1.3×) → $58–77k. The honest number is $60–75k, and the converted line is $1.8–2.6M, not $1.7–2.0M. Separately, $60k blended is a large-MSA offshore rate for mid-level pipeline maintenance; a US media company's contractor mix — boutique data-engineering firms, US-based staffing firms billing for offshore seniors, a few onshore — runs $80–120k a seat. If the true "today" column is $3.2–4.8M, the site is cheaper than the vendor and the memo's "not a cheaper thing, a different thing" sentence is false in the other direction. Either way the table's internal logic is broken: it cannot show a per-head saving and argue a per-head step-up in the paragraph above it.
**What would satisfy me:** Derive the loaded conversion cost from the vendor rate, the margin and the premium, in one line, and show it above the vendor rate. Give the "today" line as a range keyed to the contractor mix (MSA-offshore vs. boutique). Then rewrite the CFO sentence to whatever the arithmetic says.

### A2-8 — The US backfill offset contradicts the redeployment plan

**Severity:** major
**Claim under attack:** memo §3 "Offset: US seats not backfilled (5–8 at ~$220k loaded) … from the transferred components and the QoE team"; "The US does not shrink by fiat; it stops backfilling the transferred seats"; applying-it 4.4 "the QoE team's people redeploy into the domains that stay, which is where the org is short anyway"; CFO sentence "roughly the same as today by year three".
**The attack:** Two claims, one paragraph apart: (1) the US QoE and transferred-component engineers *redeploy* into browse, search, funnels and commerce, "where the org is short anyway"; (2) the US *does not backfill* 5–8 seats. If the domains are short, they absorb the redeployed people *and* keep their open reqs — that is what "short" means, and no domain director in the history of planning has surrendered a req because a peer team was dissolved. The offset is realised only if the domains' future hiring is cancelled, which is a decision the memo cannot make and does not assign. And the table compares an *actual* run-rate today ($2.4M) with a *counterfactual* avoided cost (−$1.1–1.8M): "roughly the same as today" is literally the offset line, and the memo admits it in the next sentence ("If the US backfill offset is not taken, it costs $1–2M a year more"). The honest headline is the second sentence, not the first. Note also that the offset assumes the redeployed people are worth $220k loaded in their new domains from day one; a QoE engineer moving to commerce is a six-month ramp.
**What would satisfy me:** Lead with "+$1–2M a year" as the base case and show the offset as a scenario with a named owner and a decision date ("domain directors cancel N reqs at the month-9 plan"). Remove "roughly the same as today" from the two-minute version unless that decision is in the plan.

### A2-9 — What the cost table leaves out is larger than its one-time line

**Severity:** major
**Claim under attack:** memo §3 table and "Overlap-hours stipends, travel, retention grants — $0.4–0.5M"; "One-time: vendor release fees, recruiting, transition — $0.7–1.5M"; Charter Evidence 5.5 "expect 12–18 departures by month 30 … the plan needs a backfill line and not just a threshold".
**The attack:** Missing, in rough order of size:
- *Recurring recruiting.* At the 18–25% attrition the memo adopts, a 45–50-person site replaces 9–12 people a year, forever. At the memo's own "~20% of first-year comp", that is $0.15–0.3M a year, recurring — the table books recruiting as one-time. Charter Evidence says the plan needs a backfill line; the memo has no backfill line.
- *India wage inflation.* Charter Evidence cites Zinnov's 9.8% average hike, 21% for AI/ML. Compounded, year-3 India comp is 30%+ over year 0. The table prices year 3 at year-0 bands.
- *Retention.* Conversion grants for 30–35 people vesting at 18/30 — at a modest 15–25% of comp that is $0.3–0.5M, which is the entire "stipends, travel, retention" line on its own. The US QoE team's retention through month 12 (A2-18) is not in the table at all.
- *Double-running.* Vendor seats do not end the day FTE offers are accepted; a 3–6 month overlap on 40 seats is $0.6–1.2M, and under T12 it becomes 12 months.
- *The site head's long-term incentive*, India real estate if the loaded rate does not carry it, US severance for any of the 5–8 who are not redeployed, and FX (INR depreciation historically helps USD cost by 3–4% a year; wage inflation eats it — say so rather than ignore both).
Add these and "roughly neutral by year three" is a $1.5–3M a year delta before the offset. That is the number the CFO will compute if the memo does not.
**What would satisfy me:** A backfill line (heads/year × recruiting cost), a wage-inflation line, retention as its own line (conversion + US QoE), a double-running line with its duration tied to T12, and the year-3 delta restated. If the honest answer is "+$1.5–3M a year for ownership, tenure and in-region on-call", write it — v1's reviewer asked for that sentence and v2 wrote a more comfortable one.

### A2-12 — T9: the sponsor's objectives leave with the sponsor

### A2-13 — T11: a paused clock with a US team in notice is the orphan scenario

### A2-14 — T12: "hire net-new, +6 months" has no budget and no knowledge

**Severity:** major
**Claim under attack:** T12: "The vendor invokes non-solicit terms or prices the release above budget … Fallback: hire net-new for the component with the fewest contractors first (the experimentation engine); convert nobody; timeline +6 months." Index 1.5 T12 window "month 1–3".

**What would satisfy me:** A named negotiator (procurement plus the US platform director), the double-running cost as a line in the one-time table under a T12 scenario, and an honest timeline ("+12–18 months; Phase 2 is deferred to the second annual plan"). **Severity:** major

**The attack:** Take the two grounds in turn. *Legal is unknowable until month 3.* That is an argument for waiting three months, not eighteen.
