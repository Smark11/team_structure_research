What all eight have in common

Eight companies, two research tracks, one shape. Every company in this set that runs data at scale keeps the platform central and puts the people near the domain. Netflix runs one Data Science and Engineering org with analysts aligned to business verticals and a central experimentation platformdocumented; Spotify runs a central data platform and a central experimentation platform with data scientists embedded in product areasdocumented; Google has run company-wide experiment infrastructure since 2007documented; Disney built one Experiment-X team serving nineteen stakeholder teamsdocumented; NBCU centralized data engineering and data science under a CDO while keeping teams embedded with the businessesdocumented.C1-1C1-14C1-24C2-12C2-33

The two public counter-examples are retrofits after failure. Spotify's squads were manually negotiating experiment bucket ranges with each other until a 2018 platform ended itdocumented; Amazon's operations-finance data sat in more than 25 databases with regional teams keeping local copies until a finance team built the Galaxy lake in 2019documented; Paramount ran three services on two clouds with separate data pipelines for seven years until a new owner forced consolidation in 2026documented.C1-14C1-34C2-43 Nobody publicly runs per-domain metric definitions or per-domain A/B stacksinferred.C1-14

Second: "embedded" means embedded people, never embedded platforms. Netflix keeps the reporting line central and deploys analysts into verticalsdocumented; Spotify moved the reporting line into product but keeps metric definitions in a central Metrics Catalogdocumented; Google keeps a central review gate staffed by engineersdocumented; NBCU's memo says the embedded teams are "part of" the central orgdocumented.C1-1C1-14C1-24C2-33

Third: remote sites hold capability charters, not data-domain ownership. Warsaw got infrastructure, gaming and production technologydocumented; Prime Video Bengaluru got playback, X-Ray, live and client testingdocumented; WBD India got data engineering, ML, applied data science, ad platforms and enterprise datadocumented; Sky's UK teams got the whole streaming platformdocumented.C1-6C1-36C2-22C2-29 The one remote site that grew into product-surface ownership is Google Zurich, which took roughly eighteen years and ~5,000 people to become YouTube's second development headquarters, and still does not own the analytics functiondocumented.C1-27 The one India site that owned a consumer domain end to end, Hotstar, was an India-first product on its own stack, and it is no longer Disney'sdocumented.C2-15

Netflix

Why it matters here

judgment Netflix is the closest documented analogue to a mid-sized data org with one central leader: one reporting line and hiring bar, people aligned to verticals, the org rather than the partner prioritizing scarce talent. Its only non-US engineering hub got infrastructure and production tooling, not a data domain. That is the pattern a ~140-person org should expect to copy, not the exception.

Structure

Since at least 2020 Netflix has run a single Data Science and Engineering group spanning data engineering, analytics engineering, visualization engineering, experimentation and causal-inference science, and algorithms researchdocumented.C1-1C1-2 The 2020 analytics post states the design principle: "we align our analytics professionals with the listed business area verticals rather than organizing them within a single functional horizontal," so that they "possess deep business context and are thought leaders alongside their business counterparts"; the verticals are content, studio, membership and growth, marketing, product, streaming and platform, and financedocumented.C1-1

That is a hub-and-spoke: one reporting line, one craft standard, people deployed into verticalsinferred. The same post calls analytics engineers "a specialized resource and a rare commodity" who "are given freedom to choose their projects" and must prioritize by business impact, which means the central org, not the business partner, allocates scarce talentdocumented.C1-1

How it changed and why

Elizabeth Stone joined in 2020 as VP of Product Data Science and Engineering, later VP of Data and Insights; in October 2023 she became Netflix's first-ever CTO; in February 2026 she was promoted to Chief Product and Technology Officer over "product, engineering, and data teams" after the CPO's departure, with co-CEO Greg Peters citing her ability to "simplify complexity, connect the dots across our business, and help teams move quickly"documented.C1-7C1-8 The data leader absorbed product and engineering, not the reverse; that is the strongest public signal of a centralized data org that reports highinferred.C1-8 No public statement ties a data-org reorg to the 2022 ads-tier and paid-sharing upheaval; the hub-and-spoke shape looks stable across the 2020–2024 postsinferred.C1-1C1-3

Where experimentation sits

The January 2022 post by Martin Tingley and colleagues lays it out: several data science teams "partner directly with Product Managers, engineering teams, and other business units," a central experimentation platform (XP) provides allocation, measurement and tooling, and Netflix "intentionally encourage[s] collaboration between the centralized experimentation platform and the data science teams that partner directly with Netflix business units"documented.C1-2 Embedded scientists "own all aspects of the analysis of a study" and bring "our analytics point of view to lively cross-functional debates on roll-out decisions"; launch decisions belong to neither the scientist nor the platformdocumented.C1-4 The ML platform follows the same split: the Machine Learning Platform team provides "an entire ecosystem of tools around Metaflow" on a single company-wide S3/Iceberg lake, "hundreds of Metaflow projects" are deployed, and "all the use cases were engineered by practitioners themselves"documented.C1-3

Global sites

Engineering is overwhelmingly in Los Gatos and Los Angeles. In January 2023 Netflix announced its first engineering hub outside the US, in Warsaw, to "help build the products that our internal and external creative partners use to deliver Netflix shows and films"documented.C1-5 By March 2026 the Warsaw office held ~300 staff, was described as "Netflix's only technology hub outside of the United States," and its engineering was focused on infrastructure, gaming and production technologydocumented.C1-6 India offices (Mumbai; Hyderabad opened March 2026) are content, production and AVGC, not engineeringdocumented.C1-9 No data-science or analytics charter for Warsaw appears in any public statementinferred.C1-6

What is attributable

Netflix says decisions across "every corner of the business" run through tests and DSE, but nothing public separates the structure's contribution from the "context not control" culture the same posts rely ondocumented.C1-1C1-2 What can be defended: the central-platform plus embedded-scientist arrangement scaled to hundreds of ML projects and company-wide experimentation without fragmenting into per-domain platformsinferred.C1-2C1-3

Spotify

Why it matters here

judgment Spotify is the cautionary tale for "autonomous teams, no central platform": squads negotiating experiment buckets by hand in 2017, and a CEO in 2023 calling out "work around the work." Its Boston hub is also the best precedent for graduation, because it owned a whole capability from day one. Do not import the 2012 whitepaper; import the 2018 platform and the 2023 verdict.

Structure

The October 2012 Kniberg and Ivarsson whitepaper described ~30 squads across three cities, squads as "mini-startups" with long-term missions, tribes capped "smaller than 100 people or so," and chapters and guilds for craft alignment, and it opened with the disclaimer everyone skips: "This article is only a snapshot of our current way of working — a journey in progress, not a journey completed"documented.C1-10 Squads were told to use "metrics and A/B testing to find out what really works"documented.C1-10 Data scientists and user researchers later sat together in Product Insights teams embedded with product areas, described as "not a centralized function"inferred; the post could only be read through a search excerpt, so treat the quote as probable, not verified.C1-23 A "Missions" layer above tribes is widely repeated but, for this record, folklorefolklore.C1-13

How it changed and why

Spotify's own people disowned the "model" almost immediately. Kniberg, June 2015: the material "wasn't actually intended to be a generic framework or 'model' at all. It's just an example of how one company works"documented.C1-11 Ivarsson, July 2016: "Spotify is not a model"; he named cross-team alignment as a growing problem and squad autonomy as in tension with "a consistent design language and customer journey"documented.C1-12 Jeremiah Lee, a former Spotify PM, in April 2020: the model "was only ever aspirational," with agile coach Joakim Sundén quoted as saying "Even at the time we wrote it, we weren't doing it. It was part ambition, part approximation"; Lee lists matrix management with unclear accountability, autonomy without alignment, collaboration assumed rather than taught, and unevaluable terminology, and says leadership "incrementally transitioned to more traditional management structures"documented.C1-13 The Sundén quote exists here only second-handinferred.C1-13

Then the 2023 re-centralization. On 23 January 2023 Spotify cut ~6% and named Gustav Söderström Chief Product Officer and co-president overseeing "the majority of our engineering and product work," to "drive more efficiency, control costs, and speed up decision-making" after 2022 opex growth "outpaced our revenue growth by 2X"documented.C1-19 On 4 December 2023 it cut ~17%; Daniel Ek wrote that "By most metrics, we were more productive but less efficient. We need to be both," that there were "too many people dedicated to supporting work and even doing work around the work rather than contributing to opportunities with real impact," and that "being lean is not just an option but a necessity"documented.C1-20 Ek never names the squad model, but that is a management verdict on an autonomy-heavy structure: the cost was coordination, not outputinferred.C1-20

Where experimentation sits

Central, and increasingly so. A team was spun up in 2013 to build the ABBA tool; a 2017 hack-week diagnosis found experiments coupled one-to-one to feature flags, A/B events at ~25% of total event volume, data scientists running inconsistent notebook analyses, and teams "manually coordinating bucket ranges between experiments," which was "error prone"; the 2018 Experimentation Platform with a central Metrics Catalog followeddocumented.C1-14 By 2023 it served "hundreds of squads and thousands of developers," had scaled from "fewer than 20 priority experiments per year" to thousands, and was externalized as Confidencedocumented.C1-15 In 2025 the platform team reported a 64% learning rate against a 12% win rate, automated fail-rate checks (sample-ratio mismatch, pre-exposure imbalance, misconfigured serving), and an "engagement team" acting as a "center of excellence for internal customer success," noting that "adding experiment reviewers" can "drastically impact learning rates"documented.C1-17 In January 2026 Spotify explained why personalization and experimentation are owned by different teams on deliberately separate infrastructure integrated by API; 58 teams ran 520 experiments on the mobile home page alone in one yeardocumented.C1-18 The data platform itself is a dedicated organization with Data Collection, Data Processing and Data Management areas, processing 1.4 trillion data points a day and serving "payments to experimentation"documented.C1-16

Global sites

R&D hubs as of 2019 were Stockholm, Gothenburg, New York, San Francisco, Boston, and London, opened April 2019 as "Spotify's first major technology center outside the US and Sweden"documented.C1-21 Boston exists because of the March 2014 acquisition of The Echo Nest, a music-intelligence company whose Somerville office Spotify committed to keep runningdocumented.C1-22 Spotify lists Bangalore, Gurgaon and Mumbai among its offices but nothing public assigns India an R&D charter; non-Stockholm hubs grew from an acquired capability or talent-market access and hold product-area and capability charters, not data-domain chartersinferred.C1-21C1-22

What is attributable

The one clean structure-to-outcome link is negative and self-reported: squad autonomy produced coordination waste in experimentation in 2017 and "work around the work" company-wide in 2023documented.C1-14C1-20 The growth to thousands of experiments a year is attributable to the platform and its center-of-excellence practices, not to the squad modelinferred.C1-15C1-17

YouTube / Google

Why it matters here

judgment Google supplies two things: the oldest documented experiment review gate (a council of engineers with a checklist) and the only remote site that ever grew into product ownership. The second took eighteen years and 5,000 people. A 140-person org should plan on the first and not on the second.

Structure

Google publishes almost nothing about YouTube's data-science org. Data-science roles are titled by product area ("Data Scientist, Product," "Data Scientist, Core Compute," "Business Data Scientist, gTech") and split into product and research tracks, which signals an embedded-by-product model with a shared job ladder rather than one central analytics org; ML engineering sits under software engineeringinferred. The one primary artifact is the 2010 KDD paper on Google's overlapping experiment infrastructure, which is company infrastructure, not a product-team tooldocumented.C1-24

How it changed and why

On 20 April 2023 Google merged Google Research's Brain team with DeepMind into Google DeepMind under Demis Hassabis, with Jeff Dean as Chief Scientist reporting to Pichai, because "the pace of progress is now faster than ever" and one team "backed by the computational resources of Google, will significantly accelerate our progress in AI"documented.C1-25 That is a centralization move for frontier ML under competitive pressure, in the opposite direction from product-embedded data scienceinferred.C1-25 No public YouTube data-org reorg was foundinferred.C1-24

Where experimentation sits

Tang, Agarwal, O'Brien and Meyer describe a domains/layers/experiments infrastructure deployed in March 2007 and are explicit that "an experiment infrastructure alone is insufficient": they added automated data-file checks, real-time monitoring, standardized metrics so that "two experimenters should use the same filters," an experiment council, "a group of engineers who review a light-weight checklist that experimenters fill out prior to running their experiment," and a forum where results are interpreted and archiveddocumented.C1-24 Governance is a central review gate staffed by engineers, not a data-science sign-off; product teams design and run their own experimentsdocumented.C1-24 Whether YouTube runs on this exact stack today is not publicinferred.C1-24

Global sites

Zurich is Google's largest development center outside the US: ~2,000 people in 2017 growing toward 5,000, hosting Search, Maps, Calendar, YouTube, Gmail, Assistant, Photos and Translate engineering and described as the European home base for MLdocumented.C1-26 By 2022 it held ~5,000 staff and "Zurich is currently the largest headquarters of the YouTube development team alongside the headquarters in San Bruno"documented.C1-27 That is a product-engineering charter at full-peer scale, the best example in this set of a non-HQ site owning product surface area, and it still does not own the analytics functioninferred.C1-27 India engineering centres (Hyderabad, Bengaluru) are described as ~10,000 people with charters in Search quality, Pay infrastructure, Cloud and AI, but the only reachable source is a GCC directory compiled from LinkedIn and Wikipediafolklore.C1-28 DeepMind's HQ is Londondocumented.C1-25

What is attributable

Only the 2010 paper's own result: experiment count, launches and distinct experimenters all rose after the 2007 infrastructure and the council/forum processesdocumented.C1-24 YouTube's data org is undocumented, so nothing there is attributableinferred.C1-24

Amazon & Prime Video

Why it matters here

judgment Amazon is the fully decentralized end of the spectrum, and it paid for it in 25 finance databases. Its India sites hold service charters with single-threaded leaders on site. If the ~140-person org ever moves a domain to India, the Amazon lesson is to move the decision-maker and the whole separable team, not just the analysts.

Structure

AWS's own explainer: two-pizza teams under ten people with "single-threaded ownership" of one product or service and its full lifecycle, adopted to "minimize lines of communication and decrease overhead of bureaucracy," overseen by single-threaded leaders using narratives rather than approvalsdocumented.C1-29 Bryar and Carr's Working Backwards records the correction: "the biggest predictor of a team's success was not whether it was small" but its dependencies; Amazon treated inter-team coordination as a defect and shifted emphasis from team size to a single-threaded leader with a separable team, on the principle that "the best way to fail at inventing something is by making it somebody's part-time job"documented; this record has the book only through a secondary summary.C1-30C1-31 Practitioners distinguish single-threaded ownership, where the leader controls all resources, from single-threaded leadership, where the leader drives priorities across lines that report elsewhereinferred.C1-38 The "Bezos API mandate" has only a memoir post behind itfolklore.C1-29

Amazon has no company-wide data organization; data engineers, BIEs, scientists and economists are hired by and report into business orgsinferred. The consequence is documented by Werner Vogels: before the Galaxy data lake, operations-finance data lived in "more than 25 databases with regional teams creating their own local version of datasets," each with separate credentials; a finance-operations team, not a central data org, built Galaxy in 2019 on S3 with the Andes and Elastic Data eXchange layers, and "the various teams are working on moving their data into it"documented.C1-34

How it changed and why

The structural change on record is the single-threaded-leader correction and the 2019 Galaxy retrofit; both were responses to coordination cost, and both moved toward a shared layer without creating a central data orgdocumented.C1-31C1-34 On the Prime Video business side, India's functional heads (SVOD, marketplace, marketing, originals, licensing, productions) report to a VP for APAC and MENA, with the India country-director seat vacant since July 2024documented.C1-37 Business leadership is regional; technology is globalinferred.C1-36C1-37

Where experimentation sits

Weblab is Amazon's internal A/B platform; Ron Kohavi "was the director of data mining and personalization at Amazon, where he was responsible for Weblab," which places it in the early 2000sdocumented.C1-32 The circulated numbers ("created 2011, 546 experiments year one, 12,000+ per year now") come from uncited vendor blogs and conflict with Kohavi's tenurefolklore.C1-33 What is safely inferable: a central platform used by business teams under their own single-threaded leaders, with launch decisions inside the owning team and no public central councilinferred.C1-32

Global sites

Prime Video engineering is organized as Prime Video Experience and Technology, "the technical backbone of Amazon Prime Video's global streaming service," covering discovery and search, playback, download, live, encoding, delivery, client experiences, content protection and anti-frauddocumented.C1-36 Amazon's Hyderabad campus, opened August 2019, is its largest building worldwide at 15,000 seats, hosting AWS, Kindle, Alexa, Amazon.in and Home Services teams; India country manager Amit Agarwal said "This facility will build services globally"documented.C1-35 Prime Video's Bengaluru site is expanding "spanning across X-Ray, playback services, live technologies, player and client testing and other foundational technologies," with a Principal Engineer role to "guide global Prime Video teams on architecture decisions"documented.C1-36 India sites hold capability and service charters inside global orgs with single-threaded leaders on site; none is carved out as "India owns the commerce data domain"inferred.C1-36 The oft-repeated "Amazon IDC began in Hyderabad in 2004" has no primary source; Gulf News dates Amazon's retail entry to 2013folklore.C1-35

What is attributable

Two things: the single-threaded-leader model's self-diagnosed failure mode (dependencies, not size) and the silo cost that forced Galaxydocumented.C1-31C1-34 The 2023 "microservices to monolith" post was one service team's architectural call, single-threaded-leader autonomy in action, not an org story, and Prime Video's product outcomes cannot be tied to PVXT's structure from public materialdocumented.C1-36

Disney

Why it matters here

judgment Disney is the legacy-media control case on both axes. Its one documented experimentation org is central platform plus federated domains, and the domains it lists are this reader's domains. Its one India engineering org of scale owned a whole market end to end, and that is exactly why it drained to a competitor and was JV'd away. Own the platform layer; do not build an island.

Structure

Disney's streaming tech org has been reorganized four times in eight years, each time for a CEO-level strategy reason. March 2018: the Direct-to-Consumer and International segment under Kevin Mayer bundled "BAMTECH (technology and digital products center)" with Hulu, ESPN+ and international channels, with Aaron LaBerge as CTOdocumented.C2-2C2-3 Since February 2023 technology has been one shared org, Disney Entertainment and ESPN Technology, reporting jointly to the Disney Entertainment co-chairs and the ESPN chairman, "including the teams that build and run Disney+, Hulu and ESPN+"; one tech org, three P&Lsdocumented.C2-4 In August 2024 Adam Smith, from YouTube, became Chief Product and Technology Officer with the same triple reporting line and "engineers, product managers, designers, data scientists, and technical operations teams" explicitly in his org; product-side data science sits inside P&T, not a corporate data functiondocumented.C2-5

How it changed and why

On 12 October 2020 Chapek collapsed DTCI and Media Networks into Disney Media and Entertainment Distribution under Kareem Daniel, responsible for "P&L management and all distribution, operations, sales, advertising, data and technology functions worldwide"; the most centralized Disney's data and tech ever gotdocumented.C2-1 Iger returned, Daniel exited, and on 8 February 2023 DMED was unwound into Disney Entertainment, ESPN and Experiences alongside 7,000 cuts, with the rationale of putting "decision-making back in the hands of our creative teams"documented.C2-3 DMED lasted 28 monthsdocumented.C2-3 In April 2026, after SVP Ajay Arora left, the Data Product and Engineering group was moved under Tony Donohoe, EVP of the ad platform; the "Commerce, Data and Identity" alliance that had overseen commerce, growth and account management was dissolved, and the internal "Atlas" data initiative was cited as needing "tighter integration with ad technology systems," all during a ~1,000-person cut under new CEO Josh D'Amarodocumented.C2-11 Data went from a monetization chief (2020) to the content businesses (2023) and back to ad-tech (2026); a media data org's reporting line follows whichever P&L is being asked to grow this yearinferred.C2-11

The Hulu stack decision ran in parallel. Iger announced a "one app" experience in May 2023; Hulu on Disney+ shipped 27 March 2024, which LaBerge called "the most significant technical, operational, and product evolution for Disney+ since its launch," designed for reuse in the Star+ merge and the ESPN DTC launchdocumented.C2-7C2-6 In August 2025 Iger said both services would be "on one tech platform," and a leaked May 2026 document said "the Hulu tech stack and app will be decommissioned," which Disney walked backdocumented.C2-8C2-9 The BAMTech-lineage stack won; the 2025 P&T cuts were under 2%, so this was a multi-year absorption, not a rip-and-replace with a headcount eventinferred.C2-10 "Bring Hulu onto BAMTech" in those words has no primary sourcefolklore.C2-6

Where experimentation sits

Disney Streaming's blog is the only first-party account of an experimentation org in the legacy-media set. In 2019 it formed a central Experiment-X team because "different teams were using different tools," shipped an MVP in six months and "X²" a year later, organized the team "around domains like product, backend engineering, data science and audience intelligence," and gave a Core Experimentation sub-team ownership of automated, standardized analysis (the ExpAn library) so stakeholders did not depend on analysts; by January 2022 it had onboarded 19 stakeholder teams running experiments across "Landing Page, Sign Up Funnel, Commerce, and Quality of Service (QoS)," with experiment velocity in the OKRsdocumented.C2-12 That is central platform plus federated execution, scoped across Disney+, Star+, Hulu and ESPN+ before the apps themselves were mergeddocumented.C2-12 The data platform: a Kinesis/Flink real-time pipeline feeding recommendations and operational analytics (2020), and data governance as a dedicated function under a VP on Snowflake with "a single copy of data" (2021)documented.C2-13C2-14

Global sites

India is the important case, and it is cautionary. Disney+ Hotstar (April 2020) was built on Hotstar's own platform, not BAMTech's, to "take advantage of Hotstar's existing infrastructure and customer base," with a UX distinct from global Disney+documented.C2-15 Hotstar was a full-stack, full-domain engineering org from 2015 with its own infrastructure and data platform, documented on its own engineering blogdocumented; its Bangalore base is not primary-verifiedfolklore.C2-17 After losing IPL digital rights to Viacom18/Jio in 2022, "Hotstar lost a considerable portion of its technology team" to the Jio side along with sports and sales leadership, per a single trade newsletterinferred.C2-16 In November 2024 Disney folded its whole Indian TV and streaming business into the JioStar JV (Reliance 63.16%, Disney 36.84%, Reliance operating), with the apps merged into JioHotstar on 14 February 2025documented.C2-15 Disney's only India engineering center of scale was a product-owning org with full ownership of a market, and it is no longer Disney's; no Disney streaming GCC in India post-JV was found, and Disney Streaming's tech centers remain US-basedinferred.C2-15

What is attributable

Disney streaming's swing from ~$4B annual losses to profit across 2023–2025 is consensus but driven by pricing, password-sharing enforcement, content cuts and the bundle, not org designfolklore.C2-8 Defensible structure-attributable outcomes: one shared tech org across three P&Ls is what made the Hulu and Star+ consolidations reusabledocumented; the central experimentation platform is the one data capability Disney chose to publicizeinferred; and a centralization that ignores where P&L accountability sits (DMED) did not survive a CEO changedocumented.C2-6C2-12C2-3

Warner Bros. Discovery

Why it matters here

judgment WBD has the most explicit India charter in the set, and it is capability-shaped: data engineering, ML, applied data science, ad platforms, enterprise data. It was stood up in four months at peak cost-cutting and grew while US headcount fell. That is what a platform charter looks like when a CFO, not a CTO, sponsors it.

Structure

Two stacks before the merger: HBO Max launched May 2020 on WarnerMedia's platformfolklore; Discovery+ launched January 2021 on Discovery's global DTC platform, which had already absorbed Dplay and the Eurosport Player in Europedocumented.C2-49 After the April 2022 merger, CTO Avi Saxena and CPO Tyler Whitworth both came from Discovery; the acquirer's smaller tech leadership took over the combined orgdocumented.C2-18 WBD's public data footprint is almost entirely ad sales: the "Olli" first-party data platform (April 2024) sits in Digital Ad Sales under Ryan Gould on Snowflake clean roomsdocumented.C2-21 Nothing public says where streaming product analytics, experimentation or data science report; WBD appears to run data as embedded functions in ad sales, growth marketing and the DTC tech org with no single ownerinferred.C2-21

How it changed and why

In the only substantive technical interview, Saxena described twelve months of planning "to build a cohesive platform" with "engineer teams from both HBO Max and Discovery+ sides" contributing, a "20% faster" bar and four CDNs; he did not say "Discovery+ stack"documented.C2-18 "Max = Discovery+ backend" is widely repeated and consistent with who led, but never primary-confirmedfolklore.C2-18 The August 2022 HBO Max cuts (70 people, 14% of the content org) were content, casting and international, not tech; the July 2024 ~1,000 cuts were finance and business affairs with "cuts to Max staffers in the single digits"documented.C2-19C2-20 No public source quantifies HBO Max tech attrition during the Max build; "HBO Max engineers left en masse" isfolklore.C2-20 In June 2025 WBD announced a split into Streaming and Studios (HBO, HBO Max) and Discovery Global (CNN, Discovery, Discovery+), re-splitting the portfolio by brand while still sharing a platformdocumented.C2-26 Netflix agreed to buy Streaming and Studios in December 2025; Paramount Skydance's hostile bid won with a $110.9B agreement on 27 February 2026, shareholders approved 23 April, DOJ cleared 12 June, twelve state AGs are litigating with trial in March 2027 and an outside date of 1 June 2027documented.C2-27

Where experimentation sits

Unknown. No public source places WBD's streaming experimentation function; the only documented data org is the ad-sales platforminferred.C2-21

Global sites

The India hub was announced 17 May 2023 after Telangana's IT minister met WBD's SVP Finance, as an "International Development Centre" with 1,200 professionals in year one, and inaugurated 20 September 2023 with CFO Gunnar Wiedenfels present; the minister called it WBD's "first Greenfield office in Asia after the merger"documented.C2-23C2-24 That the CFO, not the CTO, fronted it signals the mandateinferred.C2-24 WBD's careers site describes an "India Innovation Hub" across Bangalore, Hyderabad and Pune with charters in streaming technology and content platforms; data engineering and machine learning; applied data science; identity, partnerships, trust and safety; enterprise data services and automation; product management; and advertising platforms, "building the future of Warner Bros.
