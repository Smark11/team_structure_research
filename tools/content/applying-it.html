Sensitivity

The argument survives ±30% on any line. Move 8 contractors from QoE to commerce and Phase 1 gets slower, not different; halve the platform and the site still owns whole components, just fewer of them.

judgment Four further assumptions carry weight. The contractors are concentrated in pipeline maintenance for platform, QoE/sessions and commerce, and they sit under a single vendor master services agreement, which means the vendor controls who is released. There is no India site head, no India product manager and no second-line manager today. The reason the numbers matter less than the shape is Galbraith's: structure is the least important of the five design points to get right first, because processes, rewards and people can compensate for a mediocre structure and not the reverse.C3-1 Headcount changes phase durations. Three tests, from the evidence

The five research tracks disagreed about which domain India should own first — the canon argued for defense-heavy commerce data, the India evidence argued for telemetry, the case studies named QoE — and agreed, without saying so, on the criterion. Every one of them was applying the same question: how much does this work require people on the other site to say yes, and how often. The three tests below are that question decomposed, each traced to the research that produced it.C3-45C3-42

Test 1 — US decision-makers per change

Herbsleb and Mockus measured it: cross-site work items took about 2.5x as long as comparable single-site items, and the mechanism was not slower individuals but more people per item — headcount per change predicted calendar time.C3-43C5-18 Cummings, Espinosa and Pickering added that time-zone separation hurts more than spatial separation, because temporal boundaries are harder to cross with tooling.C5-19 Carmel and Agarwal's first tactic follows: reduce intensive collaboration across sites by giving each site separable work.C3-45 The test, applied to a unit of work: count the US people who must approve a typical change. Commerce packaging needs pricing, finance, legal and trust-and-safety; telemetry ingestion needs a schema owner. Multiply each of those by 2.5 across a 12-hour gap and you have the cost of the boundary.

Test 2 — Output standardizability

Mintzberg's configurations are defined by their coordinating mechanism, and only one of them — the divisionalized form, coordinated by standardization of outputs — is designed to run a unit at arm's length from headquarters; adhocracy, coordinated by mutual adjustment, is the most expensive to coordinate and the least tolerant of distance.C3-4 Thompson's interdependence ladder says the same from the task side: pooled work is coordinated by standards, sequential by plans, reciprocal by mutual adjustment.C3-52 DalleMule and Davenport map this onto data: defensive data work — quality, single source of truth, compliance — wants centralized control and standard outputs; offensive work wants flexibility and lives next to its stakeholders.C3-42 The test: can the unit's output be written down as a schema, an SLA or a metric definition that a consumer can accept without a conversation? Telemetry can. A funnel analysis cannot.

Test 3 — Separability from the warehouse

Conway's law applied to data has a twist. Warehouse facts are reciprocally interdependent — every domain reads every other domain's tables — so mirroring the org onto the data produces silos before it produces modules.C3-52 The 2026 Thoughtworks data-mesh retrospective is the field report: most organizations could not staff data-competent domain teams and ended up with a thick central platform doing the domain work anyway.C3-33 Galbraith's cheapest information-processing move is the self-contained task, which only exists if the task can actually be cut out.C3-2 The test: can this unit be owned whole without the warehouse's shared facts flowing through it? Ingestion sits upstream of the warehouse and passes; the semantic layer is the warehouse and fails by definition.

The gate — one accountable owner, on one site

A unit that passes all three tests is still not transferable unless it can be owned whole by one team on one site. Nagappan, Murphy and Basili found that organizational metrics — above all diffuse ownership, many engineers and organizations touching a component — predicted failure-proneness better than churn, complexity or coverage.C3-8 Bird and colleagues, on the same Windows data, found distributed components were not measurably worse than collocated ones once ownership was clear and tooling shared.C3-9 Amazon's self-diagnosed lesson from two-pizza teams was that dependencies, not team size, were the failure mode, and that a single-threaded owner must be able to close their own work.C1-31 So the gate excludes every "India builds, US approves" arrangement outright. If a unit cannot be owned whole on one site, it does not move.

Judgment

How the scoring resolves the disagreement. QoE/sessions is the only domain that clears all three tests: telemetry in, metrics out, its consumers are client and playback engineers plus one analytics group, and its pipelines sit upstream of the warehouse. Three of the five tracks named it independently. It goes first among domains, after the three platform components, because it is where the India contractors already sit and because it gives the site an outcome the US cannot ship without — the cure for the platform-only drift.

Fraud and paid sharing is the second candidate, not the first. It is defense-heavy and its outputs standardize well, which is why the canon liked it — but every model change touches trust-and-safety, legal and finance, and the decisions have dollar latency. It moves only once an India product manager exists and a decision-rights table is signed, and it is decided at month 18 against criteria, not sentiment. Commerce packaging, conversion funnels, browse and search fail Test 1 outright for as long as pricing, bundling and content strategy are set in the US; they stay anchored there. Experimentation splits down the middle: the platform backend is a capability with an API and passes, while analysis, the metrics catalog and the review gate are adhocratic and stakeholder-adjacent and stay central — which is how every case study runs them.

What the US org stops doing

Every source that describes a distributed-ownership failure describes the same one: the sending site kept a hand on the work. So the transfer is written as a list of things the US organization ceases to do, with the principle behind each, because ownership that is added in India without being removed in the US is diffuse ownership under a new name.C3-8

  • US platform engineers stop owning, reviewing-as-gatekeepers, and being on-call for the three transferred components. They keep warehouse modeling and the semantic layer, governance and access, the ML platform until Phase 3, and architecture as a published standard rather than a veto. The principle is Bird's: distributed components were not worse than collocated ones when ownership was clear and tooling shared — the finding depends on the ownership being real.C3-9
  • The US QoE/sessions team ceases to exist as an owning team at month 9–12. Its engineers and analysts move to browse, search, funnels or commerce, or take the one US-side liaison seat. Carmel and Agarwal's tactic is to reduce intensive collaboration across sites; two teams owning one domain is the maximal-collaboration configuration.C3-45
  • US managers stop having India directs. Every India engineer reports to an India manager; every India manager reports to the site head. A remote direct across a 12-hour gap is the Herbsleb 2.5x applied to one-on-ones, and a site whose managers sit elsewhere is the leadership vacuum Zinnov describes — centers past 100 people with no permanent head and no bridge to the global org.C3-43C5-9C3-24
  • Half-ownership is banned as a category. No "India builds, US approves" arrangement survives Phase 1. If a component cannot be owned whole on one site it is not transferred — Nagappan's diffuse-ownership result and Amazon's dependencies-are-defects lesson say the same thing from opposite ends.C3-8C1-31
Where it breaks

This is the part that fails in practice. Nothing in a US owner's incentives pushes the hand-off, and "we can't wait for India" is how shadow teams start. Tripwire 6 on the Recommendation page exists for exactly this.

judgment The US does not shrink by fiat. It stops backfilling the transferred seats, and the QoE team's people redeploy into the domains that stay — which is where the org is short anyway once four domains and the analysis function are explicitly US-anchored.

The team-size and span arithmetic

Larson's rule is that a team is six to eight people, below four it is a project, and a manager should run three to five directs if hands-on and five to eight if people-focused, never more than eight.C3-23C3-24 McKinsey refuses a single number and gives five archetypes — player-coach 3–5, coach 6–7, supervisor 8–10, facilitator 11–15, coordinator 15+ — driven by how standardized the work is and how much of the manager's own time is individual contribution.C3-20 Data-platform work sits near the coach end: standardized enough for a span of six or seven, not standardized enough for ten.

Put the floor and the ceiling together and the small-site problem appears. A twelve-person site asked to own two domains cannot honor a six-person team floor and an eight-direct span ceiling without a management layer nobody wants to fund; the frameworks are silent and budget decides.C3-23C3-24 Scaled to the whole org, the same arithmetic is why domain-owned data teams do not fit at 140: six people times eight domains is 48 heads of domain data engineering before any platform exists, which is the mesh retrospective's finding about why most organizations ended up with a thick central platform.C3-33C3-23

judgment What it implies for the India site is fewer, whole teams rather than many partial ones: every India team at or above six, every component staffed to that floor before it transfers, and no team that is half of something. At steady state that is roughly 45–50 India FTE — a site head, three to four engineering managers, one to two product managers, three to four staff engineers, and five to six teams. judgment The span line does not close cleanly. Three to four managers at eight directs cover 24–32 engineers; the table has 32–40. Either staff engineers act as tech leads under a manager spanning two teams — the player-coach archetype, and a real cost to their own output — or the site needs a fifth manager by Phase 3. Budget decides, as the tension says it would; what is not acceptable is ten-direct spans presented as economy.

The conversion has a cost step-up that the baseline hides. GCCs pay 25–40% over IT-services rates at mid-to-senior levels, and the constraint on GCC hiring is second-line managers, not engineers.C5-11 On build-operate-transfer, Forrester's warning is that the provider will not be anxious to hand over its best people, and converting current contractors is the same transaction — the vendor controls who is offered.C5-15 Bands set at vendor-plus rather than GCC market are how a site ends up in the 43% of the population that never leaves the execution tier.C5-1

Reporting lines and rewards

The site head is a peer of the US directors on the data executive's staff, with a global functional title — Head of Data Platform Engineering and Telemetry, or whatever the components are actually called — and accountability for the transferred components and domains, not a country manager. The evidence favors a functional head hired first over either a country manager or a lift of the vendor's delivery leads; the first three hiring decisions shape the center more than the next ninety-seven combined.C5-9 Second-line managers are the binding constraint in the GCC market, so the site head's first job is hiring them, before any component moves.C5-9

Below the staff row the org keeps one shape on both sites: a central reporting line and hiring bar, people aligned to verticals, and the data org rather than the business partner deciding where scarce talent goes. That is Netflix's arrangement for a data organization ten times this size, and it is the documented analogue for hub-and-spoke at any scale.C1-1 Experimentation analysis and the metrics catalog report centrally in the US, as they do at every case-study company that has written about it.C1-2C1-24

The QoE domain needs a second line of accountability written down, because its consumers — US client and playback engineering — will otherwise treat an India-owned domain as a vendor. NBCU's memo is the template: the research and data teams joined one central organization but "will remain embedded with and equally accountable to" the businesses they serve — the only explicit public phrasing of a dual-accountability data model in the case set.C2-33 Applied here, the India QoE team reports to the site head and is equally accountable to the US playback stakeholders named in its decision-rights table, and the adoption of its metrics as source-of-truth by those stakeholders is the Phase 2 graduation criterion.

Galbraith's point is the one this section turns on: rewards and processes decide whether a structure works, and structure is the least important point to get right first.C3-1 So the India site gets its own hiring budget and a promotion cycle on the same calendar and ladder as the US; India-authored roadmaps enter the same planning process; on-call compensation is identical across sites; and the first India engineers promoted into staff and lead roles are a Phase 3 criterion because a working pyramid is the observable proxy for a working ladder. Where captives did not get this, they stagnated on marginal improvements and were eventually cut or sold.C5-15

judgment Without the identical calendar, ladder and on-call terms, the charter is a slide. Every piece of the topology above can be drawn in a week; the rewards and planning processes are the year of work, and they are the part a reorg announcement never mentions.
