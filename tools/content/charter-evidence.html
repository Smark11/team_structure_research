    The base rates

    India has 2,117 global capability centers across 3,728 units, employing about 2.36 million people on $98.4B of revenue in FY26,C5-1 up from roughly 1,700 centers, 1.9 million people and $64.6B two years earlier.C5-2 Zinnov counts about 110 new centres opened between early 2024 and late 2025, EY more than 120 since January 2023 — roughly 50 a year — and 35% of mid-market centres were set up in the last two years.C5-3C5-5 Common, and mostly stuck: read the distribution, not the totals.

    Who is counting

    Every population figure in this section comes from Zinnov, EY or ANSR — firms that sell GCC set-ups, surveys to GCCs, or both. Treat their maturity and attrition numbers as a ceiling on how good things are, not a floor. Where two of them disagree, both figures are shown.

    Zinnov's maturity ladder runs Outpost → Satellite → Portfolio Hub → Transformation Hub, and puts today's population at 13% / 43% / 39% / 5%.C5-1 Read plainly: 56% of India GCCs sit in the two execution tiers and 44% in the two ownership tiers, where Portfolio Hub means real end-to-end ownership of a portfolio.C5-1 That is a population snapshot, not a graduation rate. Zinnov separately states that 27% of centres reach its portfolio-hub tier within five years of set-up; the method behind that figure is not published, so it is reported here as Zinnov's claim. It says how many centers are execution shops today; it says nothing about how many centers chartered as extensions ever leave that tier, and no cohort graduation rate is published — Zinnov does not follow centers over time, and nobody else does either. The snapshot comes from the firm that sells center set-ups, so read the 44% as a ceiling. inferred

    The older data is worse. Forrester found in 2007 that "more than 60% of the captive centers in India alone are struggling," citing lack of management support, spiraling costs, skyrocketing attrition and lack of integration — and noted that many captives existed because an expatriate executive wanted to move home.C5-12 The 2008–09 cohort was then sold off: Citi sold Citigroup Global Services to TCS for ~$505M in October 2008 and its Indian IT unit to Wipro in 2009, each tied to a multi-year service contract; AXA's 600-person captive went to Capita; AOL, Aviva, Prudential UK and Philips put theirs on the block.C5-13C5-17 The analyst reasons at the time were fixed cost, management distraction, inflexible capacity in downturns, and a talent market that preferred vendor employment for career growth.C5-13 That last reason has inverted — GCCs now pay 25–40% above IT services at mid-senior levels and post the lowest attrition of any segmentC5-4C5-11 — but the other three have not. inferred

    The current population is healthier: Everest counted 452 new set-ups in 2023, and about 50 captives sold in 2020–23 out of 1,450 created.C5-15 A ~3% divestiture rate is a different world from 2009. What has not changed is the shape of the ladder: most sites are execution shops today, and nothing published says how many were chartered that way and left.C5-1

    JPMorgan · Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad

    The largest support charter in the country

    50,000+ in August 2023, ~55,000 in 2026, adding ~1,000 technologists in cloud, cybersecurity and "AI data pipelines," with a 2M sq ft Mumbai campus for ~30,000 by 2029. documentedC5-26C5-27 The firm's own language is consistently "technology solutions, business and functional support to serve all of our clients globally."C5-26

    Hotstar · Star India / Novi Digital, 2015→

    Full domain ownership — and full separability

    Built in India from 2015, scaled to record concurrency, platform reused across Disney+ markets; a home-market product with local P&L, not a GCC charter. documentedC5-33 After the 2022 IPL rights loss its technology team drained toward Jio — per one trade newsletter quoting an unnamed executive inferredC2-16 — and the business is now a Reliance-majority JV.C5-33 Full account: Case Studies, Disney.

    Google & Amazon · Bengaluru, Hyderabad and others

    "Built for India, scaled globally"

    Google's Bengaluru campus post says "we have been building from India, for the world," citing Google Pay and flood forecasting. documentedC5-35 Amazon's India office pages headline "Built for India, scaled globally." documentedC5-36

    Lesson. The products India owned first were India-first products that later globalized, not global products handed over. The largest India engineering orgs in the world got to ownership through a local customer. inferred judgment

    What distinguished owners from back offices, read across the cases: (1) India teams first owned something whose customers or center of gravity were local — Hotstar, Google Pay — or, in Target's store-design case, a self-contained capability with clear outputs; (2) product management and data science were hired in India alongside engineering (Target, Lowe's) rather than left in the US; (3) the site head carried a global functional title, not a country title — two-thirds of "Mega GCC" heads have technical backgrounds and many run a global portfolio in addition to India;C5-3 (4) time — the successes are 10–20-year stories. The stagnation cases are almost all support charters that never got a product or platform to own. inferred

    The historical record is about captives generally, not contractor conversion specifically; the mechanisms transfer because the transaction is the same — a vendor's people become yours, and the vendor gets a say in which ones.C5-15 inferred

    Conditions under which India ownership works

    Leadership seeding

    The evidence favors a site head with a global functional mandate, hired first, over either a country manager or a lift-and-shift of vendor delivery leads.C5-3C5-9 Forrester's 2007 data warns specifically against the returning-expat-for-personal-reasons pattern.C5-12 The norm is still thin: EY's 2025 survey found about 80% of centers have under 10% of leadership roles based in India,C5-6 while 52% report "shared accountability for global decisions" and 26% are merely "formally consulted."C5-4 Global roles led from India numbered 6,500+ in 2024, projected to 30,000 by 2030.C5-2C5-3 EY's 2025 survey, as reported by VARINDIA, names the missing layer as second-line managers who can run complex teams without escalating to the country head; Zinnov's version is the 7:1–8:1 candidate ratio and hiring across the pyramid.C5-6C5-3 Nothing rigorous exists on "send a US leader versus hire local"; the boomerang pattern is folklore.

    Product and data science on site

    The documented owners all have product management in India: Target co-locates Business, Tech, Product, Data and UX; Lowe's lists product management as a function; Hotstar was a whole company. documentedC5-22C5-25C5-33 Zinnov's line that "maturity that took a decade is now a design choice made on day one" is marketing,C5-1 but it reflects a real shift: new mid-market GCCs increasingly open with product and data owners instead of graduating to them. inferred

    Cross-border personal data

    judgment None of the reports behind this site covers data-transfer law, so this is a condition stated without a source, not a finding. QoE telemetry carries device, IP and account identifiers; fraud and paid-sharing data carries payment tokens, household graphs and, for European subscribers, EU-scoped personal data. Processing either in India runs through India's DPDP Act 2023 and whatever transfer mechanisms the company already operates. For QoE that is a Phase 0 question with a named owner — privacy counsel, answered by month 3, before the first transfer. For fraud it is possibly disqualifying: if the answer is "pseudonymized only" or "no EU data," the domain is not transferable as scoped, and the month-18 decision is between ads measurement (if the org has an ads tier) and the ML-platform expansion; neither is a fallback. Get it from counsel in Phase 0, not at the phase gate.

    The distributed-work research

    The distance research (Foundations §3.10) gives the base rate for the alternative: cross-site work items took about 2.5× as long at Lucent because more people were needed per item, not because anyone was slower;C5-18 temporal separation costs more than distance;C5-19 and Microsoft found no quality penalty for distributed work once ownership was site-level and tooling identical, while diffuse ownership predicted defects at 86% precision.C3-9C3-8

    Judgment

    For a Pacific/Eastern-to-IST split the overlap is zero at nominal hours and at most 2.5 hours if Eastern and India both stretch; Pacific never reaches it (Applying It, Fig 4.3). Cummings found temporal separation costs more than distance, and this is about as separated as two sites get. Any charter that requires shared ownership of the same work items across sites — India engineers picking tickets off a US backlog, US and India engineers co-owning a pipeline — pays the Herbsleb penalty on every item, and the overlap window is too small to buy that back with stand-ups. Shared ownership of the same work is ruled out. What the research supports is whole-module ownership: India owns entire components or a whole domain with its own decision rights, and the cross-site interface is an API or a contract, not a meeting. Bird's result says that shape does not cost quality; Nagappan's says the alternative does. None of it is GCC-specific data — no primary source documents decision rights, budget authority or on-call at a named India site — so it is argued from the distributed-work research and the case pattern.

    judgment The 18–25% row is the one a gate has to be set against. An attrition gate below the base rate for the roles being converted is decorative: it fails in a normal year, gets waived, and teaches everyone that the gates are optional. The Recommendation's gate is therefore at the market and directional — regretted attrition of the converted cohort at or below 25% annualized and not rising quarter over quarter — with retention grants vesting at months 18 and 30 as the instrument behind it. At 18–25% on a converted cohort of 30–35, expect 12–18 departures by month 30 regardless; the gate detects a vendor-controlled or under-banded transfer, it does not prevent normal churn, which is why the plan carries a backfill line for it (Applying It §4.5).

    Judgment

    The evidence discriminates on three things and is silent on the rest. It rules out shared ownership of the same work across the twelve-hour gap; it shows that the graduated sites hired product, data science and managers before ownership was described as theirs, on a 10–20-year clock, in a population where 56% of centers are still execution shops and no graduation rate exists; and it shows platform charters scale, with thinner evidence than the folklore that they drift unless a product outcome depends on them. It does not tell you which domain, which components, or how to write a graduation gate — those are choices.
