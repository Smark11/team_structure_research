The recommendation

Charter the India site as the accountable owner of whole platform components — telemetry ingestion and the experimentation engine first — and of one whole domain, quality of experience. Graduate a second domain on a dated, criteria-gated decision. Keep commerce, experimentation analysis, browse, search and funnels anchored in the US.

A hybrid that transfers ownership by whole units on written gates — never a phase in which India contributes to work the US owns. The two-minute version follows; every section below it drills into the full defense.

    The defense in full

    Why not full domain ownership of commerce 1.2a

    The domain-ownership success stories are real and they are all the wrong kind. Hotstar was built and scaled in India and its platform was reused for Disney+ in Southeast Asia, Israel, South Africa and the Middle East — but it was a home-market product with a local P&L, not a charter handed to a centre (the full account is on Case Studies).C5-33 documented Google's Bengaluru campus post says the same thing in its own words: "building from India, for the world," citing Google Pay.C5-35 documented The pattern is India-first products that later globalized, not global products handed over. inferred

    Commerce is also the domain with the densest set of US decision-makers per change — pricing, bundling, finance, legal, trust and safety — and the research on cross-site work is specific about what that costs: work items spanning sites took about 2.5 times as long, not because individuals were slower but because more people were needed to resolve each one, and time-zone separation hurt more than distance.C3-43C5-19 documented The documented domain owners had three things by the time ownership was described as theirs — a site head with a global title, product management, second-line managers; the sequence is not documented — and on the assumed baseline this site has none of them today.C5-22C5-25 documented

    Hotstar's coda is often read as the risk of domain charters. It is not. After losing the IPL rights in 2022 its technology team drained toward the rights' new holder, per one trade newsletter quoting an unnamed executive, and Disney then folded the business into a joint venture it does not control.C2-16C2-15 inferred That shows a self-contained site follows its market's P&L — which is true of any charter — not that domain ownership fails. judgment The commerce sub-domain with the best case, fraud and paid sharing, is scored and gated below.

    Why not pure platform ownership 1.2b

    This is the option the recommendation is closest to — about eighty percent of it. Platform charters are the modal successful centre: JPMorgan runs about 55,000 people in India on what it calls "technology solutions, business and functional support";C5-26 Warner Bros. Discovery describes its India hub as "fundamental to building our global technology platforms"; Lowe's India ended a ten-year graduation with India-based vice presidents owning applied AI and data platforms.C5-27C5-31C5-25 documented Platform work is coordinated by standardization, which is the kind of work distance tolerates.C3-4C3-9 documented

    The first draft of this memo said platform-only "drifts into a cost centre." The evidence for that is thinner than the sentence: Citi's captive was a BPO and IT-support operation sold in 2008; the "captives become stagnant" line is one practitioner in a trade article; Sky's 2025 technology cuts are a platform consolidation, not a failed charter.C5-13C5-15C2-38 inferred What remains is a structural argument, not a documented base rate: a platform with no customer on its own site loses the platform-as-product feedback loop; a platform-only site builds a junior-heavy pyramid with no path to the second-line managers the site will need; and it gives a future P&L-driven owner nothing to notice.C3-15C5-6 judgment One domain is a hedge with a written test (tripwire T5). If the test fails, the site is this option, and that is an acceptable landing — not a failure.

    Why not a graduating extension 1.2c

    The extension model is the lowest-risk option on paper. The structural problem is that during the extension phase ownership is diffuse by definition, and diffuse ownership is the best-replicated predictor of defects in the software-engineering literature — Microsoft's organizational metrics predicted failure-prone binaries at around 86% precision, beating code churn and complexity.C3-8 documented Galbraith's point that rewards and processes decide whether a structure works cuts the same way: nothing in a US owner's incentives pushes a hand-off later.C3-1 judgment

    The market picture is a snapshot, not a rate. Zinnov's ladder puts 13% of India centres at "outpost," 43% at "satellite," 39% at "portfolio hub" and 5% at "transformation hub" — 56% in the two execution tiers — and it comes from a firm that sells centre set-ups. Zinnov also states that 27% of centres reach its portfolio-hub tier within five years of set-up; the method behind that figure is not published, so it is Zinnov's claim, not a base rate this memo can stand on.C5-1 documented Forrester found in 2007 that more than 60% of India captives were "struggling," and the 2008–09 divestitures were support charters that never got anything to own.C5-12C5-13 documented The two celebrated graduations are slow: Target from IT support in 2005C5-23 to an "integrated global headquarters" over roughly two decades; Lowe's from "one team, multiple locations" in 2015 to India-based platform VPs in ten.C5-22C5-24C5-25 documented

    This recommendation also graduates — QoE at month 9, a second domain at month 18 — and it says so. Its gate criteria are invented, as any org's would be. The honest distinction from the option it rejects is the starting state: whole ownership from the first transfer, never contribution to work the US owns. judgment

    Why "whole components," and where the experimentation line is drawn 1.2d

    Moving the entire platform on day one would strip the US of platform architecture while the site has no senior leadership — the vacuum Zinnov documents, where "the first three hiring decisions shape the center more than the next ninety-seven combined."C5-9 documented Team Topologies allows a platform to be several teams with clean interfaces, each a product for the teams it serves.C3-13 Amazon's correction to the two-pizza rule was that dependencies, not size, predict failure, and that every separable unit needs one accountable owner.C1-31 documented So components move one at a time, each with one owner on one site: telemetry ingestion and the experimentation engine first; data-quality and contracts tooling — net-new by construction — alongside the first domain; warehouse modeling, sessionization, governance and the ML platform stay in the US until a later, criteria-gated decision. judgment

    The experimentation platform is the hard case, because its customers — analysis and the review gate — stay in the US. The line: India owns assignment, exposure logging and the metrics-computation engine, including the implementation of metric definitions as versioned configuration; the US owns the semantic definitions, the catalog and the review gate. A definition change is a US-authored configuration change against a schema India maintains; a computation change is India's. That is X-as-a-service with a versioned contract — the interaction mode designed for remote customers — not "India builds, US approves."C3-14 judgment If the contract cannot be held (tripwire T4), metrics computation moves back and India keeps assignment and exposure logging.

    The phased path

    Each phase names the ownership that transfers, what must be in place first, and the observable criteria that gate the next phase — with the system that produces each number and who owns it, because a gate nobody instruments is a slogan. A missed criterion holds the phase; it does not fail the plan. Phase 1 begins when Gate 0 passes: target month 3, no later than month 6. The sequence follows what the documented graduations had in place — leadership, product and managers in India before ownership was theirs — compressed from a decade to thirty months on the argument that the components transferring first are, on the assumed baseline, already worked on by the India contractors.C5-22C5-25 judgment

    Tripwires — what would prove this wrong

    Twelve observable signals, each with what it means and a fallback the org can actually execute on that date. The first draft's fallbacks returned work to a US team the plan had already dissolved; these narrow instead of returning. Most retreat toward a platform charter, because a platform site that works is better than a domain site that does not. judgment

    1. T1 · month 6

      No site head started. The leadership-vacuum failure mode; centres have run past 100 people with no permanent head.C5-9

      Fallback

      Transfer nothing. Run as a managed extension with explicit Team APIs; revisit at month 9. An interim US leader only on a written twelve-month posting with a named local successor.

    2. T2 · month 12

      Regretted attrition of the converted cohort above 25%, or acceptance below 50% of the frozen wanted list. AI and cloud roles — the closest published proxy for data roles — already run 18–25% attrition with 18–24-month tenures, and the vendor controls who is offered.C5-7C5-15

      Fallback

      Pause Phase 2. Re-band; renegotiate the vendor release; hire net-new rather than convert.

    3. T3 · any two quarters

      Contract-breach or P1 incidents on a transferred component above baseline-plus-variance for two consecutive quarters. The evidence says this is team composition or tooling parity, not distance.C3-9

      Fallback

      Quarter 3: re-pair with a US engineer in facilitating mode; check the six-person floor. Quarter 4 still failing: narrow — India keeps the pipelines, the US takes the contract-change decisions for two quarters, named as a time-boxed shared state. Transfer back only after a US team is re-formed, never overnight.

    4. T4 · month 9

      More than 30% of India work items need a US decision, or the metric-definition contract generates cross-site work on every change. The boundary was drawn through a coupled system.C3-43

      Fallback

      Re-cut the boundary: move the decision right to India or move the work back. For experimentation, move metrics computation back and keep assignment and exposure logging in India.

    5. T5 · month 18

      QoE metrics authored in India are not the tables playback SLOs and release gates reference. The domain is not separable from its engineering stakeholders, or trust was never built.C3-44

      Fallback

      Narrow QoE to a capability: India keeps telemetry and QoE pipelines; metric definitions move to the central catalog; the US liaison seat becomes the QoE metrics owner. The site continues as platform-plus-capability, and this memo's "one domain" claim was wrong for this org.

    6. T6 · quarterly

      The duplicate audit finds a second pipeline, dashboard or "checking" query over a transferred component. Shadow teams are not announced; they are found in the catalog. A decision-rights failure, not a capability failure.C1-31

      Fallback

      Executive decision within 30 days of the audit: dissolve the duplicate or formally re-transfer the component. Either is acceptable; both at once is the failure.

    7. T7 · any time

      A P&L-driven reorg re-homes the data org under a different executive — Disney moved its streaming data platform under the ad platform in April 2026 during a thousand-person cut.C2-11

      Fallback

      None that is structural; the charter is at the new executive's discretion. The mitigation is leverage: the site owns components the likely new owner depends on daily — telemetry, and ads measurement if chosen at month 18. Team APIs make the hand-over legible; they do not protect it.

    8. T8 · month 12

      Decision-log median for India-owned changes needing US input exceeds three business days. The interface is a stand-up, not a contract; temporal separation hurts more than spatial.C5-19C5-20

      Fallback

      Move the decision right to India for that class of change, or reclassify the work as shared and stop counting it as India-owned.

    9. T9 · before month 18

      The sponsoring data executive leaves or is re-orged. Momentum "can quickly sag as a result of executive turnover."C5-15

      Fallback

      The charter, gates and dashboard are written into the site head's and the US directors' objectives at month 0; the incoming executive gets the memo and the gate dashboard in week one. Phases hold at the last passed gate; nothing is left half-transferred.

    10. T10 · any time

      The site head leaves. The leadership vacuum, second edition.C5-9

      Fallback

      The named successor — a senior India manager, in seat from month 9 — acts; no new transfers until a permanent head is in seat.

    11. T11 · any phase

      A hiring freeze. Every legacy-media company in the case studies had one in the period covered; WBD stood up its hub during cuts.C2-22

      Fallback

      Nothing below six people transfers. Components already in India stay in India with a named US escalation engineer, not on-call; the phase clock pauses and the gate dates move with it.

    12. T12 · month 1–3

      The vendor invokes non-solicit terms or prices the release above budget. Standard in master services agreements; unverifiable in advance, and the research found no litigated case. folklore judgment

      Fallback

      Hire net-new for the component with the fewest contractors first — the experimentation engine; convert nobody; timeline plus six months.
