The recommendation

Charter the India site as the accountable owner of whole platform components — telemetry ingestion and the experimentation engine first — and of one domain, quality of experience, while sessionization and the metrics catalog stay central. Graduate a second domain — fraud and paid sharing, or ads measurement — on a criteria-gated decision. Keep commerce, experimentation analysis, browse, search and funnels anchored in the US.

A hybrid that transfers ownership by whole units on written gates. After Gate 0, no phase in which India contributes to work the US owns. The two-minute version follows; every section below it drills into the full defense.

    In two minutes

    The evidence rules out both pure options and the extension. No documented case exists of a global consumer domain handed to a new India centre on day one and succeeding; the India organizations that did own a domain end to end — Hotstar, Google Pay — were home-market products with a local P&L.C5-33C5-35 documented Platform-only is the modal charter and it scales — JPMorgan runs about 55,000 people on it.C5-27 documented The graduating extension — India contributing to US-owned work until criteria are met — makes ownership diffuse by design, and diffuse ownership is the best-replicated defect predictor in the literature.C3-8 documented The market picture is a snapshot, not a rate: 56% of India centres sit in Zinnov's two execution tiers, 44% in its two ownership tiers, from a firm that sells centre set-ups.C5-1 documented

    The number that matters2.5×

    How much longer a cross-site work item took than a single-site one at Lucent — because more people were needed per item, not because anyone was slower.C3-43 It is the tax on every unit of work the two sites share.

    So: whole platform components and one domain, owned in India from the first transfer — and the US stops owning them. That last clause is the decision. No US on-call for transferred components, no US approval gate on India-owned changes, no US managers with India directs, no "India builds, US approves" after Gate 0. Dependencies, not team size, were the failure mode Amazon diagnosed in its own teams.C1-31 documented

    The defense in full — each option's strongest case, and why it loses

    Full domain ownership of commerce — strongest case, and why it loses 1.2a

    Strongest case. Hotstar proves an India engineering organization can own a streaming product at record concurrency and export its platform (the full account is on Case Studies).C5-33 documented Amazon's lesson is to move the decision-maker with the team and to treat dependencies as defects; a whole domain with a single-threaded leader on site is exactly that.C1-31 documented Contractors already touch commerce pipelines; commerce has the clearest P&L, so India would own an outcome the company cares about. Decisive ownership beats a decade-long graduation. judgment

    Why it loses. Hotstar and Google Pay were India-first products with local P&L; Google's own campus post says "building from India, for the world."C5-35 documented The pattern is India-first products that later globalized, not global products handed over. inferred Commerce packaging has the densest set of US decision-makers per change — pricing, bundling, finance, legal — and cross-site work items took about 2.5 times as long at Lucent because more people were needed to close each one.C3-43 documented Time-zone separation hurt more than distance.C5-19 inferred The documented domain owners had a site head with a global title, product management and managers by the time ownership was described as theirs — the sequence is not documented — and on the assumed baseline this site has none of them today.C5-22C5-25 documented Hotstar's coda is often read as the risk of domain charters; it is not. After losing the IPL rights in 2022 its technology team drained toward the rights' new holder, per one trade newsletter quoting an unnamed executive.C2-16 inferred That shows a self-contained site follows its market's P&L — true of any charter. The commerce sub-domain with the best case, fraud and paid sharing, is scored in 1.2f and has a month-3 re-decision point. judgment

    Pure platform ownership — strongest case, and why the memo adds one domain anyway 1.2b

    Strongest case. It is the modal successful centre charter: JPMorgan runs about 55,000 people in India on what it calls "technology solutions, business and functional support"; Warner Bros. Discovery describes its India hub as "fundamental to building our global technology platforms"; Lowe's India ended a ten-year graduation with India-based vice presidents owning applied AI and data platforms.C5-27C5-26C5-31C5-25 documented Platform work is coordinated by standardization, which is the kind of work distance tolerates; interfaces are APIs and schemas, not product debates.C3-4C3-9 documented It is what the contractors already do, fastest to charter, easiest to defend to a CFO. The recommendation is eighty percent this option, and if Gate 2 fails it is this option. judgment

    Why the memo adds one domain anyway. The claim that platform-only sites drift into cost centres is common and thinly documented: Citi's captive was a BPO and IT-support operation sold in 2008; the "captives become stagnant" line is one practitioner in a trade article; Sky's 2025 technology cuts are a consolidation.C5-13C5-15C2-38 inferred What remains is structural: a platform with no customer on its own site loses the platform-as-product feedback loop; a platform-only site builds a junior-heavy pyramid with no path to the managers it will need; and it gives a future P&L-driven owner nothing to notice.C3-15C5-6 judgment One domain is insurance against that drift, with a test that costs something to pass — playback engineering decommissions its own QoE computation — and something to fail: the domain claim is withdrawn at the next annual review and one senior role narrows (tripwire T5). judgment

    Graduating extension — strongest case, and why it loses 1.2c

    Strongest case. Lowest immediate risk; it lets the org learn who the converted people are before committing ownership. Target and Lowe's are real graduations: Target from IT support in 2005C5-23 to an "integrated global headquarters" over roughly two decades, Lowe's from "one team, multiple locations" in 2015 to India-based platform VPs in ten.C5-22C5-24C5-25 documented Olson and Olson found that common ground must precede remote collaboration, and an extension phase is how common ground gets built; Galbraith's lateral-relations-first sequencing is respectable theory.C3-44C3-2 documented

    Why it loses. During the extension phase ownership is diffuse by definition — Microsoft's organizational metrics predicted failure-prone binaries at around 86% precision, beating churn and complexity — and it is Spotify's "responsibility without accountability" pattern.C3-8C1-13 documented Nothing in a US owner's incentives pushes a hand-off later, and the celebrated graduations took ten to twenty years.C3-1 judgment Zinnov states that 27% of centres reach its portfolio-hub tier within five years; the method behind that figure is not published, so it is Zinnov's claim, not a base rate this memo stands on.C5-1 documented This recommendation also graduates — QoE at Gate 1, a second domain at Gate 2 — and concedes that Phase 0 is an extension and Phase 1 is execution under US standards. The distinction from the option it rejects is the starting state at the first transfer: whole ownership including on-call, with the transfer date, the decision rights and the test written before Phase 0 starts. judgment

    Undifferentiated hybrid — decide per team — strongest case, and why it loses 1.2d

    Strongest case. Target's operating rule (Charter Evidence) is a hybrid and it worked; Walmart runs global platforms next to a separately owned local product company; domains differ, as the offence–defence framing of data strategy says they should.C5-22C5-28C3-42 documented

    Why it loses. "Hybrid" is what mature sites became, not what they were chartered as.C5-22 inferred Without naming which units on day one, per-team choice produces half-ownership, because each US owner chooses "extension" for their own team.C3-8C1-31 judgment The recommendation is a hybrid with the choices made, scored, and re-decidable at month 3.

    Why "whole components," and where the experimentation line is drawn 1.2e

    Moving the entire platform on day one would strip the US of platform architecture while the site has no senior leadership — the leadership vacuum Zinnov documents, in which the first hires set the centre's ceiling.C5-9 documented Team Topologies allows a platform to be several teams with clean interfaces, each a product for the teams it serves; Amazon's correction to the two-pizza rule was that dependencies, not size, predict failure, and that every separable unit needs one accountable owner.C3-13C1-31 documented So components move one at a time, each with one owner on one site: telemetry ingestion — raw events to a contracted landing schema — and the experimentation engine first; data-quality and contracts tooling with the first domain; warehouse modeling, sessionization, governance and the ML platform stay in the US until a later, criteria-gated decision. Between the two India teams that touch playback events, ingestion owns raw events to the landing schema and QoE owns everything downstream of it. judgment

    The experimentation engine is the hard case, because its main customers — analysis and the review gate — stay in the US. The line: India owns the runtime — assignment, exposure logging, metrics computation — and the metric-definition schema, with a published change process and service levels; the US analysis function is a customer that files definition requests and owns the semantic catalog entry and the review gate. A new metric expressible in the schema is a request served within a stated SLA; a schema extension is India's roadmap item, prioritized with the US as customer; a sample-ratio-mismatch bug found in the US morning is a P1 that pages India's on-call at whatever hour, because that is what owning the component means. That is the minimal-collaboration interaction mode Team Topologies calls X-as-a-service: the provider evolves the service on its own roadmap.C3-14 judgment The engine has one customer on its own site — the QoE team supplies QoE guardrail metrics to experiments. Three changes are walked through, with their hand-off counts, on Applying It; tripwire T4 measures whether the line holds.

    The phased path

    Dates are offsets from Gate 0 — G0, the month the site head has started and the conversion cohort is committed: target months 3–4 (best case; the modal case is 4–6), no later than month 6. Later dates are offsets from Gate 0 and Gate 1 — G1 snaps to a quarter boundary, so a one-month Gate 0 slip can cost three, and nothing is announced against the calendar, because a plan whose announcements are dated while its transfers float is how a US team ends up in notice for a transfer that has not happened. Two calendars are shown: the best case and the tripwire-T1 path. The sequence follows what the documented graduations had in place — leadership, product and managers in India before ownership was theirs — compressed from a decade to thirty months on the argument that, on the assumed baseline, the components transferring first are already worked on by the India contractors.C5-22C5-25 judgment

    The India charter is a decision about the US organization as much as the India one. At steady state the ~140-person org is one hub-and-spoke: a central reporting line and hiring bar, people aligned to verticals, and the org rather than the business partner prioritizing scarce talent — the Netflix shape as of its 2020–2022 posts, at an order of magnitude smaller.C1-1C1-2 documented The site head sits on the data executive's staff as a peer of the US directors, not as a country manager; every India engineer reports to an India manager. judgment

    Tripwires — what would prove this wrong

    Twelve observable signals, each with what it means and a fallback the org as it exists on that date can execute: narrow, re-pair, or a named time-boxed shared state; transfer back only after a US team is re-formed on a stated hiring clock. Most retreat toward a platform charter, because a platform site that works is better than a domain site that does not. judgment

    1. T1 · month 6

      No site head started. The leadership-vacuum failure mode.C5-9

      Fallback

      Transfer nothing. Run as a managed extension with explicit Team APIs; revisit at month 9. An interim US leader only on a written twelve-month posting with a named local successor.

    2. T2 · G1, and month 12

      More than two regretted departures from the Phase 1 cohort by G1, more than three from the converted cohort in the first year, or fewer than 10 of 16 accepting by month 6. AI and cloud roles — the closest published proxy for data roles — run 18–25% attrition, and the vendor controls who is offered.C5-7C5-15

      Fallback

      Pause Phase 2; the clock moves with the pause. Re-band; renegotiate the vendor release; hire net-new rather than convert.

    3. T3 · any two quarters

      P1 hours or contract breaches on a transferred component above baseline-plus-variance for two consecutive quarters. Team composition or tooling parity, not distance.C3-9

      Fallback

      Quarter 3: re-pair with a US engineer in facilitating mode; check the six-person floor. Quarter 4 still failing: narrow — India keeps the pipelines, the US takes the contract-change decisions for two quarters, named as a time-boxed shared state. Transfer back only after a US team is re-formed, on a six-month hire.

    4. T4 · G0 + 6

      Hand-offs per experimentation change — defined in writing beside the decision log: any point where work stops until the other site acts — exceed two, or the definition SLA or committed-quarter answer is missed two months running. The provider cannot hold the service on its own roadmap.C3-43

      Fallback

      One quarter with a US analysis engineer in the India engine team's planning, facilitating. Still failing: fold assignment and exposure logging into the telemetry team and return metrics computation to a re-formed US team on a six-month hire, with the interim named as a shared state — and say the engine was not a whole component.

    5. T5 · Gate 2

      Playback engineering has not decommissioned its own QoE computation, or fewer than three India-authored definition changes were adopted. The domain is not separable from its engineering stakeholders, or India did not get the decision right in practice.C3-44

      Fallback

      The domain claim is withdrawn at the next annual review; India keeps telemetry, QoE pipelines and the metrics roadmap as a platform product; definitions register in the catalog; the liaison seat closes. Costed at one senior backfill.

    6. T6 · quarterly

      The duplicate audit finds a second pipeline, dashboard or "checking" query over a transferred component. Shadow teams are not announced; they are found in the catalog. A decision-rights failure.C1-31

      Fallback

      Executive decision within 30 days: the duplicate is dissolved and its owner's objectives amended. Re-transfer to the duplicating team is not an option — it rewards the behaviour detected; if India genuinely cannot serve the need, T3 or T4 applies.

    7. T7 · any time

      A P&L-driven reorg re-homes the data org under a different executive — Disney moved its streaming data platform under the ad platform in April 2026 during a thousand-person cut.C2-11

      Fallback

      None that is structural; the charter is at the new executive's discretion. Mitigations: the site owns components the likely new owner needs daily — telemetry, and ads measurement only if it moved into this org first; the new executive confirms the site head within 60 days or the memo assumes the charter is re-cut.

    8. T8 · G0 + 9

      Decision-log median for India-owned changes needing US input exceeds three business days. The interface is a stand-up, not a contract; temporal separation hurts more than spatial.C5-19C5-20

      Fallback

      Move the decision right to India for that class of change, or reclassify the work as shared and stop counting it as India-owned.

    9. T9 · before Gate 2

      The sponsoring data executive leaves or is re-orged. Momentum "can quickly sag as a result of executive turnover."C5-15

      Fallback

      The cost line is signed by the executive above the sponsor — CTO or CFO — who becomes the enforcer. The incoming executive decides within 60 days: continue, freeze at the last passed gate, or unwind — the unwind cost being re-formed US teams, vendor re-engagement and India severance, order $1.5–3M, not the remaining budget. Because announcements are phase-relative and a failed Gate 1 withdraws the Phase 2 announcement, neither a freeze nor a failed gate leaves a US team in notice. If the sponsor leaves before Phase 2, the base case is a platform site.

    10. T10 · any time

      The site head leaves. The leadership vacuum, second edition.C5-9

      Fallback

      The interim successor named at G1 from the engineering managers acts; no new transfers until a permanent head is in seat.

    11. T11 · any phase

      A hiring freeze. Every legacy-media company in the case studies cut headcount in the period covered — Disney, WBD, Sky and Paramount — and WBD stood up its hub during cuts.C2-10C2-20C2-38C2-47C2-24

      Fallback

      Nothing below six transfers; the clock pauses and every downstream offset moves. If the freeze lands after the Phase 2 announcement and before G1, the announcement is withdrawn and the US QoE team is re-confirmed as owner until the freeze lifts, its retention extended at $0.1–0.2M a quarter. A transferred team that falls below five during a freeze gets a US facilitating engineer under the T3 rule.
